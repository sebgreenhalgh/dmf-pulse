PURPOSE
-------
This document defines the model for predicting Fantasy Premier League player
price rises and falls and for deciding whether making a transfer early is
rational.

It must produce:

- probability_rise_next_update;
- probability_fall_next_update;
- expected_price_24h;
- expected_price_72h;
- expected_price_7d;
- confidence;
- likely_threshold_distance.

It must also produce complete price-path distributions and the decision evidence
needed to compare:

    ACT NOW

against:

    WAIT FOR MORE INFORMATION.

The central decision is not:

    "Will the player rise?"

It is:

    "Does the expected future points and affordability value of acting before
    the next update exceed the value of waiting for injury, rotation, lineup,
    fixture and market information?"

GOVERNING PRINCIPLE
-------------------
FPL's exact price-change algorithm is not publicly documented.

Official sources establish only broad mechanics such as:

- prices respond to transfer-market popularity;
- changes occur overnight UK time;
- a player may move by £0.1m in a day;
- historical movement has tended to be around a total of £0.3m per Gameweek;
- selling-price rules create asymmetric gains and losses.

Any exact threshold, counter-reset rule, wildcard treatment, flag protection or
ownership adjustment inferred from public observations is a hypothesis until it
is validated prospectively.

Pulse will therefore model price changes as:

    AN UNOBSERVED THRESHOLD/STATE PROCESS
    + OBSERVED TRANSFER FLOWS
    + PLAYER/STATUS/GAMEWEEK CONTEXT
    + RECURRENT COMPETING-RISK EVENTS
    + CALIBRATED MACHINE-LEARNING CHALLENGERS.

It will never present an inferred threshold as official source code.

CRITICAL PRE-LAUNCH STATUS
--------------------------
At the research cut-off, the 2026/27 FPL game had not launched.

Consequently:

1. The target-season price fields and behaviour are not yet verified.
2. All 2025/26 price mechanics are REFERENCE_ONLY.

   - 2026/27 player and manager totals exist;

4. Previous-season thresholds and community discoveries are priors/challengers,

======================================================================
0. EXECUTIVE MODEL DECISION
======================================================================

0.1 SELECTED PRODUCTION ARCHITECTURE
------------------------------------
The selected architecture has four linked layers.

LAYER A — OFFICIAL PRICE-STATE LEDGER

- current price;
- purchase price;
- selling price;
- price changes;
- ownership;
- transfer counters;
- FPL status/flag;
- Gameweek/deadline;
- last price event.

LAYER B — LATENT TRANSFER-PRESSURE STATE

A Bayesian state-space model estimates hidden positive and negative price
pressure from observed transfer flows and unobserved algorithm inputs.

LAYER C — RECURRENT COMPETING-RISK HAZARD

At each overnight update opportunity, each player can experience:

- RISE;
- FALL;
- NO_CHANGE.

Price changes are recurrent. The state and risk process restart or change after
each event.

LAYER D — CLASSIFICATION AND ENSEMBLE CHALLENGERS

- gradient-boosted multinomial classifier;
- calibrated binary rise/fall classifiers;
- interpretable threshold heuristic;
- survival/hazard baselines;
- predictive ensemble after time-safe validation.

0.2 PRIMARY V1 MODEL
--------------------
Initial primary:

- hierarchical Bayesian discrete-time competing-risk hazard;
- nightly update as the time interval;
- player/season/status random effects;
- nonlinear time-varying transfer-flow features;
- separate rise and fall mechanisms;
- event-history/gap-time terms;
- latent threshold-distance state;
- posterior predictive price paths.

0.3 CHALLENGER
--------------
Gradient-boosted trees, likely LightGBM or XGBoost, with:

- rare-event-aware training;
- time-based splits;
- calibrated probability output;
- no synthetic future leakage;
- interpretability diagnostics;
- strict comparison to the hazard model.

A classifier can outperform an interpretable threshold theory even when the
inferred theory is incomplete. It must not replace the time-to-event output or
the uncertainty model without evidence.

0.4 RECURRENT EVENT POLICY
--------------------------
A player can change price repeatedly during one Gameweek.

The model uses:

- event number;
- days/updates since last rise;
- days/updates since last fall;
- previous change direction;
- price changes this Gameweek;
- transfer pressure accumulated after the last event;
- Gameweek reset/deadline structure.

The model does not impose a fictional one-change lockout.

0.5 RISE AND FALL SEPARATION
----------------------------
Rises and falls are not symmetric.

Reasons:

- buyers come from the non-owner pool;
- sellers come from the owner pool;
- injury flags drive sales differently;
- purchase price and current price affect manager behaviour;
- community evidence suggests different reset/status behaviour;
- low ownership constrains absolute buying volume;
- price falls can accelerate after injury/news.

Pulse uses separate latent states, coefficients, calibration and confidence.

0.6 PRICE UPDATE TIME
---------------------
Official reference guidance says changes occur overnight UK time, not at a
guaranteed exact minute.

Pulse predicts the next official update event, while separately estimating an
update-time interval from prospectively observed snapshots.

It MUST NOT promise a precise change minute unless the target-season data prove
a stable batch time.

0.7 THRESHOLD DISTANCE
----------------------
`likely_threshold_distance` is a posterior estimate, not official fact.

Default unit:

    LATENT_PRESSURE_STANDARD_DEVIATIONS

Positive rise distance:

- >0 means latent rise pressure is estimated beyond the learned boundary;
- <0 means below it.

Fall distance is separate.

If prospective evidence supports a stable effective-transfer scale, Pulse MAY
also report:

    estimated_effective_managers_remaining

with a wide credible interval and a `MODEL_INFERRED` label.

0.8 EARLY TRANSFER POLICY
-------------------------
A player predicted to rise is not automatically an early buy.

A player predicted to fall is not automatically an early sell.

Pulse compares complete action utilities:

    utility(ACT_NOW)
    versus
    expected utility(WAIT and adapt after new information)

including:

- football points;
- price-path/affordability;
- selling-price mechanics;
- free-transfer/hit cost;
- injury/rotation risk;
- information value;
- reversal probability;
- loss of purchase-price position;
- execution risk;
- future transfer routes.

0.9 TEAM VALUE
--------------
Price predictions enter decisions only through the marginal future point value
of affordability, defined in DMFP-10.

The model does not maximize squad value.

0.10 OFFICIAL REFERENCE FACTS
-----------------------------
REFERENCE_ONLY 2025/26 official guidance:

- a heavily bought player may rise £0.1m per day;
- a heavily sold player may fall £0.1m per day;
- changes happen overnight UK time;
- historically players have tended to move by a total of £0.3m per Gameweek;
- profit is £0.1m for each complete £0.2m rise while owned;
- a price fall is passed through fully to selling price.

The exact threshold remains undisclosed.

======================================================================
1. PROBLEM FORMULATION
======================================================================

1.1 PRICE STATE
---------------
For player i at update opportunity t:

    P_i,t

integer tenths of £1m.

Example:

    £6.5m -> 65

1.2 EVENT
---------
Y_i,t in:

- RISE;
- FALL;
- NO_CHANGE.

Reference step:

    RISE: P_i,t+1 = P_i,t + 1
    FALL: P_i,t+1 = P_i,t - 1

Target-season configuration controls the step.

1.3 NEXT-UPDATE PROBABILITY
---------------------------
Required:

    probability_rise_next_update
      = P(Y_i,t = RISE | information at as_of)

    probability_fall_next_update
      = P(Y_i,t = FALL | information at as_of)

and:

    P(NO_CHANGE)
      = 1 - P(RISE) - P(FALL).

1.4 PRICE HORIZON
-----------------
For horizon h:

    expected_price_h
      =
      E[P_i,t+h | current information]

where h is mapped to actual expected update opportunities within 24h, 72h or
7d.

The output MUST also retain the full price PMF.

1.5 RECURRENCE
--------------
Each rise/fall changes future risk.

The price process is a recurrent event process, not one time-to-first-event
problem.

1.6 COMPETING RISKS
-------------------
Rise and fall are mutually exclusive at one nightly update.

Treating a fall as ordinary censoring in a rise-only survival model can
misestimate absolute rise probability.

Use multinomial/discrete competing-risk probabilities or coherent cause-specific
hazards.

1.7 INTERVAL CENSORING
----------------------
If Pulse observes:

- price unchanged at 01:00;
- changed at 02:00;

the event time is interval-censored.

For next-update prediction, the event label is the update cycle.
For exact timing research, preserve the censoring interval.

4. TRANSFER FLOW FEATURES
======================================================================

4.1 RAW CUMULATIVE COUNTERS
---------------------------
At snapshot t:

- transfers_in;
- transfers_out;
- transfers_in_event;
- transfers_out_event.

4.2 INCREMENTS
--------------
For interval Δt:

    buys_i = TI_i,t - TI_i,t-1
    sells_i = TO_i,t - TO_i,t-1
    net_i = buys_i - sells_i
    gross_i = buys_i + sells_i

Validate resets/corrections.

4.3 VELOCITY
------------
Per hour:

    v_net = net / elapsed_hours
    v_buy = buys / elapsed_hours
    v_sell = sells / elapsed_hours

Use robust handling of unequal snapshot intervals.

4.4 ACCELERATION
----------------
Difference in smoothed velocities:

    acceleration_3h_12h
      =
      EWMA_velocity_short - EWMA_velocity_long

Also:

- ratio;
- slope from recent intervals;
- second derivative of cumulative flow;
- momentum persistence.

4.5 EWMA WINDOWS
----------------
Configurable half-lives:

- 30m;
- 1h;
- 3h;
- 6h;
- 12h;
- 24h;
- since deadline;
- since last change.

No one decay rate is assumed to match the hidden algorithm.

4.6 CUMULATIVE PRESSURE

4.7 PRESSURE PERSISTENCE

======================================================================
5. OWNERSHIP-ADJUSTED TRANSFER RATES
======================================================================

5.1 ESTIMATED OWNER COUNT
-------------------------
Let:

    N_t = estimated active manager population
    own_i,t = selected_by_percent / 100
    owners_i,t = N_t x own_i,t
    nonowners_i,t = N_t - owners_i,t

Ownership percentage is rounded and manager-count timing uncertain.
Propagate denominator uncertainty.

5.2 BUY HAZARD AMONG NONOWNERS
------------------------------
    buy_rate_nonowners
      =
      buys / max(nonowners, floor)

This represents the fraction of eligible potential buyers transferring the
player in during the interval.

5.3 SELL HAZARD AMONG OWNERS
----------------------------
    sell_rate_owners
      =
      sells / max(owners, floor)

This is often more meaningful for falls than raw net transfers.

5.4 NET OWNERSHIP PRESSURE
--------------------------
Do not simply subtract rates with incompatible denominators.

Store separately and create calibrated transformations such as:

    logit_buy_pressure - logit_sell_pressure

or model both features.

5.5 LOW OWNERSHIP
-----------------
Low-owned players have:

- a small owner base;
- a very large nonowner pool;
- limited absolute transfer volume initially;
- rounded ownership measurement error.

No official ownership floor is assumed.

A 2026 practitioner analysis reported no rises below 1% ownership in its
four-season sample, but this remains an unverified community finding and may
reflect the absolute volume needed to reach a hidden threshold.

Use as:

- prior/feature;
- prospective hypothesis test;
- not a hard production rule.

5.6 HIGH OWNERSHIP
------------------
Raw sales can be large even when the per-owner sell rate is moderate.

Use both absolute and adjusted flow.

======================================================================
6. UNIQUE MANAGERS AND WILDCARD CONTAMINATION
======================================================================

6.1 PROBLEM
-----------
Official aggregate transfer counters may not reveal:

- unique managers;
- repeated in/out actions;
- Wildcard transfers;
- Free Hit transfers;
- automated/invalid patterns;
- how the hidden algorithm counts them.

6.2 COMMUNITY HYPOTHESES
------------------------
Historical community belief often held that Wildcard transfers were excluded or
discounted.

A 2026 FPLCore analysis using four seasons and a large sampled manager dataset
argues that:

- unique managers may matter more than raw transfer count;
- repeated transfers by one manager may be capped;
- chip-driven transfers contribute less pressure than raw transfer totals imply;
- the exact mechanism remains inferred, not source-code verified.

This is useful practitioner evidence but not controlling truth.

6.3 PRODUCTION POLICY
---------------------
Do not hard-code:

    wildcard transfers count zero

or:

    one transfer per manager is definitely the algorithm.

Instead estimate latent effective flow.

6.4 CHIP CONTAMINATION FEATURES
-------------------------------
If a lawful manager sample exists:

- Wildcard share among transferring managers;
- Free Hit share;
- unique manager count;
- repeated-transfer rate;
- normal-transfer count.

If not:

- Gameweek number;
- known Wildcard/chip windows;
- total gross transfers;
- ratio of gross to net;
- unusually broad squad churn;
- FPL chip usage aggregates if officially available;
- sample uncertainty.

6.5 MEASUREMENT MODEL
---------------------
Observed transfer flow:

    observed_flow
      =
      effective_flow
      + chip_contamination
      + repeated_manager_flow
      + measurement_noise

Latent effective flow enters the price pressure model.

6.6 UNCERTAINTY
---------------
When chip share is unknown:

- posterior pressure interval widens;
- threshold distance confidence falls;
- act-now decisions require stronger evidence.

======================================================================
10. LATENT THRESHOLD-PRESSURE MODEL
======================================================================

10.1 MOTIVATION
---------------
Pulse observes noisy proxies for the hidden demand counter.

Define latent rise pressure:

    Z_i,t^+

and fall pressure:

    Z_i,t^-.

10.2 STATE TRANSITION
---------------------
Illustrative:

    Z_i,t+1^+
      =
      rho_i,t^+ Z_i,t^+
      + beta^+ f_i,t^+
      + u_i,t^+
      - reset_i,t^+

    Z_i,t+1^-
      =
      rho_i,t^- Z_i,t^-
      + beta^- f_i,t^-
      + u_i,t^-
      - reset_i,t^-

where:

- f contains observed/ownership-adjusted flows;
- u is hidden effective-flow noise;
- rho is learned persistence;
- reset depends on price event/status/Gameweek;
- states are not required to be literal transfer counts.

10.3 EVENT BOUNDARY
-------------------
Latent boundaries:

    B_i,t^+
    B_i,t^-.

Boundary can vary by:

- season;
- direction;
- status;
- ownership regime;
- price/event history;
- hidden rules.

10.4 PROBABILISTIC CROSSING
---------------------------
Event probability can be:

    P(RISE at next update)
      =
      logistic(
        (Z^+ - B^+) / tau^+
        + baseline/calendar effects
      )

Fall analogous.

A multinomial link ensures coherent rise/fall/no-change probabilities.

10.5 STOCHASTIC THRESHOLD
-------------------------
Because hidden inputs are missing, use a distribution over B and Z.

A deterministic threshold on imperfect public flow would be falsely precise.

10.6 PARTICLE FILTER
--------------------
Candidate online inference:

- particles over latent pressure/boundary/regime;
- update with each transfer snapshot;
- condition on observed nightly event;
- resample;
- output boundary-crossing probability and distance.

Initial V1 may use posterior filtering in a simpler linear-Gaussian/nonlinear
state-space approximation.

10.7 FIRST-HITTING-TIME CONNECTION
----------------------------------
Threshold regression models event time as a latent stochastic process reaching a
boundary.

This is conceptually appropriate, but Pulse must respect the discrete nightly
decision/update mechanism.

10.8 DISTANCE
-------------
Normalized:

    D_i,t^+
      =
      (Z_i,t^+ - B_i,t^+) / scale_i^+

Report posterior distribution.

10.9 INTERPRETABILITY
---------------------
Threshold distance answers:

"How strongly does the observed hidden-pressure model suggest the player is
near or beyond a rise boundary?"

It does not answer:

"What exact transfer count is in FPL source code?"

======================================================================
11. DISCRETE-TIME COMPETING-RISK HAZARD
======================================================================

11.1 TIME UNIT
--------------
One official overnight price-update opportunity.

11.2 MULTINOMIAL HAZARD
-----------------------
For event k in {RISE, FALL, NO_CHANGE}:

    P(Y_i,t=k | at risk, X_i,t)
      =
      softmax_k(eta_i,t,k)

11.3 LINEAR PREDICTOR
---------------------
eta includes:

- latent threshold distances;
- transfer flow;
- ownership-adjusted rates;
- acceleration;
- event history;
- flag/status;
- price;
- deadline calendar;
- wildcard contamination;
- player/season effects;
- interactions.

11.4 HIERARCHICAL EFFECTS
-------------------------
- season;
- player;
- price bracket;
- ownership bracket;
- position;
- status regime;
- update/day;
- prior event direction.

11.5 GAP-TIME MODEL
-------------------
Price-change recurrence resembles recurrent survival data.

Use gap time since last event and event-order-specific baseline hazards,
analogous in spirit to Prentice–Williams–Peterson recurrent-event approaches.

11.6 ANDERSEN–GILL CHALLENGER
-----------------------------
A counting-process model treats repeated events with time-varying covariates.

Useful baseline, but may underrepresent event-order/reset dependence.

11.7 FRAILTY
------------
Player/season frailty/random effects capture persistent unobserved
change-proneness.

11.8 TIME-VARYING COVARIATES
----------------------------
Transfer rates and flags are updated through the risk interval.
Use the last valid values before the nightly update.

11.9 CALIBRATED PROBABILITY
---------------------------
Survival discrimination alone is insufficient.
Absolute next-update probabilities must be calibrated.

======================================================================
12. CLASSIFICATION APPROACHES
======================================================================

12.1 TARGET
-----------
Multiclass:

- rise;
- fall;
- no change.

Alternatively:

- rise versus not;
- fall versus not;

with coherent reconciliation.

12.2 MODELS
-----------
Baselines:

- logistic/multinomial logistic;
- penalized splines/GAM;
- random forest;
- gradient-boosted trees.

Primary challenger:

- LightGBM/XGBoost.

12.3 NONLINEAR VALUE
--------------------
Tree models can capture:

- threshold interactions;
- ownership/flow regimes;
- post-event reset;
- flags;
- deadline timing;
- acceleration.

12.4 CLASS IMBALANCE
--------------------
Price changes are rare relative to player-update rows.

Do not judge by accuracy.

12.5 WEIGHTING/RESAMPLING
-------------------------
Class weights can improve rare-event identification but alter probability
calibration.

If used:

- train weights documented;
- probabilities recalibrated on natural prevalence;
- evaluation uses untouched prevalence;
- no random oversampling across time.

12.6 CALIBRATION
----------------
Candidate:

- Platt/logistic;
- beta calibration;
- isotonic with enough data;
- multiclass calibration.

Fit on walk-forward validation only.

12.7 ENSEMBLE
-------------
Initial:

- hazard/state model primary;
- GBDT challenger;
- interpretable threshold baseline.

Future stacking by out-of-time log score.

13. PRICE-PATH SIMULATION
======================================================================

13.1 PURPOSE
------------
Next-update probability is insufficient for:

- 24h;
- 72h;
- 7d;
- multiple changes;
- team-value decisions.

13.2 SIMULATION STATE
---------------------
For each path:

- current price;
- latent rise/fall pressure;
- ownership;
- transfer momentum;
- flag/status;
- last event;
- Gameweek/deadline;
- model parameters;
- future news/football scenarios.

13.3 UPDATE LOOP
----------------
For every expected nightly opportunity:

1. evolve transfer-flow process;
2. update ownership and latent pressure;
3. sample status/news scenario;
4. calculate rise/fall/no-change hazard;
5. sample price event;
6. apply configured price step;
7. reset/update state;
8. continue.

13.4 FUTURE TRANSFER FLOW
-------------------------
Model:

- self-exciting/momentum process;
- decay/reversion;
- match/news triggers;
- ownership pool limits;
- deadline cycle;
- price event feedback.

A Hawkes/self-exciting challenger MAY model transfer bursts, but a simpler
state-space/count model is preferred initially.

13.5 CONDITIONAL PATHS
----------------------
Price paths are coupled to DMFP-07/09 scenarios:

- injury/benching -> sales;
- haul/role confirmation -> buys;
- fixture news -> flow;
- price change itself -> altered flow.

Avoid double counting the same simulated news in both price and utility.

13.6 PRICE BOUNDS
-----------------
Use target-season official bounds if published.
Otherwise learn observed/catalogue limits and validate.

13.7 OUTPUT PMF
----------------
At each horizon:

- exact price-unit probabilities;
- expected price;
- quantiles;
- any rise/fall;
- number of changes;
- maximum/minimum.

13.8 SELLING VALUE
------------------
For an owned player, also simulate selling-price distribution under the
purchase-price rules.

14. MULTIPLE CHANGES WITHIN A GAMEWEEK
======================================================================

14.1 RECURRENT PATH
-------------------
A price event does not end the forecast.

14.2 SAME-DIRECTION MOMENTUM
----------------------------
Post-rise demand can remain high.
Post-fall selling can remain high.

Use event-direction-specific transition.

14.3 OPPOSITE DIRECTION
-----------------------
Possible but historically may be less likely over short gaps.

Estimate, do not prohibit.

14.4 GAMEWEEK COUNTS
--------------------
Output:

- P(0 changes);
- P(1 rise);
- P(2+ rises);
- P(1 fall);
- P(2+ falls);
- P(mixed direction).

14.5 HISTORICAL CAP
-------------------
Do not impose ±£0.3 as a hard cap solely because official guidance says
historically prices have tended to move by that total.

18. EARLY TRANSFER DECISION MODEL
======================================================================

18.1 DECISION TIMES
-------------------
Compare:

- ACT_NOW;
- WAIT_UNTIL information event;
- WAIT_UNTIL latest safe time;
- DO_NOT_TRANSFER;
- alternative transfer route.

18.2 STATE
----------
Includes:

- current squad;
- bank;
- free transfers;
- hits;
- purchase/selling prices;
- predicted player points;
- injury/rotation;
- price paths;
- next information;
- future routes.

18.3 VALUE OF ACTING NOW
------------------------
Components:

- avoid target price rise;
- avoid outgoing price fall;
- secure affordability;
- future team-value point value;
- immediate football points if relevant;
- preserve another route before price boundary.

Costs:

- injury/rotation/news uncertainty;
- target may not remain preferred;
- current player may become better;
- transfer cannot be reversed for free;
- purchase-price position lost;
- free transfer consumed;
- hit;
- operational/execution risk.

18.4 VALUE OF WAITING
---------------------
Benefits:

- press conference;
- injury/training update;
- European/cup match;
- market move;
- fixture announcement;
- more role evidence;
- retained transfer option.

Costs:

- target rises;
- outgoing falls;
- alternative rises;
- execution risk;
- reduced time to react;
- missed current-price route.

18.5 EXPECTED UTILITY
--------------------
For action timing τ:

    EU(τ)
      =
      E[
        remaining-season FPL points
        + terminal/marginal affordability value
        - hits
        - forced future transfer costs
        - lock-in/reversal cost
      ]

under joint football, information and price scenarios.

18.6 DECISION DIFFERENCE
------------------------
    Delta_EU_early
      =
      EU(ACT_NOW)
      -
      E_Y[max_a EU(a | information Y at wait time)]

Act now if Delta is positive after safety/execution constraints.

18.7 VALUE OF £0.1M
-------------------
Use DMFP-10 counterfactual optimiser:

    V_0.1
      =
      optimal future expected points with price route preserved
      -
      optimal future expected points if route is lost.

This may be:

- zero;
- small;
- large near a price-bracket bottleneck.

18.8 SELLING-PRICE ASYMMETRY
----------------------------
Buying before one £0.1m target rise:

- saves £0.1m purchase cost;
- does not automatically create £0.1m sale profit.

Avoiding one £0.1m fall on an owned player:

- can protect £0.1m selling value immediately, subject to current purchase/current
  relationship.

The exact route is manager-specific.

18.9 INJURY/ROTATION RISK
-------------------------
Use DMFP-07 distributions, not generic penalty.

Cost of acting early includes:

- probability target unavailable;
- expected forced reversal;
- points lost;
- transfer/hit required.

18.10 NEW INFORMATION
---------------------
Use DMFP-10 EVSI.

High EVSI and low price risk -> WAIT.

High price-route value and low EVSI -> ACT.

18.11 DECISION OUTPUT
---------------------
- ACT_NOW;
- WAIT;
- DO_NOT_TRANSFER;
- MANUAL_REVIEW.

Include:

- expected utility difference;
- price component;
- football component;
- information value;
- reversal risk;
- switch probability;
- latest safe time.

19. EARLY TRANSFER HARD GATES
======================================================================

19.1 TARGET QUALITY
-------------------
Do not buy early solely for price if the target is not a strong football
candidate.

19.2 ACTIVE MATCH
-----------------
Normally avoid early transfer before the target/current player completes a
competitive match unless utility strongly clears injury/event risk.

19.3 EUROPEAN/CUP MATCH
-----------------------
Explicit risk event.

19.4 FLAGGED TARGET
-------------------
Manual review or wide scenario.

19.5 HIT
--------
Price-only hit requires extraordinary marginal future point value.

Reference four-point transfer cost means a speculative £0.1m alone is usually
insufficient.

19.6 FREE TRANSFER CAP
----------------------
If otherwise losing a free transfer at the cap, acting can be more valuable.

19.7 MULTIPLE ROUTES
--------------------
If price rise closes only one route but near-equivalent alternatives remain,
value is lower.

19.8 DEADLINE BUFFER
--------------------
Do not wait beyond safe execution time even if more information could arrive.
