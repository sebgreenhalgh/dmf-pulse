PURPOSE
-------
This document defines the mathematical and operational layer that converts raw
bookmaker and exchange prices into probability evidence for DMF Pulse.

It covers:

- odds-format conversion;
- raw implied probabilities;
- bookmaker overround;
- proportional/multiplicative normalisation;
- power normalisation;
- Shin normalisation;
- favourite–longshot bias;
- incomplete and nonexclusive markets;
- exchange back/lay prices;
- commission;
- source, bookmaker and liquidity weighting;
- freshness;
- stale and frozen quotes;
- outlier rejection;
- robust market consensus;
- market disagreement;
- uncertainty bounds;
- suspended and reopened markets;
- missing player markets;
- prospective calibration;
- audit, tests and backtesting.

This module does not claim that betting odds are the true probability.

It treats odds as:

    PRICE-BASED, MARGIN-CONTAMINATED, SOURCE-DEPENDENT,
    TIME-STAMPED AND IMPERFECT PROBABILITY EVIDENCE.

The output is designed for the football-event engine, not for placing bets.

GOVERNING PRINCIPLE
-------------------
The market layer must preserve four distinct concepts:

1. RAW IMPLIED PROBABILITY
   The reciprocal of quoted decimal odds.

2. SOURCE FAIR PROBABILITY
   One operator's probability after margin/commission treatment under a declared
   method.

3. MARKET CONSENSUS PROBABILITY
   A robust combination of independent, eligible source fair probabilities.

4. CALIBRATED CONSENSUS PROBABILITY
   The consensus after an out-of-time calibration model, where enough historical
   evidence exists.

These values MUST NOT be collapsed into one undocumented number.

IMPORTANT FREE-DATA CONTEXT
---------------------------
DMFP-04 selects a zero-subscription data stack.

Consequences:

- provider coverage can be sparse;
- bookmakers may be US-only for free EPL player props;
- exchange prices are delayed;
- full exchange liquidity may be unavailable;
- assist/shots/save markets may be missing;
- historical player-prop data are incomplete;
- source weights must be learned prospectively;
- missing markets must not be treated as zero probabilities.

The correct architecture is therefore:

    robust where multiple sources exist;
    explicit and conservative where only one source exists;
    model-assisted where the market is incomplete;
    never falsely precise.

======================================================================
0. EXECUTIVE MATHEMATICAL DECISION
======================================================================

0.1 PRODUCTION DEFAULTS
-----------------------
A. COMPLETE FIXED-ODDS MARKETS

Examples:
- 1X2;
- Over/Under with both sides;
- Yes/No;
- complete mutually exclusive correct-score book including an explicit "other"
  outcome.

Primary de-vigging method:
POWER.

Baselines/sensitivity:
- PROPORTIONAL;
- SHIN where mathematically applicable;
- ADDITIVE diagnostic only;
- future learned favourite–longshot calibration.

B. BINARY FIXED-ODDS MARKETS

Primary:
POWER.

Baseline:
PROPORTIONAL.

Shin:
Calculated only as diagnostic because in two-outcome markets the standard Shin
solution is equivalent to the additive method under the cited formulation, and
the additive method can behave poorly in wider/many-runner contexts.

C. THREE-WAY FOOTBALL RESULT

Primary:
POWER.

Sensitivity:
SHIN and PROPORTIONAL.

Future:
A one-parameter favourite–longshot calibration or other learned calibrator may
be promoted after time-safe validation.

D. EXCHANGE

Primary informational probability:
midpoint of reciprocal best-back and best-lay probabilities:

    q_back = 1 / O_back
    q_lay  = 1 / O_lay

where O_back < O_lay in a normal positive spread.

Probability interval:

    lower_raw = q_lay
    upper_raw = q_back

Informational midpoint:

    p_exchange = (q_back + q_lay) / 2

The midpoint is taken in probability space, not odds space.

Commission:
not applied to the default informational probability.
Commission-adjusted executable break-even bounds are stored separately.

E. CONSENSUS

Initial production method:
robust, weighted arithmetic pool of proper operator-level probability vectors,
followed by identity calibration until enough prospective evidence exists.

Why:
- simple and auditable;
- preserves the probability simplex;
- handles missing source weights;
- supports robust filtering;
- can be recalibrated;
- avoids pretending operator forecasts are independent.

F. WEIGHTS

Initial:
equal weight by independent betting operator, modified by:

- freshness;
- semantic/settlement compatibility;
- market completeness;
- source quality;
- exchange spread/liquidity where available;
- duplicate/upstream dependence.

Future:
rolling out-of-time log-score weights with hierarchical shrinkage toward equal
weights.

G. OUTLIERS

Hard exclusion:
semantic, identity, market-state or timestamp failure.

Statistical disagreement:
retained and flagged unless there is evidence of error.

Robust screening:
- logit median/MAD for binary outcomes;
- Jensen–Shannon divergence from preliminary consensus for multinomial vectors.

H. UNCERTAINTY BOUNDS

`lower_bound` and `upper_bound` are operational market-uncertainty bounds, not a
claim of an exact frequentist confidence interval.

They combine:

- operator dispersion;
- de-vigging method uncertainty;
- exchange spread;
- source freshness;
- calibration uncertainty;
- historical residual floor.

I. MISSING PLAYER MARKET

A missing anytime-goalscorer, assist, shots or saves quote is never zero.

Market states are explicit.
The football-event engine receives:

- available market evidence;
- its age;
- market missing reason;
- confidence;
- the need for proprietary team-to-player allocation.

0.2 KEY SCIENTIFIC POSITION
---------------------------
Research does not establish one universally best odds-normalisation method.

Evidence includes:

- Štrumbelj found Shin probabilities more accurate than basic normalisation in
  broad sports/bookmaker data and found bookmaker/exchange differences;
- Clarke, Kovalchik and Ingram found the power method generally superior to
  proportional normalisation and comparable to or better than Shin in their
  datasets;
- recent 2026 work argues that Multiplicative, Shin and Power can retain
  systematic football biases and proposes alternatives;
- recent work also questions interpreting event-level Shin estimates literally
  as insider activity.

Therefore Pulse will:

- implement multiple methods;
- choose by time-safe predictive performance;
- keep the production default simple;
- retain sensitivity outputs;
- avoid ideological commitment to one bookmaker theory.

1.1 IN SCOPE

1.2 OUT OF SCOPE
----------------
This document does not:

- allocate team goals to players;
- generate FPL points;
- optimise a squad;
- decide whether a bet has value;
- place bets;
- infer bookmaker settlement rules from names;
- create data not available before the FPL deadline.

1.3 OPERATOR
------------
The bookmaker or exchange originating/hosting the price.

1.4 PROVIDER
------------
The API/feed through which Pulse receives the price.

Multiple providers can carry the same operator.
They do not count as independent market opinions.

1.5 BOOK
--------
A complete set of related, mutually exclusive/exhaustive outcomes quoted by one
operator under one settlement definition.

1.6 OVERROUND / BOOKSUM
-----------------------
For raw inverse odds q_i:

    S = sum_i q_i

For an exactly-one-winner complete market:

    overround M = S - 1

For a fair complete book:
S = 1.

1.7 PAYOUT INDEX
----------------
A simple diagnostic:

    payout_index = 1 / S

This is not a universal exact expected return because stake distribution and
outcome-specific margins matter.

1.8 PROPER PROBABILITY VECTOR
-----------------------------
Every component lies in [0,1] and sums to the market's target total.

For exactly one winner:
sum = 1.

1.9 NONEXCLUSIVE MARKET
-----------------------
Multiple selections can win.

Example:
Anytime goalscorer.

Player probabilities do not sum to one because several players may score.

1.10 INCOMPLETE MARKET
----------------------
Not all required outcomes are observed.

Example:
Only "Yes, player scores" is returned, with no corresponding "No" price.

1.11 FAIR PROBABILITY
---------------------
An estimate after removing/adjusting margin under a declared model.
It is not guaranteed truth.

1.12 CONSENSUS
--------------
An aggregation of eligible, mapped, independently counted operator probability
estimates.

======================================================================
3. ODDS FORMAT CONVERSION
======================================================================

3.1 DECIMAL
-----------
For decimal odds O > 1:

    raw implied probability q = 1 / O

3.2 FRACTIONAL
--------------
For fractional odds a/b:

    O_decimal = 1 + a/b

Then:

    q = 1 / O_decimal

3.3 AMERICAN
------------
For positive American odds +A:

    O_decimal = 1 + A/100

For negative American odds -A:

    O_decimal = 1 + 100/abs(A)

Then:

    q = 1 / O_decimal

3.4 EXCHANGE
------------
Back and lay remain separate quote types.
Do not collapse them before spread analysis.

3.5 EXACT STORAGE
-----------------
Source odds are stored as exact Decimal values where practical.
Numerical optimisation may convert to floating-point after validation.

3.6 INVALID
-----------
Reject/quarantine:

- decimal <= 1 for a conventional positive-return market;
- zero/negative denominator;
- unparsable odds;
- odds outside provider/domain limit;
- line mismatch;
- price from a closed/void market used as current.

======================================================================
4. RAW IMPLIED PROBABILITIES AND MARGIN
======================================================================

4.1 RAW
-------
For outcome i:

    q_i = 1 / O_i

4.2 COMPLETE EXACTLY-ONE MARKET
-------------------------------
Booksum:

    S = sum_i q_i

Overround:

    M = S - 1

Expected normal fixed-odds case:
S > 1.

4.3 UNDERROUND
--------------
S < 1 can occur because of:

- best prices combined across operators;
- stale quotes;
- incomplete book;
- promotion;
- exchange prices;
- data error;
- arbitrage.

A single-operator complete fixed-odds underround triggers review.

4.4 TARGET SUM
--------------
Most Pulse markets target one.

For multiple-winner markets, a target sum greater than one is valid only when
the market definition truly represents a known number of winners, such as top-k.

Anytime scorer does not have a fixed count of winning selections and must not be
normalised to an arbitrary target sum.

4.5 MARGIN DIAGNOSTICS
----------------------
Store:

- booksum;
- overround;
- payout index;
- outcome-specific raw probability;
- margin percentile by operator/market;
- margin change;
- completeness;
- de-vig method.

======================================================================
5. PROPORTIONAL / MULTIPLICATIVE NORMALISATION
======================================================================

5.1 FORMULA
-----------
For a complete exactly-one-winner market:

    p_i = q_i / sum_j q_j

5.2 INTERPRETATION
------------------
The method allocates margin in proportion to raw implied probability.

5.3 ADVANTAGES
--------------
- simple;
- deterministic;
- always proper for positive complete book;
- no iteration;
- useful baseline;
- easy to audit.

5.4 LIMITATIONS
---------------
- assumes proportional distortion;
- does not explicitly model favourite–longshot bias;
- can be less accurate than alternatives;
- does not solve incomplete/nonexclusive markets.

5.5 PRODUCTION ROLE
-------------------
Always computed.

Used as:
- baseline;
- fallback if numerical method fails;
- sensitivity output;
- diagnostic comparison.

Not the primary method for a normal complete book unless validation later shows
it performs best for that market/operator.

======================================================================
6. POWER METHOD
======================================================================

6.1 FORMULA
-----------
Let:

    q_i = 1 / O_i

Find exponent alpha > 0 such that:

    sum_i q_i^alpha = 1

Then:

    p_i = q_i^alpha

For a normal overround book where sum q_i > 1:
alpha is usually greater than one.

Equivalent parameterisations may use an inverse exponent. Pulse stores the
actual applied alpha unambiguously.

6.2 ROOT FINDING
----------------
Solve:

    f(alpha) = sum_i q_i^alpha - 1 = 0

For q_i in (0,1), f is monotonic decreasing.

Use:
- bracketed Brent method;
- deterministic tolerance;
- maximum iterations;
- safe fallback.

Suggested initial bracket:
[epsilon, alpha_max], expanded until sign change.

6.3 ADVANTAGES
--------------
- proper probabilities;
- no negative probabilities;
- preserves ranking;
- flexibly allocates greater proportional margin to longshots;
- conceptually and computationally simple;
- empirical support over proportional normalisation in published datasets.

6.4 LIMITATIONS
---------------
- one distortion shape per book;
- not guaranteed best by market/operator;
- can retain systematic draw or market-specific bias;
- assumes complete book;
- no direct theory of source disagreement.

6.5 PRODUCTION DECISION
-----------------------
Primary initial method for complete fixed-odds markets.

6.6 FAILURE
-----------
If root cannot be found or output fails tolerance:

- record numerical failure;
- use proportional fallback;
- lower confidence;
- emit alert if recurring.

6.7 OUTPUT
----------
- alpha;
- fair probabilities;
- convergence;
- iterations;
- residual;
- method version.

======================================================================
7. SHIN METHOD
======================================================================

7.1 BACKGROUND
--------------
Shin's model was motivated by a bookmaker facing bettors with superior
information.

It produces a source fair-probability vector and an estimated parameter z.

Pulse treats z as a model parameter/diagnostic.
It MUST NOT label z as a literal measured percentage of insider trading in
production output.

7.2 FORMULA
-----------
For inverse odds q_i and booksum S:

    p_i(z) =
      [ sqrt( z^2 + 4(1-z) q_i^2 / S ) - z ]
      / [ 2(1-z) ]

Find z such that:

    sum_i p_i(z) = 1

7.3 NUMERICAL METHOD
--------------------
Use:

- bounded root solver;
- z in an admissible range, normally [0, 1);
- strict sum tolerance;
- stable handling near z = 1;
- proportional fallback.

7.4 ADVANTAGES
--------------
- adjusts margin nonproportionally;
- empirically outperformed basic normalisation in Štrumbelj's multi-sport
  analysis;
- widely used benchmark;
- useful sensitivity to favourite–longshot distortion.

7.5 LIMITATIONS
---------------
- theoretical assumptions may not describe modern competitive/aggregated books;
- event-level z interpretation has been questioned;
- numerical complexity;
- for two-outcome markets the standard formulation is equivalent to additive
  margin removal;
- not guaranteed best for football draws or player markets;
- requires a complete mutually exclusive book.

7.6 PRODUCTION ROLE
-------------------
Compute for:

- 1X2;
- complete correct-score;
- other complete multi-outcome books.

Use as:
- sensitivity;
- method-disagreement input;
- challenger model.

Not initial default.

7.7 INVALID OUTPUT
------------------
If:

- no admissible root;
- p outside [0,1];
- residual too high;
- pathological z;

mark Shin unavailable and do not force a result.

8. ADDITIVE AND OTHER DIAGNOSTIC METHODS
======================================================================

8.1 ADDITIVE
------------
Formula for n outcomes:

    p_i = q_i - (sum(q)-1)/n

Problem:
can produce negative probabilities, especially with many longshots.

Use:
diagnostic only.

8.2 MARGIN WEIGHTS PROPORTIONAL TO ODDS
---------------------------------------
May be implemented as a research challenger because it explicitly allocates
more margin to longer odds.

Not production default.

8.3 ODDS-RATIO METHOD
---------------------
May be implemented as a challenger.

Find common odds-ratio parameter such that transformed probabilities sum to one.

8.4 OO-EPC AND RECENT METHODS
-----------------------------
A 2026 preprint proposes an odds-only equal-profitability-confidence method and
reports improvements over Multiplicative, Shin and Power in a large football
dataset.

Status:
RESEARCH_CANDIDATE.

Promotion requires:

- code/formula audit;
- independent implementation;
- reproduction;
- EPL/market-type out-of-time test;
- comparison under the zero-cost source mix;
- no leakage.

8.5 LEARNED FAVOURITE–LONGSHOT GLM
----------------------------------
The same 2026 work proposes a one-parameter favourite–longshot adjusted GLM.

Candidate after enough prospective data.

======================================================================
10. FAVOURITE–LONGSHOT BIAS
======================================================================

10.1 DEFINITION
---------------
Common empirical pattern:

- longshots are priced with disproportionately high implied probability;
- favourites may be relatively underpriced in probability terms.

The pattern is not universal and varies by:

- bookmaker;
- sport;
- market;
- number of outcomes;
- liquidity;
- time to event;
- customer mix.

10.2 DO NOT HARD-CODE A UNIVERSAL CORRECTION
--------------------------------------------
Pulse MUST NOT assume:

- every bookmaker has the same bias;
- every player prop behaves like 1X2;
- every exchange is unbiased;
- the bias is stable through time.

10.3 DIAGNOSTICS
----------------
By operator/market/lead-time:

- reliability curve;
- calibration intercept;
- calibration slope;
- log score by raw probability bin;
- observed frequency;
- expected payout/return diagnostic;
- margin by probability;
- method comparison.

10.4 CALIBRATION SLOPE
----------------------
Binary logistic calibration:

    logit(P(Y=1)) = a + b * logit(p_market)

Interpretation:

- a != 0: calibration-in-the-large shift;
- b < 1: probabilities too extreme;
- b > 1: probabilities not extreme enough.

Interpret cautiously under selection/missingness.

10.5 DRAW BIAS
--------------
Football 1X2 methods can mis-handle draws even if they adjust general
favourite–longshot bias.

Report outcome-specific calibration.

10.6 PROSPECTIVE POLICY
-----------------------
No learned correction is activated until a minimum evidence threshold and
walk-forward validation are specified in DMFP-15.

======================================================================
11. BINARY AND PLAYER-PROP MARKETS
======================================================================

11.1 COMPLETE BINARY
--------------------
If both Yes and No are offered by the same operator under identical settlement:

    q_yes = 1/O_yes
    q_no  = 1/O_no

Apply POWER or proportional to the two-outcome book.

11.2 ONE-SIDED YES QUOTE
------------------------
If only Yes is observed:

    q_yes = 1/O_yes

This is a raw implied probability, not a de-vigged probability.

Store:

normalisation_status = INCOMPLETE_ONE_SIDED
market_probability = null unless an approved calibration/paired reconstruction
exists
raw_implied_probability = q_yes

11.3 ONE-SIDED CALIBRATION
--------------------------
A future model may estimate fair probability from one-sided odds using:

- operator;
- market type;
- raw q;
- typical margin;
- lead time;
- source;
- historical outcome.

This is a learned calibration model, not margin removal from the current book.

11.4 NONEXCLUSIVE PLAYER SET
----------------------------
Anytime scorers are not a mutually exclusive book.

Multiple players can score.

Therefore:

- do not divide player q values by their sum;
- do not force sum to one;
- do not apply Shin across the player list;
- do not call the sum an overround unless the market structure justifies it.

11.5 TEAM RECONCILIATION
------------------------
Player goal probabilities later feed DMFP-08, which reconciles them with team
goal distributions.

This module provides observations and uncertainty, not an arbitrary player
probability simplex.

11.6 MULTIPLE WINNER/TOP-K
--------------------------
Only use target sum k where the market guarantees exactly k winning selections
under the relevant definition.

======================================================================
12. EXCHANGE PRICES
======================================================================

12.1 BEST BACK AND LAY
----------------------
Let:

- B = best available back decimal odds;
- L = best available lay decimal odds;
- normally B < L.

Reciprocal probability endpoints:

    q_back = 1/B
    q_lay  = 1/L

Because L > B:

    q_lay < q_back

No-arbitrage informational interval:

    [q_lay, q_back]

12.2 PROBABILITY MIDPOINT
-------------------------
Default:

    p_mid = (q_lay + q_back)/2

Reason:
the engine needs probability evidence and probability-space spread.

12.3 ODDS MIDPOINT
------------------
Odds midpoint:

    1 / ((B+L)/2)

is stored as a diagnostic but is not the default because odds space is nonlinear
in probability.

12.4 LOGIT MIDPOINT
-------------------
Research challenger:

    p_logit_mid =
      logistic((logit(q_lay)+logit(q_back))/2)

May be compared prospectively.

12.5 LAST TRADED PRICE
----------------------
Last traded price is:

- stale if no recent trade;
- transaction-specific;
- useful with age and volume;
- not automatically superior to current executable spread.

12.6 AVAILABLE SIZE
-------------------
If available:

- depth at best back;
- depth at best lay;
- multiple ladder levels;
- total available;
- total matched.

A minimum size threshold is market/price/currency-specific.

12.7 LIQUIDITY ABSENT
---------------------
Under the free delayed/basic architecture, full depth may not be available.

Set:

liquidity_status = UNKNOWN_OR_PARTIAL

Do not invent a liquidity weight.

12.8 CROSS-MATCHING
-------------------
Betfair documents whether cross matching is enabled.
Exchange prices may reflect cross-matching mechanics.

Store market flag.
Do not treat exchange runners as independent bookmaker quotes.

======================================================================
13. EXCHANGE COMMISSION
======================================================================

13.1 OFFICIAL PRINCIPLE
-----------------------
Betfair commission is charged on net winnings on a market, not on each losing
bet and not mechanically on every quote.

The rate is account/jurisdiction/package dependent.

13.2 BACK BREAK-EVEN
--------------------
For a standalone unit back stake at odds B and commission c on winning profit:

Winning profit after commission:

    (B-1)(1-c)

Break-even event probability:

    p_back_be =
      1 / [1 + (B-1)(1-c)]

13.3 LAY BREAK-EVEN
-------------------
For a lay accepting a unit opposing stake at lay odds L:

- win if event does not occur: 1-c;
- loss if event occurs: L-1.

Break-even event probability:

    p_lay_be =
      (1-c)/(L-c)

13.4 INTERVAL
-------------
Commission-adjusted executable interval:

    [p_lay_be, p_back_be]

This is generally wider than the raw reciprocal interval.

13.5 PRODUCTION DECISION
------------------------
For football forecasting consensus:

- use commission-free market information;
- store commission-adjusted trading bounds separately;
- do not embed Sebastian's account commission in universal player probabilities.

13.6 NET-MARKET CAVEAT
----------------------
Actual commission depends on net market winnings and other positions.
The standalone formulas are diagnostics, not exact portfolio settlement.

======================================================================
14. BOOKMAKER AND SOURCE WEIGHTING
======================================================================

14.1 COUNT OPERATORS, NOT API ROWS
----------------------------------
If API-FOOTBALL and The Odds API both carry bookmaker X:

- X counts once;
- choose best/freshest valid representation;
- provider redundancy increases data confidence, not market opinion count.

14.2 INITIAL OPERATOR WEIGHT
----------------------------
Start:

    base_operator_weight = 1

Then apply factors:

    w_raw =
        w_reliability
      * w_freshness
      * w_semantics
      * w_completeness
      * w_independence
      * w_margin_quality
      * w_liquidity_if_exchange

Normalize accepted weights to sum one.

14.3 INITIAL FACTORS
--------------------
Reliability:
1 until enough prospective evidence, except known broken/degraded source.

Semantics:
1 for verified compatible settlement.
0 for incompatible/unresolved critical settlement.

Completeness:
1 for complete book.
lower or excluded for incomplete depending use.

Independence:
reduce for same operator/white-label/upstream duplicate.

Margin quality:
soft preference to lower-margin complete books, capped to avoid making margin a
proxy for truth.

14.4 LEARNED RELIABILITY
------------------------
Rolling out-of-time score:

- negative mean log loss or skill;
- sample-size uncertainty;
- market type;
- lead time;
- operator.

Shrink estimate toward equal/global weight.

14.5 WEIGHT CAP
---------------
Initial maximum normalised operator weight:

    0.35

Configurable.

Reason:
no single free source/operator should dominate solely due sparse coverage.

Exception:
if only one eligible source, weight = 1 but confidence is low.

14.6 SHARP BOOK LABEL
---------------------
Do not permanently hard-code a bookmaker as "sharp."

Use observed accuracy and margin.
Provider marketing inclusion such as "Pinnacle" is not itself a weight.

14.7 MANUAL QUOTE
-----------------
Manual quote can receive normal operator weight after validation.
Its ingestion method does not make the operator less informative, but
transcription risk lowers source-quality confidence.

======================================================================
15. LIQUIDITY WEIGHTING
======================================================================

15.1 PURPOSE
------------
Liquidity can indicate that an exchange price is supported by meaningful
capital rather than a token unmatched quote.

15.2 CANDIDATE INPUTS
---------------------
- total matched;
- runner matched;
- available size at best prices;
- depth within probability distance;
- spread;
- recent trade count;
- quote age.

15.3 NORMALISATION
------------------
Raw money values vary by market.

Convert to within-market/market-type features:

- log1p(total matched);
- liquidity percentile by lead time;
- spread in probability basis points;
- depth relative to historical market median.

15.4 INITIAL POLICY
-------------------
If reliable size is available:

    w_liquidity =
      clipped function of:
      higher depth,
      higher matched volume,
      lower spread.

Cap in [0.5, 1.5] before final normalisation.

If unavailable:
1 for source aggregation, but confidence grade notes unknown liquidity.

15.5 DO NOT DOUBLE COUNT
------------------------
Spread already affects uncertainty.
Do not let both spread and liquidity weight over-penalise the same thin market
without validation.

16. FRESHNESS AND STALE-MARKET DETECTION
======================================================================

16.1 FRESHNESS AGE
------------------
Preferred age:

    decision_time - source_updated_at

If missing:

    decision_time - received_at

and:
freshness_basis = RECEIPT_TIME.

16.2 MARKET-TYPE HALF-LIFE
--------------------------
Freshness weight:

    w_freshness = exp(-ln(2) * age / half_life)

subject to hard stale cutoff.

Initial configurable guidance:

More than 24h before relevant cutoff:
- half-life 6h;
- hard stale 24h.

24h to 6h:
- half-life 90m;
- hard stale 6h.

6h to 2h:
- half-life 30m;
- hard stale 2h.

Inside 2h before relevant cutoff:
- half-life 10m;
- hard stale 30m.

For ten-minute/free delayed sources:
thresholds must reflect documented cadence and be displayed.

16.3 FPL DEADLINE VERSUS KICKOFF
-------------------------------
For an FPL decision, freshness is evaluated at the FPL deadline.

A match may kick off much later.
A quote updated after the FPL deadline is ineligible even if closer to kickoff.

16.4 FROZEN QUOTE DETECTION
---------------------------
Flag FROZEN when:

- quote has not updated across expected cycles;
- peers moved materially;
- market remains apparently open;
- source age exceeds expected cadence;
- provider heartbeat continues.

Candidate movement trigger:

absolute logit movement of peer consensus > configured threshold.

16.5 STALE STATES
-----------------
FRESH
AGING
STALE_SOFT
STALE_HARD
FROZEN
SOURCE_TIME_UNKNOWN
POST_CUTOFF

16.6 HARD STALE
---------------
Excluded from current consensus.

May remain in:
- historical trace;
- fallback diagnostic;
- last-known view with explicit state.

======================================================================
18. HARD VALIDATION AND OUTLIER REJECTION
======================================================================

18.1 HARD INVALID
-----------------
Exclude before statistics:

- wrong fixture;
- wrong player/team;
- wrong period;
- wrong line;
- incompatible settlement;
- in-play price in pre-match consensus;
- post-cutoff;
- suspended/closed;
- odds <= 1;
- unresolved mapping;
- duplicate operator representation;
- incomplete book used as complete;
- stale hard.

18.2 STATISTICAL OUTLIER IS NOT AUTOMATIC ERROR
-----------------------------------------------
A genuinely different price can contain information.

Statistical outliers are:

- retained in raw data;
- flagged;
- downweighted or excluded only under declared policy;
- included in disagreement diagnostics.

18.3 BINARY ROBUST SCREEN
-------------------------
For eligible source probabilities p_j:

    x_j = logit(clip(p_j, eps, 1-eps))

Preliminary center:
weighted median x_med.

Scale:

    MAD = median(|x_j - x_med|)

    robust_scale = 1.4826 * MAD

Robust z:

    z_j = |x_j - x_med| / max(robust_scale, scale_floor)

Policy:

- z <= 3: normal;
- 3 < z <= 6: soft downweight / flag;
- z > 6: exclude only if corroborated by stale/semantic/source issue;
  otherwise retain as high disagreement and human-review candidate.

18.4 MULTINOMIAL SCREEN
-----------------------
1. Build preliminary equal/quality-weight linear pool p_bar.
2. Compute Jensen–Shannon divergence between each source vector and p_bar.
3. Use median/MAD of divergences.
4. Apply soft/hard policy.

Jensen–Shannon divergence is finite and symmetric for proper vectors.

18.5 SMALL N
------------
n = 1:
no outlier detection.

n = 2:
cannot robustly identify which source is wrong.
Report full disagreement.

n = 3:
use conservative flagging, not automatic exclusion.

18.6 LINE OUTLIER
-----------------
A price on a different total/handicap line is not an outlier.
It is a different market.

======================================================================
19. CONSENSUS FORMATION
======================================================================

19.1 PIPELINE
-------------
1. Query eligible quotes by exact market semantics.
2. Collapse duplicate provider representations to operator.
3. Form complete operator book.
4. Convert raw odds.
5. De-vig by primary method.
6. Compute sensitivity methods.
7. Apply hard validation.
8. Compute preliminary consensus.
9. Detect statistical disagreement.
10. Apply soft/hard source weights.
11. Compute final raw consensus.
12. Apply calibration model if approved.
13. generate uncertainty interval.
14. assign confidence grade.
15. publish audit record.

19.2 COMPLETE MULTINOMIAL POOL
------------------------------
For operator j proper vector p_j and normalized weight w_j:

    p_pool,i = sum_j w_j p_j,i

Because each vector sums to one and weights sum to one, the pool sums to one.

19.3 BINARY POOL
----------------
Same weighted mean of fair Yes probabilities.

19.4 LOGARITHMIC POOL CHALLENGER
--------------------------------
Candidate:

    p_log,i proportional to product_j p_j,i^(w_j)

Then normalize.

Logarithmic pooling corresponds to weighted averaging of log probabilities and
can be attractive under log loss, but may behave differently in dispersion and
zero-probability cases.

Not initial default.
Compare prospectively.

19.5 MEDIAN FALLBACK
--------------------
Weighted median in logit space is used when:

- one operator is extreme;
- n is small;
- source dependence is unclear;
- robustness policy activates.

Return:
consensus_method = ROBUST_MEDIAN_FALLBACK.

19.6 LINEAR-POOL CALIBRATION
----------------------------
Forecast-combination research shows a linear pool can require recalibration even
when components are calibrated.

Therefore identity-calibrated linear pooling is the starting point, not the
final scientific assumption.

19.7 SOURCE DEPENDENCE
----------------------
If two bookmakers/brands are not independent:

- group under dependence cluster;
- cap total cluster weight;
- record source relationship.

19.8 METHOD ENSEMBLE
--------------------
The primary consensus uses the selected primary de-vigging method.

Method uncertainty uses:
- POWER;
- PROPORTIONAL;
- SHIN where valid;
- approved challenger.

Do not average methods blindly into the central value unless prospective
evidence supports a method ensemble.

======================================================================
20. CONSENSUS CALIBRATION
======================================================================

20.1 IDENTITY INITIAL
---------------------
Before enough time-safe samples:

    p_calibrated = p_pool

with:
calibration_status = IDENTITY_INSUFFICIENT_DATA.

20.2 BINARY LOGISTIC CALIBRATION
--------------------------------
Candidate:

    p_cal =
      logistic(a + b * logit(p_pool))

Fit on prior observations only.

20.3 TEMPERATURE / POWER RECALIBRATION
--------------------------------------
For multinomial vector p:

    p_cal,i =
      p_i^gamma / sum_k p_k^gamma

This is a simple one-parameter sharpness/bias adjustment.

20.4 OUTCOME-SPECIFIC 1X2
-------------------------
If draw bias persists, a multinomial calibration may be required.

Use strong regularisation.
Do not fit a flexible model on one short season.

20.5 ISOTONIC
-------------
May be useful for large binary samples, but can overfit and produce flat/step
probabilities.

Use only with enough data and cross-validation.

20.6 TRAINING SCOPE
-------------------
Possible hierarchy:

- market definition;
- competition/global football;
- operator;
- lead-time;
- source.

Pool information where samples are weak.

20.7 PROMOTION
--------------
Calibration is promoted only if:

- walk-forward log score improves;
- calibration improves;
- no material subgroup degradation;
- parameter stable;
- code/data versioned;
- rollback exists.

======================================================================
21. MARKET DISAGREEMENT
======================================================================

21.1 PURPOSE
------------
Disagreement is information.

It can indicate:

- uncertainty;
- stale quote;
- different settlement;
- low liquidity;
- new information not yet incorporated;
- operator bias;
- mapping error.

21.2 BINARY DISAGREEMENT
------------------------
Primary raw statistic:

    sigma_logit =
      sqrt(sum_j w_j (logit(p_j)-mu_logit)^2)

where:

    mu_logit = sum_j w_j logit(p_j)

Also report:

- weighted IQR;
- probability range;
- max absolute difference.

21.3 MULTINOMIAL DISAGREEMENT
-----------------------------
Primary:

weighted mean Jensen–Shannon divergence from final consensus.

Normalize to [0,1]:

    market_disagreement =
      weighted_JSD / ln(2)

when natural logs and valid conditions apply.

21.4 METHOD DISAGREEMENT
------------------------
Separate field:

- max/SD difference between POWER, SHIN and PROPORTIONAL consensus.

21.5 EXCHANGE SPREAD
--------------------
Separate field:

    exchange_probability_spread = q_back - q_lay

21.6 NO SOURCE
--------------
market_disagreement = null
reason = NO_MARKET.

One source:
0 is misleading.
Use null and:
disagreement_status = NOT_IDENTIFIABLE_SINGLE_SOURCE.

======================================================================
22. UNCERTAINTY BOUNDS
======================================================================

22.1 MEANING
------------
`lower_bound` and `upper_bound` are an 80% operational market-evidence interval
by default.

They reflect uncertainty about the market-derived probability, not football's
aleatoric outcome variance and not a formal guarantee that the true probability
lies inside 80% of the time until prospectively validated.

22.2 UNCERTAINTY COMPONENTS
---------------------------
- operator dispersion;
- source weighting uncertainty;
- de-vig method spread;
- exchange spread;
- freshness/staleness;
- settlement uncertainty;
- calibration parameter uncertainty;
- historical residual floor;
- missing-market reconstruction.

22.3 INITIAL ENSEMBLE PROCEDURE
-------------------------------
For each accepted market:

1. Create source-method candidates:
   - primary method for every operator;
   - sensitivity methods with lower method weights;
   - exchange endpoints/midpoint where present.

2. Bootstrap independent operator clusters with replacement.

3. Sample one admissible de-vig method according to configured method
   probabilities.

4. For exchange source, sample within [q_lay,q_back] using a triangular
   distribution centred at p_mid if no better microstructure model exists.

5. Apply calibration parameter draw if estimated.

6. Form consensus replicate.

7. Repeat N times, initial N = 2,000.

8. Marginal 10th and 90th percentiles become bounds.

22.4 MINIMUM FLOOR
------------------
With sparse sources, bootstrap can collapse.

Apply historical market-type residual floor on logit scale.

Before historical evidence:
use conservative configured floor by confidence grade.

22.5 MULTINOMIAL
----------------
Every replicate is a proper vector.
Marginal bounds need not sum to one, which is acceptable if labelled as
outcome-wise intervals.

22.6 SINGLE SOURCE
------------------
Bounds must include:

- method spread;
- historical floor;
- freshness;
- settlement uncertainty.

Never return lower = upper = one quote probability.

22.7 NO CURRENT MARKET
----------------------
Bounds are null in this module.
The football-event model supplies prior/model uncertainty separately.

23. CONFIDENCE GRADING
======================================================================

23.1 DIMENSIONS
---------------
- independent operator count;
- provider redundancy;
- freshness;
- completeness;
- settlement verification;
- margin quality;
- disagreement;
- exchange spread/liquidity;
- method stability;
- calibration history;
- mapping quality.

23.2 GRADES
-----------
A — STRONG

Typical:
- >=5 independent operators, or >=3 plus a liquid/tight exchange;
- all fresh;
- complete market;
- settlement verified;
- low disagreement;
- stable method/calibration;
- no critical missingness.

B — GOOD

Typical:
- >=3 independent operators;
- fresh;
- complete;
- settlement verified;
- moderate/low disagreement;
- exchange optional.

C — LIMITED

Typical:
- 2 operators, or one good exchange/source;
- moderate age/disagreement;
- incomplete redundancy;
- no mature calibration.

D — WEAK

Typical:
- one source;
- manual one-sided prop;
- stale-soft source;
- uncertain settlement;
- incomplete market.

E — NO CURRENT MARKET

Model-only fallback.
No consensus probability from market.

23.3 FREE-STACK EXPECTATION
---------------------------
Many EPL player props will be C or D.
That is acceptable if displayed and propagated.

Do not inflate grade to make the system appear sophisticated.

23.4 MACHINE RULE AND NARRATIVE
-------------------------------
A deterministic scoring rubric may suggest a grade.
Final fields retain dimension breakdown.

A single blocking settlement/mapping issue overrides numeric score.
