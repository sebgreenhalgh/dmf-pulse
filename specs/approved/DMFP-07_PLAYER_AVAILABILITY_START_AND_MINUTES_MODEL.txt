PURPOSE
-------
This document defines the most important player-level predictive subsystem in
DMF Pulse:

    WHO IS AVAILABLE?
    WHO STARTS?
    WHO IS ON THE BENCH?
    WHO APPEARS?
    WHEN DO THEY ENTER OR LEAVE?
    HOW MANY OFFICIAL FPL MINUTES DO THEY RECORD?

It estimates:

- probability of eligibility;
- probability of being medically available;
- probability of making the matchday squad;
- probability of starting;
- probability of being named as a substitute;
- probability of not being in the squad;
- expected minutes conditional on starting;
- the complete starter-minutes distribution;
- probability of appearing from the bench;
- substitute entry-time and minutes distributions;
- probability of 60+ minutes;
- probability of zero minutes;
- tactical rotation risk;
- injury and return-to-play uncertainty;
- suspension and ineligibility status;
- European and domestic-cup workload effects;
- fixture-congestion effects;
- manager-change uncertainty;
- preseason and post-tournament readiness;
- coherent team lineup and substitution scenarios.

The output MUST be a probability distribution.

The system MUST NOT reduce the problem to:

    expected_minutes = 74

A single mean hides the distinction between materially different situations:

- 80% chance of 90 and 20% chance of zero;
- 100% chance of 72–76 minutes;
- 55% chance of starting, 40% chance of a 25-minute substitute appearance and
  5% chance of no involvement.

Those distributions can have similar means and radically different FPL value,
captaincy risk and transfer utility.

GOVERNING PRINCIPLE
-------------------
Playing time is a structured sequence of dependent decisions and events:

1. Is the player legally eligible?
2. What is the player's latent medical/readiness state?
3. Does the manager include the player in the matchday squad?
4. Does the player start, sit on the bench or miss out?
5. If starting, when and why does the player's participation end?
6. If benched, whether and when does the player enter?
7. How do the score, cards, injuries and remaining substitutions alter that
   process?

Pulse will model that sequence explicitly.

IMPORTANT 2026/27 CONTEXT
-------------------------
The official Premier League preseason analysis on 16 July 2026 said the season
would begin with a record eight new managers and highlighted that tactics,
starting lineups and set-piece roles could change. It also noted that players
involved on the final weekend of the 2026 World Cup would have less than five
weeks before the Premier League season begins and advised monitoring their
preseason minutes closely.

A separate official analysis identified nine Premier League clubs involved in
European competitions and warned that manager changes and European schedules
would create unusually high early-season uncertainty.

Therefore:

- old-manager lineup history cannot be carried forward at full weight;
- preseason availability cannot be treated as ordinary competitive evidence;
- World Cup return dates and training exposure matter;
- European fixture context is a first-class feature;
- uncertainty must be wider early in the season;
- "expected starter" is not enough for high-value FPL decisions.

======================================================================
0. EXECUTIVE MODEL DECISION
======================================================================

0.1 SELECTED TARGET ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------
Pulse will use a hierarchical Bayesian, joint state-and-time model with five
layers:

LAYER A — HARD ELIGIBILITY
Deterministic or near-deterministic fixture eligibility:

- suspension;
- registration;
- loan-parent restriction;
- competition ineligibility;
- confirmed absence.

LAYER B — LATENT READINESS
A dynamic latent medical/readiness state inferred from time-stamped evidence.

LAYER C — JOINT MATCHDAY ROLE
A coherent team model for:

- START;
- BENCH;
- OUT_OF_SQUAD.

LAYER D — CONDITIONAL PLAYING-TIME PROCESS
Discrete-time competing-risk hazards for starter exits and substitute entries.

LAYER E — COHERENT TEAM SCENARIOS
Formation, XI, bench and substitution paths that obey team-level constraints.

0.2 INITIAL PRODUCTION MODEL
----------------------------
The first production-capable implementation will be:

1. a hierarchical Bayesian multinomial role model;
2. a constrained team-level sampler that produces exactly the configured number
   of starters and substitutes;
3. a discrete-time competing-risk starter-exit model;
4. a discrete-time bench-entry model;
5. posterior-predictive minute distributions;
6. calibrated persistence and gradient-boosted challengers;
7. explicit human/source evidence and uncertainty.

0.3 LATER JOINT MODEL
---------------------
The target advanced model will add:

- latent formation and role slots;
- team substitution-event hazards;
- explicit outgoing/incoming player choice;
- substitution-window and maximum-substitute constraints;
- score-state-dependent hazards;
- coupling with DMFP-08 match simulation;
- hidden semi-Markov injury/readiness transitions.

0.4 WHY HIERARCHICAL BAYESIAN
-----------------------------
The dataset is strongly grouped and uneven:

- players;
- positions/roles;
- teams;
- managers;
- competitions;
- seasons;
- injury types;
- newly promoted clubs;
- new signings;
- new managers.

Hierarchical partial pooling allows:

- established players to retain individual history;
- sparse/new players to shrink toward role/team/league priors;
- new managers to borrow carefully from their prior clubs and the league;
- uncertainty to expand automatically when data are sparse;
- posterior distributions rather than unsupported point estimates.

0.5 WHY DISCRETE-TIME SURVIVAL
------------------------------
Official FPL minutes are discrete and bounded.

Discrete-time hazards:

- handle strong nonlinear minute effects;
- model halftime and substitution peaks;
- include time-varying score state;
- support competing exit causes;
- produce a complete probability mass function;
- are easier to calibrate directly than a single minutes regression.

0.6 WHY NOT DEEP LEARNING FIRST
-------------------------------
DeepHit and related models can learn flexible competing-risk distributions, but
the initial public dataset is too small, heterogeneous and missing many
private-load variables.

Deep survival models remain challengers after:

- enough prospective data exist;
- strong simple baselines are established;
- calibration is verified;
- time-safe validation demonstrates value.

0.7 CORE OUTPUT
---------------
For every player/fixture/as-of time:

- role-state probabilities;
- 0–90 official-minute probability mass;
- conditional distributions;
- coherent scenario samples;
- uncertainty decomposition;
- source and model lineage;
- confidence grade.

======================================================================
1. FORMAL PROBLEM
======================================================================

1.1 RANDOM VARIABLES
--------------------
For player i and fixture f:

E_i:
Eligibility state.

H_i:
Latent health/readiness state.

R_i:
Matchday role:
- START;
- BENCH;
- OUT.

M_i:
Official FPL minutes, integer 0–90 for a normal league fixture under target
season semantics.

C_i:
Participation-ending/entry cause.

F:
Team formation.

L:
Complete starting lineup.

B:
Complete bench.

Z(t):
Time-varying match state:
- score difference;
- red cards;
- remaining substitutions;
- competition state;
- time.

1.2 FACTORISATION
-----------------
Conceptually:

P(E, H, F, L, B, substitution path, M | I)
=
P(E | I)
x P(H | E, I)
x P(F | team, manager, fixture, I)
x P(L, B | E, H, F, manager, squad, I)
x P(substitution path, M | L, B, H, Z(t), manager, fixture, I)

I is the information legally and operationally available before the FPL cutoff.

1.3 MARGINAL MINUTES MIXTURE
----------------------------
For a player:

P(M=m)
=
P(R=START) P(M=m | START)
+
P(R=BENCH) P(M=m | BENCH)
+
P(R=OUT) 1[m=0]

subject to eligibility and team-level coherence.

1.4 ZERO MINUTES
----------------
Default:

P(M=0)
=
P(R=OUT)
+
P(R=BENCH) P(no entry | BENCH)
+
P(pre-kickoff withdrawal after submitted prediction, where explicitly modelled)

An actual starter label implies positive minutes unless the official fixture
semantics create a rare exceptional case.

1.5 60+ MINUTES
----------------
P(M >= 60)
=
P(START) P(M >= 60 | START)
+
P(BENCH) P(M >= 60 | BENCH)

The second term is usually small but not forced to zero because early forced
substitutions can produce 60+ minutes for a substitute.

1.6 EXPECTED MINUTES
--------------------
Unconditional:

E[M] = sum_m m P(M=m)

Conditional start:

E[M | START] = sum_m m P(M=m | START)

Conditional bench:

E[M | BENCH] = sum_m m P(M=m | BENCH)

1.7 BENCH APPEARANCE
--------------------
Conditional:

P(appear | BENCH)
=
1 - P(M=0 | BENCH)

Unconditional:

P(bench appearance)
=
P(BENCH) P(appear | BENCH)

Both outputs are required.

4. TIME-SAFE INPUT CONTRACT
======================================================================

4.1 DEADLINE ELIGIBILITY
------------------------
Every input MUST come from the DMFP-05 LIVE_OPERATIONAL bundle with:

    usable_at <= official FPL deadline

unless the use case is explicitly a later kickoff-lineup model rather than an
FPL decision model.

4.2 NO OFFICIAL LINEUP LEAKAGE
------------------------------
Official Premier League lineups are generally announced after the FPL deadline
for the first fixture and far after the deadline for later fixtures.

They are:

- labels;
- evaluation inputs;
- future-match history.

They are not current-Gameweek FPL features when published after the cutoff.

4.3 LATE PRESS/INJURY NEWS
--------------------------
A news report published after the cutoff cannot enter the decision even if it
describes an injury occurring earlier.

4.4 RETROSPECTIVE INJURY RETURN
-------------------------------
Final injury duration and eventual return date are labels.
They cannot be historical features.

4.5 AS-OF DEPTH CHART
---------------------
Depth charts, recent-start shares and manager patterns must be calculated from
matches whose lineups were known before the cutoff.

- manager statements;
- registration;
- preseason return;

5.4 PREDICTED LINEUP SOURCES
----------------------------
Every predicted lineup source is treated as an expert forecast.

It receives a historical source-performance model.

Do not convert:
"listed in predicted XI"
into:
p_start = 1.

5.5 BETTING MARKET
------------------
Player market listing, price or removal can contain participation information.

However:

- some markets are conditional on starting;
- some books omit low-interest players;
- markets open at different times;
- free coverage is sparse;
- market absence is confounded.

Use only after source/market-specific calibration.

5.6 PRESEASON
-------------
Preseason lineups/minutes are informative but downweighted because:

- managers build fitness;
- substitutions are atypical;
- opponents and rules vary;
- World Cup returners may be absent;
- fringe players may temporarily start;
- new signings are integrated gradually.

5.7 MANUAL EVIDENCE
-------------------
Structured analyst observations may be entered with:

- source;
- quote;
- time;
- evidence type;
- confidence;
- expiry;
- reviewer.

======================================================================
6. HARD ELIGIBILITY LAYER
======================================================================

6.1 PURPOSE
-----------
Separate legal/competition eligibility from medical/tactical selection.

6.2 HARD ZERO
-------------
Set p_start = p_bench = p_appearance = 0 and p_zero = 1 when a fresh,
authoritative source confirms:

- active suspension covering the fixture;
- player not registered/eligible;
- loan-parent restriction;
- fixture cancelled;
- other formal ineligibility.

6.3 APPEAL/UNCERTAINTY
----------------------
If suspension appeal or fixture applicability is unresolved:

- scenario states;
- do not hard zero;
- P(eligible) reflects authoritative evidence.

6.4 MEDICAL "RULED OUT"
-----------------------
An official medical statement can justify a very low probability.

It SHOULD not automatically be treated as a mathematical zero unless the
statement and timing make participation impossible.

6.5 SUSPENSION DATA
-------------------
Store:

- authority;
- competition;
- matches covered;
- start/end;
- appeal;
- source time;
- mapping.

6.6 INELIGIBILITY OVERRIDES
---------------------------
Hard eligibility overrides all market and lineup-source signals.

======================================================================
7. LATENT READINESS MODEL
======================================================================

7.1 TARGET MODEL
----------------
A Bayesian hidden semi-Markov process over readiness states.

"Semi-Markov" is important because transition probability depends on time spent
in the state.

7.2 STATE TRANSITIONS
---------------------
Typical:

OUT_MEDICAL
  -> REHABILITATION
  -> PARTIAL_TRAINING
  -> FULL_TRAINING_NOT_MATCH_READY
  -> MATCH_READY_LIMITED
  -> MATCH_READY_FULL

Possible regressions:

- PARTIAL_TRAINING -> OUT_MEDICAL;
- MATCH_READY_LIMITED -> OUT_MEDICAL;
- MATCH_READY_FULL -> OUT_MEDICAL.

7.3 OBSERVATION MODEL
---------------------
Evidence updates latent state through source/type-specific likelihoods.

Examples:

- official "full team training";
- official "will be assessed";
- FPL yellow/red flag;
- structured injury record;
- preseason bench appearance;
- competitive substitute appearance;
- predicted lineup;
- market listing;
- press quote.

7.4 SOURCE RELIABILITY
----------------------
Learn source reliability from its historical ability to predict:

- squad inclusion;
- start;
- appearance;
- return timing.

Do not claim the source diagnosed the true medical state.

7.5 INITIAL MVP
---------------
Before enough data for a full hidden semi-Markov model:

- construct explicit readiness features;
- use a hierarchical Bayesian availability classifier;
- maintain state labels and time-since-evidence;
- preserve the later upgrade path.

7.6 EVIDENCE DECAY
------------------
Evidence value decays according to:

- source type;
- newer contradictory evidence;
- days to fixture;
- training progression;
- injury type;
- manager update.

A fixed one-size half-life is prohibited.

7.7 UNKNOWN INJURY
------------------
Unknown diagnosis increases uncertainty.

Do not assign a precise recovery curve based only on a generic "knock" label.

======================================================================
8. MATCHDAY ROLE MODEL
======================================================================

8.1 TARGET
----------
Jointly predict:

P(START)
P(BENCH)
P(OUT)

8.2 WHY MULTINOMIAL
-------------------
Separate independent start and bench classifiers can create incoherent outputs.

The three states are mutually exclusive.

8.3 HIERARCHICAL LINEAR PREDICTOR
---------------------------------
For player i, role r, team-manager regime g and fixture f:

eta_i,r =
  global intercept_r
  + player effect_i,r
  + position/role effect
  + team effect
  + manager effect
  + manager-team regime effect
  + fixture/context effects
  + health/readiness effects
  + workload effects
  + depth-chart/teammate effects
  + source-evidence effects
  + interactions.

Probabilities use a softmax, subject to team-level reconciliation.

8.4 RANDOM EFFECTS
------------------
Cross-classified/hierarchical:

- player;
- FPL position;
- tactical role;
- team;
- manager;
- manager-team spell;
- competition;
- season;
- injury class;
- age/experience band where justified.

8.5 PLAYER PERSISTENCE
----------------------
Recent actual lineup role is a powerful feature but must not become a deterministic
rule.

Use exponentially weighted or state-space persistence:

- start after start;
- start after bench;
- return after absence;
- consecutive starts;
- role continuity.

8.6 MATCHDAY SQUAD
------------------
p_matchday_squad = p_start + p_bench.

The bench-size configuration belongs to competition rules/context.

8.7 COLD START
--------------
New player:

- position/role prior;
- transfer fee/market-value-type data only if lawful and shown useful;
- prior club minutes;
- competition-strength uncertainty;
- preseason evidence;
- new-team depth chart.

Unmapped new signing remains blocked.

======================================================================
9. COHERENT TEAM LINEUP MODEL
======================================================================

9.1 WHY COHERENCE MATTERS
-------------------------
Twenty independent player start probabilities can imply:

- 13 expected starters;
- no goalkeeper;
- four centre-forwards;
- mutually exclusive alternatives both starting with high probability.

Downstream event simulation requires valid joint scenarios.

9.2 TARGET FORMATION MODEL
--------------------------
Predict formation F:

P(F | team, manager, opponent, home/away, competition, congestion, available
players)

Formation is tactical-role structure, not FPL position.

9.3 ROLE SLOTS
--------------
Example latent slots:

- GK;
- left/right centre-back;
- full-back/wing-back;
- holding midfielder;
- central midfielder;
- attacking midfielder;
- winger;
- striker.

Role ontology is versioned.

9.4 PLAYER-ROLE UTILITY
-----------------------
For player i and role slot r:

u_i,r =
  historical role fit
  + manager preference
  + health
  + workload
  + opposition/tactical context
  + teammate alternatives
  + recent lineup evidence
  + uncertainty.

9.5 ASSIGNMENT
--------------
Sample without replacement.

Candidate implementation:

- Gumbel perturbations of player-role utilities;
- constrained maximum-weight assignment;
- one player per role;
- one role per player;
- exactly 11 starters;
- configured goalkeeper/formation constraints.

This is related to a Plackett–Luce/random-utility selection process.

9.6 BENCH
---------
Sample remaining matchday-squad slots from nonstarters using bench utilities and
competition constraints.

9.7 MARGINALS
-------------
Generate many coherent lineup samples.

Estimate:

p_start_i = fraction samples player starts
p_bench_i = fraction samples player benched
p_out_i = fraction samples player out

9.8 INITIAL RECONCILIATION
--------------------------
Before the full role model:

- fit marginal multinomial probabilities;
- sample a fixed-size XI and bench using weighted sampling without replacement;
- enforce one goalkeeper and broad position plausibility;
- calibrate marginals through iterative adjustment.

Label:
COHERENCE_MODEL_V1.

9.9 TEAM SUM CHECK
------------------
Across coherent samples:

sum_i 1[start_i] = 11 exactly.

Marginal:

sum_i p_start_i = 11 within Monte Carlo error.

Similarly for bench size.

======================================================================
10. STARTER MINUTES: COMPETING-RISK SURVIVAL
======================================================================

10.1 EVENT PROCESS
------------------
Conditional on starting, participation can end through:

- tactical substitution;
- injury/illness;
- dismissal;
- other forced exit;
- match end.

These are competing terminal causes for the player's on-pitch spell.

10.2 DISCRETE HAZARD
--------------------
For minute bin t and cause k:

h_i,k(t)
=
P(T_i=t, C_i=k | T_i >= t, START, X_i(t))

Require:

sum_k h_i,k(t) <= 1.

10.3 SURVIVAL
-------------
S_i(t)
=
product over u <= t of
[1 - sum_k h_i,k(u)]

10.4 EVENT MASS
---------------
P(T_i=t, C_i=k | START)
=
S_i(t-1) h_i,k(t)

10.5 MATCH-END MASS
-------------------
Probability of completing official full time is the residual survival mass at
the configured endpoint.

10.6 BASELINE HAZARD
--------------------
Use a flexible time function:

- piecewise minute bins;
- B-splines;
- halftime indicator;
- time-varying spline by cause.

Do not assume proportional hazards across 90 minutes.

10.7 HALFTIME
-------------
Halftime substitutions create a discontinuity.

Represent explicitly rather than forcing a smooth curve.

10.8 TACTICAL HAZARD FEATURES
-----------------------------
- minute;
- score state;
- pre-match team strength;
- player's role;
- position;
- yellow card;
- performance proxy where simulated/available;
- manager;
- remaining substitutions;
- formation;
- team chasing/protecting;
- upcoming fixture;
- workload;
- player importance;
- replacement quality.

The 2008 Spanish-league substitution study found current score to be a leading
factor in first-substitution timing, supporting time-varying score-state inputs.

10.9 INJURY EXIT HAZARD
-----------------------
- readiness state;
- recent return;
- previous injury;
- workload;
- congestion;
- age/position;
- injury type;
- environmental/context where available.

This is a low-frequency event and requires strong pooling.

10.10 DISMISSAL
---------------
Can be supplied by DMFP-08's card-event model in joint simulation.

For standalone minutes baseline:
use a small position/player/team-aware hazard.

Avoid double counting when coupled.

10.11 ABANDONED MATCH
---------------------
Exclude from normal training or model as a separate administrative endpoint.

======================================================================
11. BENCH ENTRY AND SUBSTITUTE MINUTES
======================================================================

11.1 ENTRY HAZARD
-----------------
Conditional on BENCH:

e_i,k(t)
=
P(entry at minute t for cause k |
  not entered before t,
  BENCH,
  team state X(t))

11.2 NO ENTRY
-------------
Residual survival to match end is P(no entry | BENCH).

11.3 FEATURES
-------------
- player tactical role;
- manager substitution habits;
- replacement hierarchy;
- score state;
- team strength/opponent;
- injuries/cards to starters;
- available substitutes;
- remaining substitution windows;
- competition;
- fixture congestion;
- player readiness/return;
- preseason ramp;
- whether player is attacking/defensive specialist.

11.4 ENTRY CAUSE
----------------
Tactical and forced entries can have different timing/distributions.

11.5 SUBSTITUTE MINUTES
-----------------------
Convert simulated entry time into official FPL minutes according to target
scoring/event conventions.

The training target is the official recorded minute total, not a naive
wall-clock subtraction when they differ.

11.6 EARLY SUBSTITUTE
---------------------
The distribution must permit 60+ minutes from an early forced substitution.

11.7 UNUSED BENCH
-----------------
P(M=0 | BENCH) remains explicit and calibrated.

12.5 INCOMING CHOICE

Conditional choice among bench uses:

13. MATCH-STATE COUPLING

13.1 PROBLEM

13.2 BASELINE PASS
------------------
DMFP-07 first produces a pre-match minutes distribution using:

- expected score-state paths;

13.3 JOINT PASS
---------------
DMFP-08 may simulate:

- goals/cards;

17. RETURNING FROM INJURY
======================================================================

17.1 RETURN TO PLAY IS NOT FULL PERFORMANCE
-------------------------------------------
Research distinguishes medical clearance/full availability from complete return
to prior performance.

Pulse must distinguish:

- training return;
- squad return;
- bench return;
- start return;
- 60-minute return;
- full-minute stability;
- performance return.

17.2 NO GENERIC RECOVERY CLOCK
------------------------------
The UEFA Elite Club Injury Study shows return times differ materially by injury
diagnosis and severity.

Do not use:

    hamstring injury = exactly 21 days

without direct evidence.

17.3 RECURRENCE
---------------
Studies of professional football report meaningful recurrent injury risk,
including higher recurrence after early return for some injuries and substantial
variation by injury location.

Use recurrence as uncertainty/risk evidence, not as a diagnosis.

17.4 MINUTES RAMP
-----------------
Returning players may follow:

- unused bench;
- late substitute;
- earlier substitute;
- limited start;
- repeated limited start;
- full start.

Model the progression sequence and time spent in each state.

17.5 FEATURES
-------------
- injury class;
- severity;
- prior recurrence;
- date of full training;
- date of squad return;
- appearances/minutes since return;
- days between appearances;
- manager comments;
- market listing;
- teammate alternatives;
- upcoming schedule.

17.6 LIMITED START
------------------
A start is not equivalent to full-minute availability.

`MATCH_READY_LIMITED` increases tactical/injury exit hazard.

17.7 PUBLIC INFORMATION LIMIT
-----------------------------
No medical inference beyond public evidence.
Uncertainty remains wide when diagnosis is vague.

- manager regime handled online/hierarchically;
- calibration drift;
- rule/competition change;

35.4 MODEL FAILURE
------------------

1. previous healthy model;
2. hierarchical baseline;
3. recent lineup persistence with injury rules;
4. human-reviewed distribution.

Never fallback to one expected-minute point.

38. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

A. DISTRIBUTION
[ ] Full 0–90 minute PMF is required.
[ ] Conditional start and bench distributions are required.
[ ] p0, p60 and expectations derive from the PMF.
[ ] Role probabilities sum one.

[ ] Eligibility, readiness, role and minutes are separate.
[ ] Starts/bench/out are modeled jointly.
[ ] Team lineup scenarios are coherent.
[ ] Starter exit and bench entry use competing-risk/time models.
[ ] Team substitution limits are represented.

C. UNCERTAINTY
[ ] Hierarchical pooling is accepted.
[ ] New-manager uncertainty is explicit.
[ ] Injury and rotation are separate.
[ ] Missing player markets increase uncertainty.
[ ] Preseason evidence is downweighted.

D. CONTEXT
[ ] European and congestion effects are learned, not fixed.
[ ] World Cup return is first-class for GW1.
[ ] Returning from injury is a state progression.
[ ] Suspension/ineligibility can hard-zero where authoritative.

E. SCIENTIFIC VALIDITY
[ ] Walk-forward validation is mandatory.
[ ] Proper scores and calibration are primary.
[ ] C-index alone is insufficient.
[ ] Postdeadline lineups/news are prohibited.
[ ] Baselines and ablations are required.

F. OPERATIONS
[ ] Posterior prediction must be deadline-fast.
[ ] Manual evidence is structured and expires.
[ ] Fallback remains probabilistic.
[ ] Every output has lineage and confidence.
