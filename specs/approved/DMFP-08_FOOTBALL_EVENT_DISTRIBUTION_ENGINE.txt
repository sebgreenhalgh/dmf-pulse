PURPOSE
-------
This document defines the engine that converts market information, team/player
history and coherent lineup/minute scenarios into complete football match-event
distributions.

It models:

- home and away goals;
- exact scorelines;
- team-goal totals;
- player goals;
- multiple-goal probabilities;
- official/provider assists;
- assistable and unassisted goal mechanisms;
- clean sheets;
- goals conceded;
- team and player shots;
- shots on target;
- goalkeeper saves;
- penalty awards;
- penalty goals;
- missed penalties;
- penalty saves;
- yellow and red cards;
- own goals;
- clearances;
- blocks;
- interceptions;
- tackles;
- ball recoveries;
- event timing;
- correlations between teammates;
- correlations between opponents;
- correlations between events in the same match.

The critical requirement is INTERNAL CONSISTENCY.

Examples:

- player goals plus opponent own goals MUST equal team goals in every simulated
  scenario;
- player goal expectations cannot imply materially more goals than the team's
  calibrated goal distribution;
- a clean sheet is a deterministic consequence of conceding zero goals;
- saves cannot exceed shots on target faced;
- a penalty save must correspond to an unscored penalty and a goalkeeper on the
  pitch;
- a scorer cannot assist the same goal;
- an own goal cannot simultaneously be credited as an opponent player goal;
- a player cannot record events while off the pitch;
- a player sent off cannot later score, assist or record defensive actions;
- the team lineup, substitutions and player minutes must remain coherent;
- every FPL-facing event must be traceable to one simulated match path.

GOVERNING PRINCIPLE
-------------------
Pulse will not fit separate independent models for every player statistic and
hope they add up.

Instead, it will simulate one joint match world:

    MARKET-CALIBRATED SCORE ENVIRONMENT
    + COHERENT LINEUPS AND MINUTES
    + SHARED MATCH-STATE FACTORS
    + CONSTRAINED EVENT ALLOCATION
    = INTERNALLY CONSISTENT FOOTBALL SCENARIOS

IMPORTANT MODELLING POSITION
----------------------------
Poisson models remain powerful football baselines, but they are not treated as
universal physical laws.

The production engine uses:

1. a flexible prior score model;
2. a market-constrained joint score matrix;
3. conditional player/event allocation;
4. posterior predictive scenario simulation;
5. challenger count distributions where data show residual under/overdispersion,
   excess zeros, tail error or dependence.

0. EXECUTIVE MODEL DECISION
======================================================================

0.1 SELECTED PRODUCTION ARCHITECTURE
------------------------------------
The initial production engine is a two-stage coherent model.

STAGE 1 — MARKET-CONSTRAINED SCORE DISTRIBUTION

- construct a prior joint score matrix from a dynamic bivariate football model;
- ingest market probabilities from DMFP-06;
- reconcile 1X2, totals, team totals, clean sheets, BTTS, correct-score and other
  compatible markets;
- solve a constrained information-projection problem;
- output a proper joint home/away score distribution.

STAGE 2 — CONDITIONAL EVENT ALLOCATION

Conditional on:

- sampled scoreline;
- coherent lineup/minute scenario;
- match-state latent factors;

allocate:

- goal times;
- player scorers;
- own goals;
- goal mechanisms;
- assists;
- shots and shots on target;
- saves;
- penalties;
- cards;
- defensive events.

0.2 SCORE-DISTRIBUTION PRIMARY REPRESENTATION
---------------------------------------------
The canonical pre-match score forecast is a finite joint probability matrix:

    P(G_home = h, G_away = a)

with adaptive score support and explicit tail treatment.

This matrix is more flexible than exposing only:

    lambda_home
    lambda_away

It can represent:

- low-score dependence;
- draw corrections;
- asymmetric tails;
- market constraints;
- overdispersion;
- score correlation;
- clean sheets;
- exact score;
- all totals and outcome probabilities.

0.3 SCORE PRIOR
---------------
Initial prior:

- dynamic bivariate Poisson team-strength model;
- low-score Dixon–Coles correction;
- hierarchical Bayesian attack, defence and home effects;
- time decay / state evolution;
- lineup/minutes context where available;
- promoted/new-manager uncertainty.

Challengers:

- independent Poisson;
- bivariate Poisson without low-score correction;
- gamma–Poisson / negative-binomial mixture;
- diagonal-inflated model;
- double Poisson / Conway–Maxwell–Poisson for underdispersion;
- bivariate Weibull count;
- sequential goal-hazard process.

0.4 MARKET RECONCILIATION
-------------------------
Primary method:

    soft constrained KL projection

Choose score matrix P that remains close to prior P0 while matching all reliable
market probabilities within their uncertainty.

Conceptually:

    minimise:
        KL(P || P0)
        + 1/2 sum_k [(A_k P - m_k)^2 / sigma_k^2]
        + optional tail/smoothness penalties

subject to:

    P_h,a >= 0
    sum_h,a P_h,a = 1

where:

- A_k maps scorelines to market event k;
- m_k is the DMFP-06 consensus probability;
- sigma_k reflects market uncertainty/confidence.

Hard equality constraints are used only for deliberately trusted synthetic/unit
tests or unusually strong, mathematically consistent market inputs.

0.5 PLAYER GOAL ALLOCATION
---------------------------
Each sampled team goal is assigned to exactly one category:

- eligible team player;
- opponent own goal;
- unresolved/other scorer only where source/model scope requires.

Player scorer shares are constrained to sum one for each team goal and depend
on:

- whether player is on the pitch at goal time;
- role;
- penalty/set-piece role;
- player scoring ability;
- team goal mechanism;
- lineup;
- opposition;
- match state.

Player market probabilities calibrate these shares without violating team totals.

0.6 PLAYER GOAL MARKET CALIBRATION
----------------------------------
For fixed player goal share s_i and team goal count G:

    P(player i scores at least once)
    =
    1 - E[(1 - s_i)^G]

This uses the complete team goal distribution.

If G is Poisson(lambda_team):

    P(G_i >= 1)
    =
    1 - exp(-lambda_team s_i)

and:

    lambda_i = -log(1-p_i)

only when the player's event count can validly be represented as a fixed-exposure
Poisson count.

The engine MUST NOT apply:

    lambda_i = -log(1-p_i)

blindly when:

- starting/minutes are uncertain;
- the market is conditional on starting;
- player share changes by lineup/time;
- team goals are non-Poisson;
- multiple goal mechanisms exist.

0.7 ASSIST ALLOCATION
---------------------
Each non-own-goal team goal receives a mechanism:

- assisted open play;
- unassisted open play;
- set piece;
- penalty;
- direct free kick;
- rebound/other provider-defined mechanism.

If assistable, exactly one eligible assister or a no-assist state is selected.
The scorer cannot assist the same goal.

0.8 SHOTS AND SAVES
--------------------
The engine models a coherent shot tree:

    shots
    -> on target / off target / blocked
    -> goal / goalkeeper save / last-line block / other result

Goalkeeper saves are derived from on-target shots faced and goal outcomes,
subject to provider/FPL definitions.

0.9 CORRELATIONS
----------------
Correlations arise through shared latent and deterministic structure:

- scoreline;
- match tempo;
- team dominance;
- possession/territory;
- finishing;
- discipline;
- set pieces;
- lineup/minutes;
- substitutions;
- red cards;
- score state;
- event conservation constraints.

No arbitrary independent player-event sampling is allowed in production output.

0.10 INITIAL AND ADVANCED SIMULATORS
------------------------------------
V1:
Final-score-first coherent conditional simulator.

V2:
Sequential marked point-process simulator calibrated/reweighted to the market
score matrix.

V1 is selected for initial production because it is easier to validate and
guarantees market-consistent score totals.

======================================================================
4. TEAM SCORE PRIOR
======================================================================

4.1 INDEPENDENT POISSON BASELINE
--------------------------------
Baseline:

    H ~ Poisson(lambda_H)
    A ~ Poisson(lambda_A)

independent conditional on rates.

Advantages:

- simple;
- interpretable;
- analytically tractable;
- often a strong football baseline;
- exact score matrix easy to compute;
- Poisson thinning supports player allocation.

Limitations:

- conditional mean equals variance;
- no score dependence;
- draw/low-score error;
- no dynamic match-state feedback;
- tails can be wrong.

4.2 TEAM-STRENGTH PARAMETERISATION
----------------------------------
Historical prior:

    log(lambda_H)
      = home_advantage
        + attack_home
        - defence_away
        + lineup_context
        + regime_context

    log(lambda_A)
      = attack_away
        - defence_home
        + lineup_context
        + regime_context

Attack/defence effects use hierarchical constraints for identifiability.

4.3 DYNAMIC STRENGTH
--------------------
Team strengths evolve over time through:

- random walk/state space;
- discounted observations;
- manager/lineup regime changes;
- promoted-team priors;
- season transitions.

Koopman and Lit's dynamic bivariate Poisson work supports stochastic evolution
of team attack/defence strengths.

4.4 BIVARIATE POISSON
---------------------
One representation:

    H = X_H + X_C
    A = X_A + X_C

where independent:

    X_H ~ Poisson(lambda_H)
    X_A ~ Poisson(lambda_A)
    X_C ~ Poisson(lambda_C)

Then:

    Cov(H,A) = lambda_C >= 0

Advantages:
- simple positive dependence;
- shared match factor.

Limitations:
- only nonnegative covariance;
- shared component means simultaneous/common-count structure rather than a full
  causal match process;
- may not capture score-state feedback or negative dependence.

4.5 DIXON–COLES CORRECTION
--------------------------
Dixon and Coles adjust low scoreline probabilities, particularly:

- 0-0;
- 0-1;
- 1-0;
- 1-1.

Use as prior correction, not as a substitute for market calibration.

The correction parameter is:

- time-varying or hierarchically estimated where evidence permits;
- constrained to valid probabilities;
- tested prospectively.

4.6 DIAGONAL INFLATION
----------------------
Karlis and Ntzoufras describe bivariate Poisson extensions including diagonal
inflation to improve draw modelling.

Challenger when:

- draw scorelines remain miscalibrated after Dixon–Coles/market constraints;
- complete-score data justify extra complexity.

4.7 HIERARCHICAL BAYESIAN PRIOR
-------------------------------
Baio and Blangiardo show a hierarchical Bayesian structure for attack/defence
strengths and discuss over-shrinkage of extreme teams.

Pulse must:

- perform prior predictive checks;
- inspect promoted/elite teams;
- consider mixture/heavy-tailed team effects if normal priors over-shrink;
- preserve uncertainty.

4.8 MARKET-AUGMENTED PRIOR
-------------------------
Egidi, Pauli and Torelli combine historical scoring parameters with bookmaker
odds in a hierarchical Poisson model and report improved predictive use.

Pulse goes further by treating the entire score matrix as the market-calibrated
object.

======================================================================
5. WHEN POISSON IS ADEQUATE
======================================================================

5.1 CONDITIONAL, NOT MARGINAL, QUESTION
---------------------------------------
Across a whole league, goal counts mix teams and strengths and can be
overdispersed even when goals are conditionally Poisson.

Test Poisson adequacy after conditioning on:

- team;
- opponent;
- home;
- lineup;
- market strength;
- regime;
- fixture context.

5.2 ADEQUACY DIAGNOSTICS
------------------------
- mean/variance of randomized quantile residuals;
- rootogram;
- observed versus predicted zeros;
- tail probabilities;
- exact-score log score;
- probability integral transform/rank diagnostics;
- 1X2/RPS;
- clean-sheet calibration;
- total-goal calibration;
- draw score calibration.

5.3 ACCEPT POISSON WHEN
-----------------------
- residual dispersion is close to one;
- zero and tail calibration acceptable;
- no material subgroup failure;
- market constraints are reproduced;
- challenger fails to improve out-of-time proper scores.

5.4 DO NOT REJECT SOLELY BECAUSE
--------------------------------
- marginal league variance exceeds mean;
- football is "complex";
- a more flexible model fits training data better.

======================================================================
6. NEGATIVE BINOMIAL AND OVERDISPERSION
======================================================================

6.1 GAMMA–POISSON MIXTURE
-------------------------
If:

    Lambda ~ Gamma(shape, rate)
    G | Lambda ~ Poisson(Lambda)

then marginal G is negative binomial.

Interpretation:
uncertain/random intensity creates overdispersion.

6.2 PREFERRED USE
-----------------
Rather than inserting an arbitrary negative-binomial count immediately, Pulse
can model uncertainty in latent scoring intensity.

This naturally reflects:

- lineup uncertainty;
- tactical uncertainty;
- market bounds;
- finishing environment;
- regime uncertainty.

6.3 WHEN TO USE
---------------
Promote an NB/gamma–Poisson component when:

- residual variance materially exceeds Poisson after conditioning;
- high-score tail is systematically underestimated;
- out-of-time log score improves;
- score-market fit improves;
- calibration remains stable.

6.4 TEAM-SPECIFIC DISPERSION
----------------------------
Do not estimate unrestricted dispersion for every team on one season.

Use hierarchical:

- global;
- league;
- team/regime deviations.

6.5 CORRELATION
---------------
Independent negative-binomial margins do not by themselves create a coherent
joint distribution.

Use:

- shared latent gamma factors;
- copula;
- score-matrix prior;
- joint event process.

6.6 CAUTION
-----------
Historical research is mixed: some datasets are adequately Poisson after
conditioning, and underdispersion alternatives can outperform overdispersion
models in some football samples.

Model selection is empirical.

======================================================================
7. ZERO INFLATION AND HURDLE MODELS
======================================================================

7.1 DEFINITION
--------------
Zero-inflated model:

    with probability pi:
      structural zero
    otherwise:
      count model.

Hurdle model:

- one model for zero versus positive;
- separate zero-truncated distribution for positive counts.

7.2 TEAM GOALS
--------------
A 0-goal outcome is ordinary in football.

Do not introduce zero inflation merely because zeros are common.

7.3 WHEN PLAUSIBLE
------------------
Use only if:

- market-calibrated Poisson/NB still underestimates zero;
- a plausible latent state exists;
- out-of-time scoring improves.

Potential structural states:

- severe attacking/lineup impairment;
- unusual tactical shutdown;
- red-card match state;
- player not on pitch.

For team pre-match goals, these are usually better represented by lineup/state
mixtures than an opaque zero-inflation parameter.

7.4 PLAYER COUNTS
-----------------
Player goals/shots often show many zeros because:

- player may not start;
- player may not appear;
- minutes vary.

The correct zero mechanism is the DMFP-07 role/minute mixture, not a generic ZIP
term.

7.5 DEFENSIVE EVENTS
--------------------
For low-event player/role categories, hurdle or zero-inflated models may be
reasonable after exposure is included.

7.6 PROMOTION
-------------
Requires:

- residual-zero test;
- time-safe proper-score improvement;
- interpretable state;
- stable parameters;
- no double counting with minutes/availability.

======================================================================
8. UNDERDISPERSION AND ALTERNATIVES
======================================================================

8.1 PROBLEM
-----------
Negative binomial handles overdispersion only.

Football counts can be underdispersed conditional on strong information.

8.2 CHALLENGERS
---------------
- double Poisson;
- Conway–Maxwell–Poisson;
- generalized Poisson;
- bivariate Weibull count;
- finite score-matrix model.

8.3 PRODUCTION POLICY
---------------------
The market-constrained score matrix is flexible enough to represent either
dispersion direction.

Parametric challengers are useful for:

- prior;
- extrapolation;
- compactness;
- diagnostics.

9. ADAPTIVE SCORE GRID AND TAILS
======================================================================

9.1 GRID
--------
Initial support:

    h = 0...G_H_max
    a = 0...G_A_max

9.2 ADAPTIVE MAXIMUM
--------------------
Increase limits until prior tail mass outside the grid is below configured
tolerance, e.g.:

    < 1e-8

for each marginal/joint approximation.

9.3 MARKET HIGH LINES
---------------------
Grid must cover all quoted total/correct-score lines plus safety margin.

9.4 OVERFLOW
------------
Preferred:
expand grid.

Fallback:
explicit tail states, not silent renormalisation.

9.5 NUMERICAL LOG SPACE
-----------------------
Compute low-probability score cells in log space where needed.

======================================================================
10. MARKET-CONSTRAINED SCORE MATRIX
======================================================================

10.1 SCORE VECTOR
-----------------
Flatten matrix P_h,a into vector p of length K.

10.2 MARKET DESIGN ROWS
-----------------------
For event k define indicator/weight vector A_k.

Examples:

Home win:
    A(h,a) = 1[h>a]

Draw:
    1[h=a]

Over 2.5:
    1[h+a >= 3]

Home clean sheet:
    1[a=0]

BTTS:
    1[h>=1 and a>=1]

Home team over 1.5:
    1[h>=2]

Correct score 2-1:
    1[h=2,a=1]

10.3 INPUT PROBABILITIES
------------------------
From DMFP-06:

- calibrated consensus m_k;
- lower/upper;
- confidence;
- disagreement;
- freshness;
- operator count.

10.4 SOFT OBJECTIVE
-------------------
Recommended:

    L(p)
      =
      sum_s p_s log(p_s / p0_s)
      +
      1/2 sum_k w_k (A_k p - m_k)^2
      +
      tail_penalty
      +
      optional roughness_penalty

where:

    w_k = 1 / sigma_k^2

and sigma derives from market uncertainty plus a model floor.

10.5 WHY KL
-----------
KL projection:

- preserves prior structure not contradicted by markets;
- yields a proper distribution;
- is conservative under incomplete constraints;
- avoids arbitrary parameter inversion;
- supports many market types.

10.6 HARD CONSTRAINTS
---------------------
May use:

    A_k p = m_k

only if constraints are mutually feasible and deliberately trusted.

Real bookmaker markets often disagree after independent margin treatment.
Soft constraints are safer.

10.7 CORRELATED MARKET EVIDENCE
-------------------------------
1X2, totals, BTTS and correct score are not independent observations.

Do not multiply their likelihoods as independent with full weight.

Use:

- covariance/market-family weighting;
- operator/source dependence;
- confidence caps;
- one market-family composite;
- robust sensitivity.

10.8 MARKET FAMILY
------------------
Suggested:

RESULT
TOTAL
TEAM_TOTAL
BTTS
CLEAN_SHEET
CORRECT_SCORE
HANDICAP

Weights within family are capped.

10.9 OPTIMISATION
-----------------
Use:

- convex solver where objective/constraints permit;
- softmax parameterisation;
- mirror descent;
- scipy/cvxpy audited implementation.

Need:

- deterministic convergence;
- gradient checking;
- feasibility diagnostics;
- fallback.

10.10 FALLBACK
----------------
If solver fails:

1. use reduced high-confidence constraint set;
2. fit lambda_H/lambda_A to 1X2 + total;
3. use market-augmented prior;
4. lower confidence.

10.11 OUTPUT CHECK
------------------
- probabilities nonnegative;
- sum one;
- constraint residuals;
- prior KL;
- tail mass;
- effective markets;
- sensitivity to each market.

- stoppage-time masses;
- home/away;
- pre-match strength;
- game state in a second-pass adjustment.

13.4 CONDITIONAL COUNT PROPERTY
-------------------------------
For an inhomogeneous Poisson process conditional on event count, event times are
distributed according to normalized intensity.

This provides a principled V1 timing mechanism when state dependence is modest.

13.5 SCORE-STATE ADJUSTMENT
---------------------------
V1:

1. initial times from baseline density;
2. order;
3. reweight/resample based on resulting score path;
4. run DMFP-07 substitution refinement once.

13.6 V2 SEQUENTIAL PROCESS
--------------------------
Minute/continuous-time hazards:

    lambda_home(t, state, lineup)
    lambda_away(t, state, lineup)

Simulate next event and update state.

Calibrate scenario weights or parameters to reproduce the pre-match score
matrix.

13.7 NO UNLIMITED ITERATION
---------------------------
Use one controlled refinement pass initially.
Measure whether additional iterations change distributions materially.

======================================================================
14. PLAYER GOAL ALLOCATION
======================================================================

14.1 SCORER CATEGORIES
----------------------
For a team goal at time t:

- eligible on-pitch team players;
- opponent own-goal category;
- OTHER/UNRESOLVED only in model fallback.

14.2 PLAYER PRIOR ABILITY
-------------------------
Hierarchical scoring ability from:

- goals and nonpenalty goals;
- shots;
- shots on target;
- xG where available;
- penalty role;
- tactical role;
- historical rates;
- team context;
- promoted/league transfer;
- player uncertainty.

Whitaker et al. provide an interpretable Bayesian Poisson approach to player
event abilities and connect player abilities to team scoring rates.

14.3 EXPOSURE
-------------
At goal time:

    eligibility_i(t) = 1 if player on pitch and attacking team

Weight:

    w_i(t)
      =
      eligibility_i(t)
      x exp(
          player_scoring_ability
          + role_effect
          + penalty/set_piece_effect
          + lineup_interaction
          + opponent_context
          + score_state_effect
        )

Normalize across eligible scorer categories.

14.4 GOAL TYPE
--------------
Penalty and direct-free-kick goals use specialized taker probabilities.
Open-play goals use open-play shares.

14.5 SUM CONSTRAINT
-------------------
For every goal:

    sum_i scorer_probability_i
    + P(opponent own goal)
    + P(other)
    = 1

14.6 TEAM-LEVEL EXPECTATION
--------------------------
Automatically:

    sum_i E[G_i] + E[opponent own goals] = E[team goals]

This is a hard validation property.

======================================================================
15. DERIVING PLAYER GOAL INTENSITY FROM ANYTIME ODDS
======================================================================

15.1 SIMPLE POISSON INVERSION
-----------------------------
If player goal count X is Poisson(lambda):

    P(X >= 1) = 1 - exp(-lambda)

Therefore:

    lambda = -log(1 - p_anytime)

15.2 LIMITATION
---------------
This inversion is valid only under the assumed count distribution and exposure.

It fails or becomes misleading when:

- minutes are random;
- p is conditional on starting;
- player's team score is non-Poisson;
- player goal share changes through time;
- penalties/open play differ;
- source probability is uncertain;
- goals are constrained by the team score matrix.

15.3 MINUTES-MIXTURE INVERSION
------------------------------
If scenario s has weight pi_s and player intensity lambda_s(theta):

    p_anytime(theta)
      =
      sum_s pi_s [1 - exp(-lambda_s(theta))]

Solve:

    p_anytime(theta) = market p

for player ability/share parameter theta.

15.4 TEAM-COUNT SHARE FORMULA
-----------------------------
If each of G team goals is assigned independently to player i with constant
share s_i:

    P(G_i = 0 | G=g)
      = (1-s_i)^g

Thus:

    P(G_i >= 1)
      =
      1 - sum_g P(G=g)(1-s_i)^g

This is selected for score-matrix consistency.

15.5 MULTIPLE GOALS
-------------------
Conditional binomial allocation:

    G_i | G=g ~ Binomial(g, s_i)

Then:

    P(G_i >= 2)
      =
      1
      - sum_g P(G=g) [
          (1-s_i)^g
          + g s_i (1-s_i)^(g-1)
        ]

More generally:

    P(G_i=k)
      =
      sum_g P(G=g) BinomialPMF(k; g, s_i)

15.6 TIME-VARYING SHARE
-----------------------
With minutes/goal times:

- simulate score count/times;
- only on-pitch players eligible;
- compute scenario-specific probability;
- calibrate by Monte Carlo/root solving.

15.7 CONDITIONAL-ON-START MARKET
--------------------------------
If settlement is conditional on start:

- compare to P(score>=1 | START under market rules);
- do not multiply/decondition without DMFP-07 and exact settlement;
- retain source uncertainty.

15.8 ONE-SIDED ODDS
-------------------
DMFP-06 may provide a raw/estimated market probability with wide bounds.

Use as soft evidence, not exact constraint.

======================================================================
16. RECONCILING PLAYER MARKETS WITH TEAM TOTALS
======================================================================

16.1 PRIOR SHARE VECTOR
-----------------------
For team T:

    s0 = {
      player shares,
      opponent own-goal share,
      unlisted-other share
    }

sum s0 = 1.

16.2 MARKET TARGETS
-------------------
For listed player i:

- anytime p_i;
- 2+ p_i;
- scorer line;
- penalty-specific market where available.

16.3 CONSTRAINED OPTIMISATION
-----------------------------
Choose player ability/share parameters theta to minimize:

    KL(s(theta) || s0)
    +
    1/2 sum_i [
      (P_theta(G_i>=1) - p_i)^2 / sigma_i^2
    ]
    +
    multi_goal penalties
    +
    regularisation

subject to:

- shares nonnegative;
- sum one;
- own-goal/other minimum priors;
- on-pitch eligibility;
- role constraints where justified.

16.4 WHY SOFT
-------------
Player markets can be inconsistent because:

- different books;
- conditional starting rules;
- margin;
- sparse coverage;
- stale prices;
- team market disagreement.

16.5 UNLISTED PLAYERS
---------------------
Unlisted players retain prior shares.

An explicit OTHER/UNLISTED group prevents listed players from absorbing all
team scoring probability.

16.6 MULTIPLE LINEUPS
---------------------
Calibrate over DMFP-07 lineup/minute scenarios.

Player shares differ by who starts and who is on the pitch.

16.7 PENALTY TAKER
------------------
Penalty scorer share is modeled separately from open-play share.

Uncertain penalty taker is a latent categorical state.

16.8 CONSISTENCY REPORT
-----------------------
Output:

- team expected goals;
- sum player expected goals;
- own-goal expectation;
- other expectation;
- residual;
- market residual by player;
- prior-to-posterior share shift.

Residual must be within numerical tolerance.

17. ASSIST MODEL
======================================================================

17.1 GOAL-LEVEL PROCESS
-----------------------
For each non-own goal:

1. classify mechanism;
2. determine whether assistable;
3. choose assister or NO_ASSIST;
4. preserve event chain for FPL assist rules.

17.2 ASSISTABILITY
------------------
Mechanism probabilities depend on:

- open play;
- set piece;
- penalty;
- direct free kick;
- rebound;
- defensive touches;
- provider event definitions.

DMFP-09 performs target-season FPL assist classification.

17.3 ASSISTER CATEGORIES
------------------------
Eligible on-pitch teammates excluding scorer.
Include:

- NO_ASSIST;
- OTHER/UNRESOLVED where necessary.

17.4 PRIOR ASSIST ABILITY
-------------------------
Hierarchical inputs:

- assists;
- expected assists;
- key passes;
- chances created;
- set-piece role;
- crosses;
- tactical role;
- minutes;
- scorer/teammate relationships.

17.5 ALLOCATION
---------------
For assistable goal k:

    P(assister=i)
      proportional to
      on_pitch_i
      x exp(assist_ability_i + role + goal_type + scorer_pair_effect)

17.6 SCORER–ASSISTER CORRELATION
-------------------------------
The goal event creates:

- positive correlation between scorer and assister outputs;
- negative within-goal constraint because scorer cannot assist self;
- teammate competition for the one assist.

17.7 ASSIST MARKET CALIBRATION
------------------------------
Analogous to goal allocation:

- P(player >=1 assist);
- optional multi-assist;
- soft fit;
- complete/minutes/settlement scenarios;
- NO_ASSIST mass retained.

17.8 PAIR EFFECTS
-----------------
Player pairs may have sparse data.

Use hierarchical shrinkage.
Do not fit unrestricted pair parameters.

======================================================================
18. SHOTS, SHOTS ON TARGET AND SAVES
======================================================================

18.1 HIERARCHY
--------------
A coherent attacking-event tree:

TEAM SHOTS
  -> PLAYER SHOOTER
  -> ON TARGET / OFF TARGET / BLOCKED
  -> if ON TARGET:
       GOAL / GOALKEEPER SAVE / LAST-LINE BLOCK

18.2 TEAM SHOT COUNT
--------------------
Candidate distribution:

- negative binomial / Poisson-lognormal;
- conditioned on team goals, tempo, possession and pressure.

Shots generally provide larger counts than goals and often show overdispersion.

18.3 PLAYER SHOT ALLOCATION
---------------------------
Conditional on team shots:

- multinomial/Dirichlet-multinomial across on-pitch players;
- shares depend on role, minutes, shot ability and match state.

18.4 GOAL CONSISTENCY
---------------------
Every non-own, nonadministrative goal must be associated with a scoring shot
category.

The number of goal-producing shots cannot exceed shots or SOT.

18.5 ON-TARGET
--------------
For each shot:

- probability on target;
- player finishing/shooting accuracy;
- location/type where simulated;
- game state.

18.6 GOALKEEPER SAVES
---------------------
For goalkeeper g:

    saves_g
      =
      opponent SOT faced while g on pitch
      - goals from on-target shots conceded while g on pitch
      - last-line blocks by non-GK
      + provider-definition adjustments

Penalty saves remain explicitly labelled.

18.7 OWN GOALS
--------------
Own goals usually do not count as an opponent player's shot on target under
standard event definitions.

Provider mapping controls exact statistics.

18.8 KEEPER SUBSTITUTION
------------------------
Allocate shots faced and goals by on-pitch interval.

18.9 SAVE MARKET
----------------
If a goalkeeper save market exists:

- calibrate keeper-save distribution softly;
- preserve consistency with opponent SOT and goals.

18.10 SHOT/SOT MARKETS
----------------------
Player lines calibrate:

- shot-share;
- on-target probability;
- count dispersion.

Do not fit each independently in a way that permits SOT > shots.

19. PENALTY PROCESS
======================================================================

19.1 COMPONENTS
---------------
- team penalties awarded;
- taker;
- keeper;
- result;
- goal mechanism;
- missed penalty;
- saved penalty;
- retake/void.

19.2 AWARD COUNT
----------------
Rare-event model:

- Bernoulli or low-rate Poisson;
- optional zero-inflated/hurdle only if justified;
- team/opponent/referee/match-state effects;
- market evidence where available.

19.3 SCORELINE CONSISTENCY
-------------------------
V1 final-score-first procedure:

1. sample final team goals;
2. sample how many scored goals are penalties conditional on total goals and
   penalty-scoring prior;
3. sample additional unscored penalties;
4. ensure scored-penalty count <= team goals;
5. allocate remaining goals to other mechanisms.

19.4 UNCONVERTED PENALTY
------------------------
Results:

- saved;
- off target;
- woodwork;
- other/void.

19.5 PENALTY SAVE
-----------------
A saved penalty:

- creates one missed penalty for taker;
- creates one penalty-save event for on-pitch goalkeeper;
- may count as ordinary save depending provider/FPL definition;
- does not create a goal.

19.6 PENALTY TAKER
------------------
Latent categorical distribution based on:

- recent responsibility;
- lineup;
- hierarchy;
- manager comments;
- prior events;
- substitution.

19.7 RETAKE
-----------
Provider/official events must distinguish void first attempt and valid retake.
Do not count both as independent penalties.

29. COHERENCE VALIDATION
======================================================================

29.1 HARD INVARIANTS
--------------------
For every scenario:

- home/away final scores equal goal events;
- credited player goals + opponent own goals equal team score;
- every player event occurs while on pitch;
- scorer belongs to scoring team;
- assister belongs to scoring team;
- assister != scorer;
- at most one official assist per goal under taxonomy;
- SOT <= shots;
- goals from shots <= SOT plus own-goal exceptions;
- goalkeeper saves >= 0;
- goalkeeper saves <= SOT faced adjusted by definitions;
- penalty saved implies missed penalty and eligible keeper;
- red-carded player has no later events;
- player defensive sums equal team totals;
- probability vectors/PMFs sum one;
- no negative counts.

29.2 SOFT DIAGNOSTICS
---------------------
- score matrix market residual;
- player market residual;
- team/player expectation residual;
- unusual tail;
- extreme latent factor;
- excessive rejected scenarios.

29.3 SCENARIO REJECTION
-----------------------
If hard invariant fails:

- reject/resample;
- count error;
- alert if rate exceeds threshold.

Never patch final counts silently.
