===================================================

Document ID: DMFP-01
Version: 0.9 (Research Governance Baseline)
Status: Proposed controlling research standard for Sebastian approval
Target programme: DMF Pulse — FPL Decision Engine
Target season: Fantasy Premier League 2026/27
Research cut-off for this version: 16 July 2026
Primary owner and final decision-maker: Sebastian Greenhalgh
Research agent: ChatGPT, under human review
Implementation agent: Codex, under human review
Parent architecture: DMFP-00 — MASTER ARCHITECTURE AND PRODUCT DEFINITION
Next planned document: DMFP-02 — FPL RULES, SCORING AND SEASON CONFIGURATION

IMPORTANT PURPOSE NOTE
----------------------
Every later DMF Pulse specification will be partly produced through AI-assisted
web research. That creates speed and breadth, but it also creates material risks:

- invented or misquoted sources;
- plausible but unsupported claims;
- outdated API, rule or pricing information;
- reliance on search-result snippets rather than the source;
- citation laundering, where many pages repeat one unsupported claim;
- confusing official claims with independent validation;
- treating peer review as a guarantee of truth;
- treating a preprint as settled evidence;
- ignoring corrections, retractions and version changes;
- overlooking licence, database-right or contractual restrictions;
- silently converting uncertainty into certainty;
- selecting evidence that supports a preferred architecture;
- allowing ChatGPT summaries to become the source of record.

This document is the control layer designed to prevent those failures.

It governs:

- how research questions are framed;
- how sources are discovered, retrieved, checked and archived;
- which sources are authoritative for each type of claim;
- how claims are atomised and linked to evidence;
- how reliability and certainty are judged;
- how conflicting evidence is handled;
- how ChatGPT may and may not participate;
- how licensing and data-use rights are recorded;
- how evidence is refreshed;
- how research becomes an architecture decision;
- how Codex validates the evidence register automatically.

This is an operational governance standard. It is not legal advice, a formal
academic systematic-review protocol, or a substitute for specialist statistical,
technical or legal review where the risk warrants it.

NORMATIVE LANGUAGE
------------------
MUST / MUST NOT:
A non-negotiable requirement.

SHOULD / SHOULD NOT:
The default. A documented architecture decision record is required to deviate.

MAY:
Optional, provided no MUST requirement is violated.

0. EXECUTIVE CONTROL SUMMARY
======================================================================

0.1 THE STANDARD IN ONE SENTENCE
--------------------------------
No material DMF Pulse claim, dependency or architecture decision may rely on a
forgotten link, an unverified AI summary, an outdated document or an
unrecorded assumption.

0.2 THE THREE LINKED REGISTERS
------------------------------
Research governance is built around three linked registers:

1. SOURCE REGISTER
   What is the source, which version was examined, what does it actually say,
   how reliable is it for this purpose, and what are the usage rights?

2. CLAIM REGISTER
   What atomic claim is being made, which independent sources support or oppose
   it, how certain is the claim, and when must it be checked again?

3. DECISION REGISTER
   Which architectural choice was made, what alternatives were considered,
   which claims support it, what trade-offs remain, and what evidence would
   reverse it?

A bibliography alone is insufficient because it does not reveal which source
supports which claim or how that claim affects a decision.

0.3 CONTROLLING RESEARCH DECISIONS
----------------------------------
RD-01-01. No universal evidence hierarchy.
The best source depends on the claim. Official FPL rules are controlling for FPL
rules. Official provider documentation is primary for a documented API contract.
Independent testing is stronger for whether that API works as claimed.
Peer-reviewed empirical research is stronger than a vendor page for model
performance. Contracts and legislation are controlling for permitted use.

RD-01-02. Source class is not claim certainty.
A prestigious source can be indirect, stale or inapplicable. A lower-status
source can directly document an otherwise undocumented behaviour. Every source
is assessed in relation to a specific atomic claim.

RD-01-03. ChatGPT is a research instrument, never an evidence source.
AI output can discover, organise, compare and draft. The underlying human- or
organisation-authored source is cited and registered. Unsupported model memory
is not evidence.

RD-01-04. Search snippets are discovery aids only.
A search-result title or snippet MUST NOT support a material claim. The source
must be opened, inspected and recorded.

RD-01-05. Important claims require traceable locators.
The register must record the exact page, section, paragraph, line range, API
field, endpoint, code commit or contractual clause supporting the claim.

RD-01-06. Recency is a property of the claim.
FPL rules, API schemas, provider pricing and terms can change quickly and require
short verification windows. Foundational mathematics may remain valid for years.

RD-01-07. Corroboration must be independent.
Five articles repeating one press release are one evidence chain. Independence is
assessed through origin and methodology, not domain count.

RD-01-08. Reproducible evidence receives additional weight.
Code, data, executable examples, test accounts, schemas and live probes increase
confidence when they are relevant and independently inspectable.

RD-01-09. Negative and conflicting evidence is retained.
Evidence that contradicts the selected approach MUST remain in the register.
Disagreement is not removed by choosing a preferred conclusion.

RD-01-10. Licensing is part of evidence quality.
A technically useful source that cannot lawfully be stored, transformed or used
in the intended product is not a viable production dependency.

RD-01-11. Important architecture decisions use a predeclared research protocol.
The question, scope, search plan, inclusion criteria and stopping rule should be
written before synthesis to reduce confirmation bias.

RD-01-12. Every critical claim has a refresh trigger.
A source is not permanently verified. The register records a next-review date or
an event-based trigger such as FPL launch, API version change, contract renewal,
new paper version, correction or retraction.

RD-01-13. Research quality is graded, not implied.
The final specification distinguishes:
- verified fact;
- empirical estimate;
- inference;
- recommendation;
- assumption;
- unresolved question.

RD-01-14. Failure to find evidence is a result.
The report must say what was searched, what was not found and what uncertainty
remains. It must not manufacture an answer to complete the architecture.

RD-01-15. Implementation cannot outrun evidence.
Codex MUST NOT implement a critical dependency or algorithm whose governing
claim remains unverified or whose usage rights remain unresolved.

1. PURPOSE, SCOPE AND NON-GOALS
======================================================================

1.1 PURPOSE
-----------
The purpose of DMFP-01 is to make every later specification:

- traceable;
- current;
- evidence-aware;
- reproducible;
- reviewable;
- legally usable;
- resistant to AI confabulation;
- explicit about uncertainty;
- capable of being corrected without rewriting history.

It is designed for a mixed evidence environment containing:

- official game rules;
- undocumented or semi-documented web APIs;
- commercial data feeds;
- betting exchange and bookmaker documentation;
- statistical research;
- optimisation research;
- software documentation;
- legislation, contract terms and intellectual-property guidance;
- FPL practitioner research;
- community observations;
- live tests performed by DMF Pulse.

1.2 IN SCOPE
------------
This document governs research used to:

- write all DMFP specification files;
- select data providers;
- select algorithms;
- define mathematical assumptions;
- interpret FPL rules;
- assess APIs;
- assess data licensing;
- establish backtesting methods;
- define uncertainty and decision policy;
- instruct Codex;
- justify model promotion;
- publish technical explanations;
- revise previous decisions.

1.3 OUT OF SCOPE
----------------
This document does not:

- declare a single algorithm optimal;
- replace the module-specific research;
- conduct legal due diligence;
- guarantee that a source is truthful;
- convert every module review into a formal academic systematic review;
- require paid access to every potentially relevant source;
- require exhaustive coverage when a targeted operational answer is sufficient;
- permit copying or redistributing protected content merely because it was cited;
- allow citation count or journal prestige to replace critical appraisal;
- treat popularity in the FPL community as validation.

1.4 RELATION TO DMFP-00
-----------------------
DMFP-00 requires:

- market-first but not market-only forecasting;
- time-travel-safe data;
- configurable rules;
- probabilistic outputs;
- reproducibility;
- legal provider access;
- no unsupported LLM numbers;
- a validated vertical slice.

DMFP-01 defines the evidence process by which those architectural commitments
and all future refinements are checked.

======================================================================
2. TERMINOLOGY
======================================================================

2.1 SOURCE
----------
A retrievable object that can support, oppose or contextualise a claim.

Examples:
- official rules page;
- API documentation;
- contract;
- legislation;
- research paper;
- dataset;
- code repository;
- software release;
- test result;
- forum post;
- press conference transcript.

A ChatGPT answer is not a source.

2.2 PRIMARY SOURCE
------------------
The closest available record to the subject or event.

Examples:
- official FPL rules for scoring;
- provider's API schema for documented fields;
- raw odds snapshots for a market;
- original research paper for a method or result;
- legislation or signed contract for legal obligations;
- original code and data for a computational result.

"Primary" does not automatically mean unbiased or correct.

2.3 SECONDARY SOURCE
--------------------
A source that analyses, synthesises, explains or reports primary sources.

Examples:
- systematic review;
- textbook;
- technical tutorial;
- news report;
- community explanation.

A high-quality secondary source can be more useful than one weak primary study,
but should not replace the primary contract or rule when the exact wording
controls implementation.

2.4 ATOMIC CLAIM
----------------
One proposition that can be independently supported or contradicted.

Bad:
"Provider X is the best API because it is cheap, accurate and has historical
player props."

Atomic:
- Provider X documents EPL player props.
- A sample account returned player props for 95% of expected starters.
- Historical snapshots are available on the selected plan.
- The contract allows internal storage for 12 months.
- Monthly cost is £Y on the date checked.
- Provider X had lower missingness than Provider Z in the sample.

2.5 FACT
--------
A claim directly established by controlling or strongly verified evidence.

Example:
"The official 2026/27 FPL rules assign N points for event X."

It remains time-scoped and version-scoped.

2.6 EMPIRICAL ESTIMATE
----------------------
A quantity inferred from observations, with sampling, measurement or model
uncertainty.

Example:
"The power de-vigging method had the best mean log score in the 2022–2025 sample."

2.7 INFERENCE
-------------
A reasoned conclusion not directly stated by the source.

Example:
"Because provider coverage is incomplete before line-ups, Pulse will need a
fallback player-allocation model."

The supporting observations must be cited and the inference labelled.

2.8 RECOMMENDATION
------------------
A proposed action based on evidence, constraints and preferences.

Example:
"Use Provider X as primary and Provider Y as fallback."

A recommendation is not transformed into fact by citing sources.

2.9 ASSUMPTION
--------------
A proposition temporarily accepted so work can proceed despite incomplete
evidence.

Assumptions require:
- owner;
- rationale;
- impact;
- expiry or verification trigger;
- sensitivity;
- status.

2.10 PROVENANCE
---------------
Information about the entities, activities and agents involved in producing a
research object, claim, dataset or decision.

For DMF Pulse this includes:
- source;
- version;
- retrieval time;
- transformation;
- researcher or agent;
- code;
- data;
- review;
- output linkage.

2.11 REPRODUCIBILITY
--------------------
DMF Pulse adopts the National Academies' computational meaning:

Obtaining consistent computational results using the same input data,
computational steps, methods, code and conditions of analysis.

2.12 REPLICABILITY
------------------
Obtaining consistent findings when the same question is examined with new data
or an independently constructed implementation.

2.13 VERIFICATION
-----------------
Checking that a source, claim, implementation or transformation matches its
specified requirement.

2.14 VALIDATION
---------------
Checking that the requirement, model or decision is appropriate for the intended
real-world purpose.

2.15 RELIABILITY
----------------
The degree to which a source is dependable for the specific claim at issue.

2.16 CERTAINTY / CONFIDENCE
---------------------------
The overall judgement about how likely an atomic claim is to remain materially
correct for its stated context and time.

2.17 DIRECTNESS
---------------
How closely the source addresses the exact population, season, endpoint, market,
decision or implementation context.

2.18 FRESHNESS
--------------
Whether the source is current enough for the claim's rate of change.

2.19 INDEPENDENCE
-----------------
Whether evidence was generated from a genuinely separate information or
methodological chain.

2.20 SOURCE BUNDLE
------------------
The immutable manifest of all sources, versions, snapshots, search logs and
extractions used for one specification or architecture decision.

3. RESEARCH THREAT MODEL
======================================================================

3.1 AI CONFABULATION
--------------------
Generative AI can produce fluent but false statements, references and
explanations. Confidence of tone is not confidence of evidence.

Controls:
- no source exists until opened;
- verify title, author, date and URL;
- verify the source supports the exact claim;
- store a locator;
- independently inspect all critical citations;
- label unsuccessful searches;
- never cite ChatGPT as evidence;
- require human sign-off for blocking claims.

3.2 AUTOMATION BIAS
-------------------
A user may accept an AI synthesis because it is organised and detailed.

Controls:
- display source grades and dissent;
- require alternatives;
- separate extraction from synthesis;
- require a "what could change this decision?" section;
- sample-check source-to-claim mappings;
- use independent review for critical decisions.

3.3 SEARCH ENGINE BIAS
----------------------
Search ranking favours popular, optimised, accessible and recent pages rather
than necessarily the best evidence.

Controls:
- search official domains directly;
- use bibliographic databases and DOI registries;
- use backward and forward citation searching;
- search for criticism, failure and contrary evidence;
- record queries;
- perform lateral reading;
- do not stop at the first plausible result.

3.4 CONFIRMATION BIAS
---------------------
Research may be framed to justify a preferred design.

Controls:
- predeclare the decision question;
- list plausible alternatives before searching;
- include explicit disconfirmation queries;
- record rejected evidence;
- separate researcher judgement from source statements;
- use a stopping rule independent of preferred conclusion.

3.5 CITATION LAUNDERING
-----------------------
Multiple sources may repeat a claim that originates from one vendor, press
release, forum post or misunderstood paper.

Controls:
- trace claims to their earliest or controlling source;
- record evidence-chain origin;
- cluster dependent sources;
- count independent chains, not URLs;
- prefer original data or documentation.

3.6 AUTHORITY BIAS
------------------
Official, prestigious or highly cited sources may be accepted outside their
domain of authority.

Examples:
- a provider is authoritative about its advertised endpoint, not necessarily
  its real uptime or predictive value;
- FPL is authoritative about scoring, not about the optimal strategy;
- a peer-reviewed football paper is not authoritative about a changed FPL rule;
- a lawyer's blog is not the controlling contract.

3.7 RECENCY BIAS
----------------
New research can be interesting but unreplicated. Old research can be robust but
inapplicable to current APIs or rules.

Controls:
- separate publication recency from applicability;
- retain foundational methods;
- apply stricter verification to recent preprints;
- version current operational claims;
- use evidence-age thresholds by claim type.

3.8 SURVIVORSHIP AND PUBLICATION BIAS
-------------------------------------
Successful methods and positive findings are more visible than failures.

Controls:
- search for null results and critiques;
- inspect unpublished technical reports where credible;
- rely on prospective evaluation;
- preserve failed DMF experiments;
- avoid selecting methods on reported headline performance alone.

3.9 DATA SNOOPING AND LEAKAGE
-----------------------------
A method can appear strong because it was selected after seeing the target
period or used future information.

Controls:
- require temporal splits;
- record decision-time information sets;
- distinguish exploratory from confirmatory results;
- pre-register prospective tests internally;
- inspect data construction and timestamps;
- penalise repeated experimentation on the same holdout.

3.10 LICENSING BLINDNESS
------------------------
Technical access may be mistaken for legal permission.

Controls:
- record contract and terms;
- distinguish access, storage, transformation, publication and commercial use;
- require legal review for ambiguous production dependencies;
- record geographic and account restrictions;
- do not infer permission from the absence of a technical block.

3.11 VERSION BLINDNESS
----------------------
A paper, API, rules page, preprint, package or contract may change.

Controls:
- record version and date;
- save permitted snapshots or hashes;
- check publisher status and corrections;
- record supersession;
- use versioned software docs;
- reverify before implementation and release.

3.12 FALSE PRECISION
--------------------
A numerical evidence score can create unjustified confidence.

Control:
The standard uses structured dimensions and an overall judgement. Dimension
ratings guide reasoning; they are not blindly summed into a truth score.

4. ROLES, ACCOUNTABILITY AND SIGN-OFF
======================================================================

4.1 SEBASTIAN — ACCOUNTABLE OWNER
---------------------------------
Sebastian:

- defines the product objective;
- approves critical architecture decisions;
- approves use of paid or restricted sources;
- accepts or rejects residual risk;
- signs off the official FPL season configuration;
- approves any manual assumptions used in live decisions;
- owns the final research and implementation outputs.

4.2 CHATGPT — RESEARCH AGENT
----------------------------
ChatGPT may:

- formulate research questions;
- search the web;
- locate primary and secondary sources;
- extract and compare claims;
- identify uncertainty and contradiction;
- draft source, claim and decision records;
- propose implementation implications;
- create the specification file.

ChatGPT must:

- use current web research where claims may have changed;
- cite every material external claim in its working answer;
- open and inspect sources;
- state when evidence is missing;
- distinguish fact, inference and recommendation;
- avoid unsupported numerical claims;
- avoid presenting its own prose as authority;
- preserve the source trail.

4.3 HUMAN VERIFIER
------------------
For critical claims, Sebastian or a nominated reviewer must:

- open the controlling source;
- confirm the locator and interpretation;
- confirm applicability;
- confirm the licence status or escalate;
- check key contrary evidence;
- mark the record VERIFIED_HUMAN.

4.4 CODEX — IMPLEMENTATION AND MACHINE VALIDATION
-------------------------------------------------
Codex may implement:

- register schemas;
- validators;
- link and metadata checks;
- document templates;
- version comparisons;
- query logs;
- source-bundle manifests;
- test probes;
- evidence dashboards.

Codex must not:

- manufacture missing evidence;
- infer legal rights;
- upgrade claim confidence automatically;
- replace human review for a blocking claim;
- implement a dependency marked RED or UNRESOLVED;
- delete contradictory records.

4.5 OPTIONAL SPECIALIST REVIEW
------------------------------
Specialist review SHOULD be obtained when:

- a licensing term controls commercial viability;
- a statistical method materially changes decisions and is not well understood;
- a security design handles authenticated FPL data;
- a provider contract creates liability;
- a method is used outside the population or conditions studied;
- a novel rank-utility model drives aggressive live decisions.

4.6 SIGN-OFF STATES
-------------------
DRAFT_AI:
Created by ChatGPT, not independently checked.

CHECKED_SOURCE:
Source opened and metadata/claim checked by the research agent.

VERIFIED_HUMAN:
Human checked the source, claim and applicability.

SPECIALIST_VERIFIED:
A relevant specialist checked the claim.

SUPERSEDED:
Retained for history but no longer current.

REJECTED:
Insufficient, contradicted or unusable.

BLOCKED:
Cannot be used until a stated issue is resolved.

5. CLAIM TAXONOMY AND CRITICALITY
======================================================================

5.1 CLAIM TYPES
---------------
RULE:
Official FPL or competition rule.

API_CONTRACT:
Documented endpoint, field, rate limit or behaviour.

OBSERVED_API_BEHAVIOUR:
What a test call or production probe actually returned.

LICENSING:
Permitted access, storage, transformation, publication or commercial use.

MATHEMATICAL:
Theorem, derivation or property of an algorithm.

EMPIRICAL_PERFORMANCE:
Measured predictive, computational or operational performance.

CAUSAL:
Claim that one factor causes another.

DESCRIPTIVE:
Observed frequency, coverage, price or characteristic.

FORECASTING_METHOD:
Claim that a model/method is appropriate for a prediction task.

OPTIMISATION_METHOD:
Claim that a formulation or solver is suitable.

SOFTWARE_BEHAVIOUR:
Versioned package, tool or platform capability.

FPL_STRATEGY:
Claim about decisions, ownership, prices, chips or rank.

OPERATIONAL:
Latency, uptime, cost, support or workflow.

SECURITY_PRIVACY:
Data protection or security requirement.

COMMUNITY_OBSERVATION:
Anecdotal or crowdsourced observation awaiting validation.

5.2 CRITICALITY
---------------
BLOCKING:
Wrong evidence can make the engine illegal, unusable, unable to run or
fundamentally incorrect.

Examples:
- official scoring rule;
- deadline rule;
- provider storage right;
- player identity mapping;
- information-cut-off rule.

CORE:
Material to model quality or season decisions.

Examples:
- de-vigging method;
- minutes model;
- optimisation horizon;
- chip opportunity-cost method.

SUPPORTING:
Improves a module but has a safe fallback.

Examples:
- a secondary injury source;
- an optional calibration diagnostic;
- one additional provider.

CONTEXT:
Useful background with no direct production dependency.

5.3 EVIDENCE MINIMUM BY CRITICALITY
-----------------------------------
BLOCKING:
- controlling primary source where one exists;
- exact locator;
- CHECKED_SOURCE and VERIFIED_HUMAN;
- licence field complete if relevant;
- live test if operational;
- no unresolved material contradiction;
- revalidation trigger.

CORE:
- at least one strong primary or peer-reviewed source;
- preferably one independent corroborating chain;
- applicability and limitations recorded;
- empirical validation plan;
- human review before production promotion.

SUPPORTING:
- one credible direct source;
- limitations recorded;
- fallback documented.

CONTEXT:
- credible source and clear citation;
- no production reliance.

6. CLAIM-SPECIFIC SOURCE HIERARCHY
======================================================================

6.1 WHY THERE IS NO SINGLE LADDER
---------------------------------
A source hierarchy such as "official, then peer-reviewed, then practitioner" is
a useful starting point, but it can produce errors when applied mechanically.

Examples:
- official FPL rules outrank a research paper for scoring;
- a peer-reviewed benchmark outranks a vendor claim for accuracy;
- an actual API response can contradict stale official documentation;
- a signed contract outranks a marketing page for data rights;
- an independently replicated preprint can be stronger than a weak
  peer-reviewed study;
- a community observation can reveal an undocumented price behaviour, but it
  remains provisional until tested.

The standard therefore uses a general discovery hierarchy plus claim-specific
rules.

6.2 GENERAL DISCOVERY HIERARCHY
-------------------------------
H1 — Controlling official and legal sources
- official FPL and Premier League rules;
- official competition announcements;
- legislation and regulator guidance;
- signed provider contract, licence or data-processing terms;
- official standards.

H2 — Official technical sources and direct evidence
- official API documentation;
- schemas and changelogs;
- provider support response;
- live API probe;
- raw dataset;
- source code and versioned release;
- official methodology note.

H3 — High-quality research synthesis
- systematic review;
- meta-analysis;
- consensus or standards document;
- evidence-based guideline;
- well-scoped review with transparent methods.

H4 — Peer-reviewed primary research
- empirical study;
- methods paper;
- validated software paper;
- replication study.

H5 — Credible working papers and preprints
- identifiable researchers or institutions;
- clear methods;
- data/code where possible;
- version history;
- no known correction or retraction.

H6 — Technical books and university materials
- established textbooks;
- graduate notes;
- institutional teaching materials;
- technical monographs.

H7 — Practitioner research with reproducible evidence
- documented code;
- public data;
- clear timestamp and methodology;
- benchmark or field report;
- provider-neutral technical analysis.

H8 — Practitioner commentary and journalism
- useful context or leads;
- requires trace-back and corroboration.

H9 — Community observations
- forum posts;
- social posts;
- Discord observations;
- crowd predictions;
- undocumented anecdotes.

H10 — Unusable as evidence
- AI-generated answer without verified sources;
- search snippet;
- copied content with unknown origin;
- anonymous unsupported claim;
- affiliate page posing as independent analysis;
- source that cannot be retrieved or checked.

The hierarchy is not a scoring formula.

6.3 OFFICIAL FPL RULE CLAIMS
----------------------------
Preferred order:
1. official in-game rules/help page for the target season;
2. official Premier League/FPL announcement for the target season;
3. target-season application behaviour verified in a controlled test;
4. official clarification/support response;
5. previous-season rules for comparison only;
6. respected practitioner explanation;
7. community observation.

Rule text and observed implementation must both be recorded if they differ.

6.4 FIXTURE, DEADLINE AND PLAYER-STATUS CLAIMS
----------------------------------------------
Preferred order:
1. official FPL game data and official Premier League fixture announcement;
2. official club/competition announcement;
3. authoritative data feed;
4. reputable news source quoting the primary source;
5. practitioner/community source.

Deadline-sensitive claims require current timestamps.

6.5 API CAPABILITY CLAIMS
-------------------------
Preferred order:
1. signed contract/service schedule;
2. official versioned API documentation/schema;
3. official changelog/status page;
4. live authenticated test;
5. official support response;
6. independent implementation experience;
7. community report.

A documented endpoint is not accepted as production-ready until a live test
confirms:
- access;
- expected fields;
- target competition/market coverage;
- latency;
- rate limit;
- historical depth where required.

6.6 PROVIDER PERFORMANCE CLAIMS
-------------------------------
Preferred order:
1. DMF's own prospective benchmark;
2. independent reproducible benchmark;
3. peer-reviewed comparison;
4. transparent practitioner comparison;
5. provider-published benchmark;
6. customer testimonial.

Vendor evidence is useful but carries an incentive conflict.

6.7 DATA LICENSING CLAIMS
-------------------------
Preferred order:
1. signed contract and service schedule;
2. legislation;
3. regulator or official government guidance;
4. provider terms and licence text;
5. explicit written provider clarification;
6. specialist legal advice;
7. provider FAQ;
8. community interpretation.

Marketing pages do not establish reuse rights.

6.8 MATHEMATICAL AND STATISTICAL CLAIMS
---------------------------------------
Preferred order:
1. formal proof or established reference;
2. peer-reviewed methods paper;
3. authoritative technical monograph;
4. high-quality university materials;
5. transparent preprint;
6. reproducible practitioner derivation;
7. informal explanation.

For applied suitability, mathematical correctness is necessary but not
sufficient. Empirical validation is still required.

6.9 EMPIRICAL FORECASTING CLAIMS
--------------------------------
Preferred order:
1. independent replication on relevant out-of-time data;
2. peer-reviewed study with available data/code;
3. systematic synthesis of relevant studies;
4. peer-reviewed study without full artefacts;
5. credible preprint with reproducible artefacts;
6. practitioner backtest with strict temporal controls;
7. in-sample or opaque performance claim.

Relevance to EPL, FPL, player markets and the target information set matters more
than a headline metric from another domain.

6.10 SOFTWARE AND LIBRARY CLAIMS
--------------------------------
Preferred order:
1. documentation for the exact pinned version;
2. source code and tests for that version;
3. release notes/changelog;
4. executable minimal reproduction;
5. maintainer issue/discussion;
6. third-party tutorial;
7. forum answer.

Never cite "latest documentation" for a pinned old version without checking
compatibility.

6.11 FPL STRATEGY CLAIMS
------------------------
Preferred order:
1. prospective DMF evaluation;
2. historical, time-safe reproducible analysis;
3. peer-reviewed or transparent optimisation research;
4. robust simulation;
5. respected practitioner research with data;
6. community consensus;
7. anecdote.

A successful manager's opinion is a hypothesis, not proof of causality.

6.12 COMMUNITY OBSERVATIONS
---------------------------
Community evidence may:
- surface undocumented behaviour;
- identify edge cases;
- provide test hypotheses;
- reveal operational changes faster than documentation.

It must be labelled PROVISIONAL and independently verified before it controls
production.

7. SOURCE APPRAISAL DIMENSIONS
======================================================================

Every important source is assessed against the dimensions below. Ratings are:

STRONG
ADEQUATE
WEAK
UNKNOWN
NOT_APPLICABLE

No automatic sum is used.

7.1 AUTHORITY
-------------
Does the author or organisation control, create or possess relevant expertise
over the subject?

7.2 DIRECTNESS
--------------
Does the source address the exact claim, population, season, endpoint, market,
provider version and decision context?

7.3 METHODOLOGICAL RIGOUR
-------------------------
Where empirical:
- design;
- sample;
- measurement;
- validation;
- uncertainty;
- comparators;
- leakage control;
- statistical analysis;
- disclosure of limitations.

7.4 REPRODUCIBILITY
-------------------
Are data, code, environment, queries or executable examples available?
Can the result be recreated?
Has another party reproduced or replicated it?

7.5 TEMPORAL FIT
----------------
Is the source current for the claim?
Was it published before or after the relevant rule/API change?
Does it use data from a comparable era?

7.6 APPLICABILITY
-----------------
Does the evidence transfer to:
- Premier League;
- FPL;
- pre-deadline information;
- player-level forecasting;
- Sebastian's operating context;
- the 2026/27 rules;
- the planned provider and software stack?

7.7 INDEPENDENCE
----------------
Is the source an independent information chain?
Does it rely on the same underlying dataset, press release or model as another
source?

7.8 CONFLICTS AND INCENTIVES
----------------------------
Does the author sell the product, method or service?
Are funding, affiliate relationships or commercial incentives disclosed?

7.9 COMPLETENESS
----------------
Can the methods, limitations and scope be inspected?
Is only an abstract, slide, marketing summary or partial document available?

7.10 STATUS
-----------
Is it:
- current version;
- peer reviewed;
- preprint;
- corrected;
- retracted;
- withdrawn;
- deprecated;
- superseded;
- archived?

7.11 ACCESS AND LICENSING
-------------------------
Can Pulse:
- read;
- cite;
- store;
- transform;
- use internally;
- use commercially;
- display outputs;
- redistribute?

7.12 OVERALL RELIABILITY ASSESSMENT
-----------------------------------
A short narrative answers:

"For this precise claim, why should Pulse trust or distrust this source?"

======================================================================
8. CLAIM CERTAINTY FRAMEWORK
======================================================================

8.1 CERTAINTY LEVELS
--------------------
HIGH:
The claim is directly supported by controlling or convergent strong evidence,
has good applicability and no unresolved material contradiction. Further
evidence is unlikely to change the material conclusion, although current claims
still need scheduled verification.

MODERATE:
The claim is probably correct and suitable for provisional architecture, but one
or more limitations remain: indirectness, incomplete replication, uncertain
freshness, limited sample or moderate disagreement.

LOW:
The claim is plausible but materially uncertain. It may guide experimentation,
not a high-risk production dependency without fallback and sensitivity testing.

VERY_LOW:
Evidence is anecdotal, indirect, conflicting, opaque or substantially outdated.
The claim is hypothesis-generating only.

UNRATED:
Appraisal is incomplete.

CONTRADICTED:
Strong current evidence indicates the claim is materially false.

8.2 FACTORS THAT LOWER CERTAINTY
--------------------------------
- risk of bias;
- inconsistent findings;
- indirectness;
- imprecision;
- publication or availability bias;
- stale version;
- non-independent corroboration;
- unresolved conflict;
- missing methods;
- no temporal controls;
- selective reporting;
- licensing uncertainty for a production claim;
- provider incentive;
- abstract-only access;
- target-season mismatch.

These adapt concepts used in GRADE and risk-of-bias frameworks. DMF Pulse does
not claim formal GRADE compliance because its evidence includes contracts, APIs,
software and operational tests as well as research studies.

8.3 FACTORS THAT MAY RAISE CERTAINTY
------------------------------------
- direct controlling source;
- exact live behaviour test;
- independent replication;
- available code/data;
- prospective validation;
- strong out-of-time performance;
- multiple independent evidence chains;
- stable result under sensitivity analysis;
- consistent result across relevant seasons/providers;
- explicit correction and version history;
- specialist verification.

8.4 EVIDENCE CLASS AND CERTAINTY ARE DISTINCT
---------------------------------------------
Example:
An official provider page may be H2 but support only MODERATE certainty that a
market is practically available, because the claim has not been tested in the
target account and jurisdiction.

Example:
A high-quality community dataset could be H7 but support HIGH certainty about a
historical event if it contains immutable raw records independently matched to
official outcomes.

8.5 DECISION RULE
-----------------
A recommendation must state:
- claim certainty;
- decision criticality;
- residual risk;
- fallback;
- reversal trigger.

A HIGH certainty claim can still lead to a poor recommendation if the preference
or trade-off is wrong. Evidence certainty is not decision desirability.

======================================================================
9. RESEARCH DEPTH LEVELS
======================================================================

9.1 R0 — DISCOVERY
------------------
Purpose:
Find candidate sources or vocabulary.

Requirements:
- query recorded;
- no production claim supported;
- snippets not cited as evidence.

Use:
Initial exploration only.

9.2 R1 — TARGETED VERIFICATION
------------------------------
Purpose:
Verify a narrow operational fact.

Examples:
- current API parameter;
- publication date;
- exact FPL rule;
- package function signature.

Requirements:
- primary source opened;
- exact locator;
- access date;
- version;
- one corroborating live test where operational;
- register entry.

9.3 R2 — STRUCTURED MODULE REVIEW
---------------------------------
Default for a normal module design question.

Requirements:
- written question and scope;
- alternatives listed;
- search log;
- primary and academic sources;
- inclusion/exclusion rationale;
- source and claim register;
- critical appraisal;
- contrary-evidence search;
- synthesis;
- selected approach;
- empirical validation plan;
- refresh triggers.

9.4 R3 — CRITICAL ARCHITECTURE REVIEW
-------------------------------------
Required for:
- provider selection;
- data rights;
- scoring interpretation;
- odds normalisation policy;
- minutes model family;
- optimisation objective;
- chip strategy;
- rank utility;
- backtesting methodology.

Adds:
- second human review;
- independent evidence chain;
- source-bundle hash;
- explicit sensitivity;
- predeclared stopping rule;
- proof-of-access or executable prototype;
- formal ADR;
- no unresolved blocking issue.

9.5 R4 — SYSTEMATIC OR SCOPING REVIEW
-------------------------------------
Rare. Used when:
- the evidence base is broad and disputed;
- publication-quality synthesis is intended;
- a major scientific claim requires exhaustive coverage.

Requirements:
- protocol;
- database-specific searches;
- complete eligibility criteria;
- duplicate screening where feasible;
- flow diagram;
- formal appraisal tool where appropriate;
- PRISMA/PRISMA-S-aligned reporting.

DMF Pulse MUST NOT label an ordinary ChatGPT web search a systematic review.

10. RESEARCH PROTOCOL
======================================================================

10.1 PROTOCOL ID
----------------
Every R2/R3/R4 review receives:

RP-{module}-{number}-{version}

Example:
RP-06-001-v1 — Compare de-vigging methods for multi-outcome football markets.

10.2 REQUIRED PROTOCOL FIELDS
-----------------------------
- protocol ID;
- module;
- owner;
- review depth;
- date started;
- decision deadline;
- research question;
- decision that depends on it;
- claim types;
- population/context;
- methods/interventions/options;
- comparators;
- outcomes;
- time period;
- jurisdiction;
- target season/provider/software version;
- source classes;
- search locations;
- planned queries;
- inclusion criteria;
- exclusion criteria;
- quality appraisal;
- synthesis method;
- stopping rule;
- update trigger;
- conflicts of interest;
- deviations.

10.3 QUESTION FORMULATION
-------------------------
The question should be decision-ready.

Weak:
"What is the best odds API?"

Strong:
"For a UK-based private FPL decision engine operating before 2026/27 deadlines,
which legally accessible provider or provider combination supplies current and
historical EPL team and player markets with sufficient timestamp precision,
coverage, storage rights, reliability and cost for market-to-FPL projection and
time-safe backtesting?"

10.4 ALTERNATIVES BEFORE SEARCH
-------------------------------
List plausible choices before searching.

This prevents the research from becoming a justification for the first provider
or model discovered.

10.5 OUTCOMES
-------------
Outcomes should include more than predictive performance.

Examples:
- calibration;
- log score;
- coverage;
- historical depth;
- latency;
- missingness;
- reproducibility;
- complexity;
- cost;
- licence;
- maintenance;
- failure behaviour;
- decision value.

10.6 STOPPING RULE
------------------
The review must define when searching ends.

R2 example:
- controlling official sources found;
- at least two independent relevant research chains checked;
- contrary-evidence search completed;
- saturation reached across two additional query rounds;
- no unresolved CORE contradiction.

R3 example:
- all R2 criteria;
- proof-of-access;
- licence reviewed;
- executable comparison;
- human verification;
- fallback identified.

Stopping because a preferred answer was found is prohibited.

10.7 PROTOCOL DEVIATIONS
------------------------
Any material deviation is logged:
- what changed;
- why;
- date;
- impact;
- approver.

11. SEARCH STRATEGY AND LOGGING
======================================================================

11.1 SEARCH ORDER
-----------------
For each question:

1. Identify controlling official source.
2. Search official technical documentation and changelogs.
3. Search academic databases and DOI-indexed literature.
4. Search references in high-quality reviews/papers.
5. Search papers that cite key sources.
6. Search reproducible practitioner work.
7. Search community sources for edge cases and leads.
8. Search explicitly for failures, criticism and contrary evidence.
9. Verify current status, versions, corrections and retractions.

11.2 SEARCH LOCATIONS
---------------------
As relevant:
- official FPL/Premier League domains;
- official provider domains;
- Crossref/DOI metadata;
- Google Scholar or comparable academic search;
- arXiv;
- publisher databases;
- university repositories;
- standards organisations;
- government legislation/guidance;
- GitHub or official code repository;
- package documentation;
- practitioner sites;
- FPL community sites.

Access to a database does not imply permission to bulk harvest it.

11.3 QUERY LOG
--------------
Record:
- query ID;
- exact query string;
- search system;
- filters;
- date/time;
- result count where available;
- pages inspected;
- sources retained;
- notes;
- researcher/agent.

Queries should be reproducible and free of hidden personalisation where
possible.

11.4 QUERY DESIGN
-----------------
Use combinations of:
- exact product/method name;
- synonyms;
- version/year;
- population/context;
- outcome metric;
- "validation";
- "calibration";
- "replication";
- "limitations";
- "failure";
- "bias";
- "benchmark";
- "terms";
- "licence";
- "historical";
- "changelog";
- "deprecated".

11.5 DISCONFIRMATION QUERIES
----------------------------
At least one explicit contrary query for every CORE/BLOCKING question.

Examples:
- "[method] limitations football odds";
- "[provider] missing player props";
- "[API] deprecated";
- "[strategy] no effect";
- "[paper title] critique";
- "[DOI] correction retraction";
- "[claim] replication failure".

11.6 LATERAL READING
--------------------
Do not judge an unfamiliar source only by its own presentation.

Investigate:
- who operates it;
- funding and incentives;
- external coverage;
- original source;
- author expertise;
- correction history;
- domain reputation;
- whether content is copied.

11.7 FORWARD AND BACKWARD SEARCHING
-----------------------------------
For important papers:
- inspect references;
- inspect later citing work;
- check corrections;
- check newer versions;
- locate code/data;
- identify independent replications.

11.8 SEARCH EXHAUSTIVENESS
--------------------------
The search log must state:
- databases not accessible;
- paywalled sources not obtained;
- language limits;
- date limits;
- missing provider documents;
- incomplete grey literature.

======================================================================
12. SOURCE RETRIEVAL AND VERIFICATION
======================================================================

12.1 OPEN THE SOURCE
--------------------
A source is eligible only when its relevant content has been opened and
inspected.

Exceptions:
- inaccessible source may be registered as NOT_RETRIEVED;
- it cannot carry a material claim unless independently verified elsewhere.

12.2 METADATA CHECK
-------------------
Verify:
- exact title;
- author(s);
- organisation;
- publisher;
- publication date;
- last updated date;
- version;
- DOI or persistent ID;
- canonical URL;
- source type;
- peer-review status;
- correction/retraction status.

12.3 CONTENT CHECK
------------------
Verify:
- exact claim;
- qualifiers;
- denominator;
- time period;
- jurisdiction;
- population;
- outcome definition;
- method;
- limitations;
- whether the claim is measured, inferred or promotional.

12.4 LOCATOR
------------
Record one or more:
- page number;
- section heading;
- paragraph;
- table/figure;
- equation;
- line range;
- API endpoint;
- JSON path;
- code file/line;
- Git commit;
- contract clause;
- timestamp in audio/video.

12.5 PDF AND VISUAL SOURCES
---------------------------
For a material PDF:
- inspect parsed text;
- visually inspect the relevant page;
- record page number;
- verify charts/tables in context;
- avoid OCR unless necessary;
- preserve a permitted copy or hash.

12.6 ABSTRACT-ONLY EVIDENCE
---------------------------
An abstract may support only claims explicitly contained in the abstract.

It cannot support:
- detailed methodology;
- subgroup result;
- limitation;
- exact implementation;
- licence;
- table value not shown.

12.7 PAPER STATUS CHECK
-----------------------
For important research:
- resolve DOI;
- inspect publisher page;
- check Crossmark or update metadata where available;
- check correction/retraction notices;
- check preprint version history;
- record version of record;
- record whether code/data remain available.

Crossmark improves visibility of updates but its presence is not a guarantee.

12.8 API DOCUMENT STATUS
------------------------
Record:
- documentation version;
- API version;
- changelog date;
- deprecation warning;
- status page;
- test date;
- account/plan;
- jurisdiction;
- response hash or test fixture.

12.9 ARCHIVING
--------------
Where permitted, preserve:
- downloaded document;
- raw HTML/PDF;
- screenshot;
- JSON response;
- repository archive;
- terms version;
- SHA-256 hash.

If preservation is forbidden:
- preserve metadata, locator and permitted derived notes;
- record the restriction;
- do not circumvent it.

======================================================================
13. SCREENING AND ELIGIBILITY
======================================================================

13.1 INCLUSION CRITERIA
-----------------------
A source may be included when it:
- addresses an atomic claim;
- is retrievable;
- has identifiable provenance;
- is sufficiently current or historically relevant;
- has inspectable methods/content;
- contributes support, contradiction or limitation;
- can be lawfully consulted.

13.2 EXCLUSION CRITERIA
-----------------------
Exclude as supporting evidence when:
- origin is unknown;
- content is AI-generated without primary sources;
- only a search snippet is available;
- it duplicates another source without added evidence;
- it is out of scope;
- it uses future information for a pre-deadline claim;
- it is withdrawn/retracted for the relevant reason;
- it has a fatal methodological flaw;
- the source cannot be interpreted reliably;
- it is promotional with no evidence and stronger direct evidence exists.

Retain an exclusion record for important candidates.

13.3 DUPLICATES
---------------
Duplicates include:
- same paper on publisher and preprint server;
- syndicated press release;
- copied provider documentation;
- articles summarising one study.

Choose a canonical record and link alternate locations.

13.4 LANGUAGE
-------------
English is the default operational language. Relevant non-English controlling
sources should be included and translated with:
- original text locator;
- translation method;
- human check for BLOCKING claims.

13.5 PAYWALLS
-------------
A paywall is not a quality signal.

If important:
- seek author manuscript;
- use institutional access;
- contact author;
- purchase if justified;
- record that full text was unavailable.

Do not infer unavailable details.

14. EVIDENCE EXTRACTION
======================================================================

14.1 EXTRACTION RULE
--------------------
Extract only what is needed for the claim and decision.

14.2 REQUIRED EXTRACTION FIELDS
-------------------------------
- source ID;
- claim ID;
- exact source locator;
- source statement or careful paraphrase;
- extracted quantitative value and unit;
- conditions/qualifiers;
- population/sample;
- time period;
- method;
- comparator;
- uncertainty;
- limitations;
- conflicts/funding;
- applicability to Pulse;
- researcher notes;
- extraction date;
- extractor;
- verification state.

14.3 QUOTATION
--------------
Use minimal quotation:
- exact legal/rule wording where necessary;
- short technical definition;
- avoid long copyrighted passages;
- preserve quotation marks and locator;
- otherwise paraphrase accurately.

14.4 NUMERIC EXTRACTION
-----------------------
For every number record:
- numerator;
- denominator;
- units;
- interval;
- metric definition;
- sample;
- rounding;
- whether read from text, table or chart;
- any calculation performed;
- calculation code where material.

14.5 ASSUMPTIONS DURING EXTRACTION
----------------------------------
Do not silently:
- convert odds formats;
- infer missing units;
- equate correlation with causation;
- infer market availability from a generic list;
- assume endpoint stability;
- generalise from another league;
- treat "up to" as typical performance;
- treat marketing latency as measured latency.

14.6 DOUBLE EXTRACTION
----------------------
For BLOCKING R3 claims, a human should independently verify the extraction.

======================================================================
15. AI-ASSISTED RESEARCH CONTROL
======================================================================

15.1 PERMITTED AI USE
---------------------
ChatGPT may:
- broaden queries;
- translate terminology;
- classify sources;
- extract structured metadata;
- compare methods;
- identify gaps;
- draft evidence tables;
- propose contrary queries;
- write the specification.

15.2 PROHIBITED AI USE
----------------------
ChatGPT MUST NOT:
- invent a source;
- cite its own answer;
- rely on memory for current rules/APIs;
- fill a missing number with a plausible estimate;
- claim exhaustive review without protocol;
- infer licence rights;
- conceal uncertainty;
- create a fake quotation;
- cite a source it did not inspect for a material claim;
- treat search snippets as evidence;
- make legal conclusions beyond the source and review level.

15.3 AI SESSION RECORD
----------------------
For R3 reviews, record:
- model/product used;
- date;
- user research request;
- major search prompts or query log;
- source bundle;
- output file version;
- human reviewer;
- known limitations.

Private chain-of-thought is not required. Store the reproducible research plan,
queries, evidence, concise rationale and decisions instead.

15.4 CITATION VERIFICATION
--------------------------
Every critical AI-proposed citation is checked for:
- existence;
- correct authors/title;
- correct version;
- source supports claim;
- locator;
- status;
- applicability.

15.5 AI SUMMARISATION RISK
--------------------------
A summary can erase qualifiers.

The extractor must preserve:
- "may";
- "under these conditions";
- sample limitations;
- uncertainty;
- conditional definitions;
- negative findings;
- exclusions.

15.6 NIST-ALIGNED CONTROLS
--------------------------
NIST's Generative AI Profile identifies confabulation and recommends reviewing
and verifying sources/citations and tracking provenance. DMF Pulse operationalises
that guidance through:
- mandatory source verification;
- AI-output status labels;
- provenance records;
- human review;
- no AI evidence class;
- model/version logging where material.

15.7 OPENAI RESEARCH TOOLS
--------------------------
OpenAI research tools can synthesise many sources and provide citations. Their
output remains a research report, not self-validating evidence. The cited
sources, activity record and limitations must still be checked under this
standard.

16. SYNTHESIS
======================================================================

16.1 SYNTHESIS OUTPUT
---------------------
Every R2/R3 review produces:

1. research question;
2. sources searched;
3. included/excluded evidence;
4. claim table;
5. evidence quality;
6. conflicts;
7. findings;
8. limitations;
9. selected approach;
10. alternatives rejected;
11. empirical validation plan;
12. residual uncertainty;
13. reversal conditions;
14. refresh schedule.

16.2 FACT / INFERENCE / RECOMMENDATION LABELS
---------------------------------------------
Use explicit labels.

FACT:
"Provider documentation lists endpoint X."

OBSERVATION:
"Test account returned 72 player selections at 14:00 UTC."

INFERENCE:
"Coverage may be insufficient before line-ups."

RECOMMENDATION:
"Use a secondary source or fallback model."

ASSUMPTION:
"Until a longer sample exists, assume the observed missingness range persists."

16.3 NARRATIVE SYNTHESIS
------------------------
When evidence is heterogeneous:
- group by claim;
- compare directness and methods;
- explain disagreement;
- avoid vote counting;
- avoid treating the majority as automatically correct;
- state which evidence carries more weight and why.

16.4 QUANTITATIVE SYNTHESIS
---------------------------
Meta-analysis or pooled metrics require:
- compatible definitions;
- comparable information sets;
- comparable outcome units;
- independence assessment;
- heterogeneity;
- uncertainty;
- documented model choice.

Do not pool incomparable backtests.

16.5 ABSENCE OF EVIDENCE
------------------------
Distinguish:
- evidence of absence;
- absence of evidence;
- inaccessible evidence;
- not searched;
- not measurable.

Example:
"No assist market was observed in ten sample fixtures" is not the same as
"Provider never offers assist markets."

16.6 EVIDENCE CONFLICT
----------------------
When sources conflict:
1. confirm they address the same claim;
2. check date/version;
3. identify definitions;
4. inspect primary data;
5. assess independence;
6. assess methodology;
7. identify rule/context changes;
8. retain both;
9. reduce certainty;
10. define a test or escalation.

16.7 SOURCE INDEPENDENCE GRAPH
------------------------------
For critical claims, record common origins.

Example:
- News A -> Provider press release
- Blog B -> News A
- Forum C -> Blog B

This is one chain.

Independent:
- provider docs;
- DMF live test;
- independent benchmark.

16.8 RESEARCHER JUDGEMENT
-------------------------
Judgement is unavoidable. It must be visible.

Record:
- why one source was weighted more;
- why evidence was indirect;
- why an alternative was rejected;
- how uncertainty affected the decision.

17. LICENSING, COPYRIGHT, DATABASE RIGHTS AND TERMS
======================================================================

17.1 PRINCIPLE
--------------
Citation permission and production permission are different.

A source may be lawful to read and cite while its data may not be lawful to:
- scrape;
- store;
- transform;
- redistribute;
- display;
- train on;
- use commercially.

17.2 REQUIRED RIGHTS FIELDS
---------------------------
For data, API, code and substantial content, record:

- rights holder;
- governing contract/terms URL;
- contract version/date;
- copyright status;
- database-right status;
- software licence;
- data licence;
- access right;
- automated-access right;
- raw-storage right;
- retention period;
- backup right;
- transformation/derivative right;
- model-training right;
- internal-use right;
- public-display right;
- commercial-use right;
- redistribution right;
- attribution requirement;
- share-alike requirement;
- noncommercial restriction;
- no-derivatives restriction;
- geographic restriction;
- user/account restriction;
- termination effect;
- deletion obligation;
- audit requirement;
- legal-review status.

17.3 LICENSING TRAFFIC LIGHT
----------------------------
GREEN:
Rights are explicit and compatible with the planned use.

AMBER:
Rights are limited, ambiguous, plan-dependent or require written clarification.
May be used only in the permitted provisional context.

RED:
Use is prohibited or materially incompatible.

UNKNOWN:
Not yet assessed. Treated as AMBER/BLOCKED for production.

17.4 OPEN LICENCES
------------------
Record exact licence and version.

Creative Commons examples:
- CC BY permits adaptation and commercial use with attribution.
- CC BY-SA adds share-alike.
- CC BY-ND prohibits adaptations.
- CC BY-NC restricts to noncommercial use.
- combinations retain their conditions.

Do not write only "Creative Commons."

17.5 SOFTWARE LICENCES
----------------------
Use standard SPDX identifiers where possible.

Record:
- direct dependencies;
- transitive risk where material;
- copyleft obligations;
- attribution;
- patent terms;
- compatibility with distribution.

17.6 UK DATABASE RIGHTS
-----------------------
UK guidance recognises copyright in original selection/arrangement and separate
database rights where substantial investment exists. Public availability does
not remove these rights.

DMF Pulse must not assume:
- an API response is free to republish;
- factual data has no database protection;
- a public endpoint grants bulk storage rights.

17.7 GOVERNMENT / OPEN DATA
---------------------------
Record exact open-government licence and attribution requirements. Do not infer
that all government content has the same terms.

17.8 LEGAL ESCALATION
---------------------
Escalate when:
- raw odds will be retained;
- data will be displayed publicly;
- product monetisation begins;
- terms prohibit benchmarking;
- derived-data ownership is unclear;
- provider termination affects stored data;
- jurisdiction conflicts;
- substantial database extraction is planned.

The evidence register records operational status; only qualified legal advice
resolves material ambiguity.

Prefer:
- DOI;
- SWHID;
- repository release;
- Git commit;
- official version number;
- canonical URL.

DataCite metadata concepts inform consistent title, creator, publisher, date,
resource type, related identifier and rights fields.

18.6 HASHES
-----------
Hashes establish file identity, not truth.

Use SHA-256 for:
- downloaded source files;
- API test fixtures;
- search logs;
- evidence register releases;
- source bundles;
- specifications.

22. CORRECTIONS, RETRACTIONS, VERSIONS AND SUPERSESSION

22.1 CHECKS
-----------

- publisher status;

- correction/erratum;

22.2 CROSSMARK

22.3 PREPRINTS
--------------
Record:
- version number;
- submission/revision date;
- later journal publication;
- withdrawal status;
- material changes;

22.4 SOFTWARE
-------------

- package version;
- documentation version;
- release date;
- deprecations;
- security advisory;
- Git commit for source inspection.

22.5 WEB PAGES
--------------
Record:
- access date;
- page update date if present;
- snapshot/hash if permitted;
- material changes on reverification.

22.6 SUPERSESSION
-----------------
Never delete a superseded source or claim. Link it to the replacement so the
decision history remains intelligible.

23. FRESHNESS AND REVERIFICATION
======================================================================

23.1 DEFAULT MAXIMUM AGES
-------------------------
These are maximums, not guarantees.

Official FPL rules and scoring:
- verify at game launch;
- verify after every official rule announcement;
- verify before production sign-off.

Deadlines and fixtures:
- ingest continuously;
- verify before every decision run.

FPL API schema/endpoints:
- automated contract test daily in season;
- manual documentation review monthly or on failure.

Odds provider market coverage:
- automated daily sampling;
- human review monthly;
- immediate review on missingness or contract change.

Provider pricing, terms and licence:
- verify before purchase/renewal;
- at least every 30 days during selection;
- quarterly after contract;
- immediately on notice/change.

Software documentation:
- tied to pinned version;
- review on upgrade.

Research methods:
- review annually or before major model redesign;
- alert for correction/retraction.

Recent preprints:
- check every 90 days until published, withdrawn or no longer relied on.

Community observations:
- expire after 14 days unless verified.

News/injury/status:
- timestamp at ingestion;
- short operational expiry defined by module.

23.2 EVENT-BASED TRIGGERS
-------------------------
- FPL launch;
- rule update;
- provider changelog;
- API error/schema change;
- price-plan change;
- contract renewal;
- source correction/retraction;
- new paper version;
- model failure;
- prospective calibration drift;
- new season;
- public/commercial product launch.

23.3 STALENESS STATES
---------------------
CURRENT
DUE
STALE
EXPIRED
SUPERSEDED

A BLOCKING stale claim prevents production use.

24. CITATION AND WRITING STANDARD
======================================================================

24.1 INLINE SUPPORT
-------------------
Every material external fact in a specification should have an adjacent citation
or source ID.

24.2 SOURCE ID AND FULL RECORD
------------------------------
Human-facing specification:
[SRC-RES-2016-001]

Register:
Full metadata, URL, locator, assessment and licence.

24.3 URL RULES
--------------
- store canonical URL without tracking parameters;
- store DOI when available;
- retain alternate URL separately;
- do not use a search-results URL as canonical.

24.4 DATES
----------
Use ISO 8601:
YYYY-MM-DD

Unknown:
UNKNOWN

Year-only:
2016

Do not invent a precise date.

24.5 AUTHORS
------------
Preserve author order.
Use organisation for institutional authors.
Record ORCID where useful.

24.6 CLAIM LANGUAGE
-------------------
Use calibrated language:
- establishes;
- supports;
- suggests;
- is consistent with;
- does not establish;
- remains uncertain;
- provider documents;
- DMF observed.

Avoid:
- proves, unless formally justified;
- always;
- guaranteed;
- unquestionably;
- industry standard, without a standard source;
- best practice, without defining whose practice and evidence.

24.7 QUOTING
------------
Use short quotations only where wording matters. Long copyrighted content is not
copied into specifications.

24.8 INTERNAL SOURCES
---------------------
Internal experiments receive source IDs and full provenance:
- code commit;
- data bundle;
- protocol;
- output;
- reviewer.

They are not lower quality merely because unpublished, but their independence
and replication status are explicit.

26.3 LINK CHECKING
------------------
Automated link checking MAY:
- confirm HTTP status;
- record redirects;
- flag changed content hash;
- check DOI resolution.

It MUST NOT equate HTTP 200 with source validity.

26.4 RETRACTION/UPDATE CHECK
----------------------------
Where metadata permits:
- query Crossref/Crossmark;
- flag update/retraction relation;
- flag arXiv version change;
- require human review.

26.5 LICENCE CHECK
------------------
Use SPDX identifiers for software when possible.
Do not automatically infer a data licence from a software repository licence.

26.6 COVERAGE DASHBOARD

- claims by certainty;
- blocking claims unverified;
- stale sources;
- sources by hierarchy;
- decisions with weak evidence;
- licensing status;
- source independence count;
- human verification backlog.

These metrics indicate governance status, not truth.

29. EXAMPLE APPLICATIONS
======================================================================

29.1 OFFICIAL FPL SCORING
-------------------------
Question:
What are the 2026/27 clean-sheet and defensive-contribution points?

Evidence:
- official target-season rules;
- official announcement;
- live scoring test after launch.

Not sufficient:
- 2025/26 article;
- FPL community graphic;
- ChatGPT memory.

Decision:
Do not sign season configuration until official rules and a representative test
agree.

29.2 ODDS API SELECTION
-----------------------
Question:
Can Provider X support time-safe EPL player projections?

Evidence:
- contract;
- API docs;
- market list;
- test account;
- historical sample;
- missingness benchmark;
- latency;
- licence;
- support clarification.

Not sufficient:
- "supports soccer player props" on a marketing page.

29.3 DE-VIGGING METHOD
----------------------
Question:
Which method best converts provider prices for each market type?

Evidence:
- methods literature;
- reproducible implementations;
- historical time-safe dataset;
- out-of-time log score/calibration;
- sensitivity by bookmaker and market.

Decision:
Select by market type; retain alternative methods; continue prospective
monitoring.

29.4 PRICE-CHANGE MODEL
-----------------------
Question:
Can transfer activity predict the next official price change?

Evidence:
- time-stamped FPL transfer and price data;
- reproducible walk-forward benchmark;
- community hypotheses as features only;
- prospective test.

Not sufficient:
- undocumented threshold claims repeated across forums.

29.5 EARLY-SEASON UNCERTAINTY
-----------------------------
Question:
How strongly should new observations update priors?

Evidence:
- hierarchical/Bayesian methodology;
- multi-season FPL/football validation;
- changed-manager and promoted-team subgroups;
- calibration;
- decision simulation.

Decision:
Define a model and test; do not translate generic advice into fixed shrinkage
without evidence.

32. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA FOR DMFP-01
======================================================================

DMFP-01 is accepted when Sebastian agrees that:

A. Evidence model
[ ] Sources, claims and decisions are separate linked objects.
[ ] No universal source ladder is applied mechanically.
[ ] Claim certainty is separate from source prestige.
[ ] Criticality controls required review depth.

B. AI controls
[ ] ChatGPT is a research agent, not a source.
[ ] Search snippets cannot support claims.
[ ] Critical citations require human verification.
[ ] Missing evidence is reported, not invented.

C. Research method
[ ] R1–R4 review levels are practical.
[ ] R2 is the default for normal modules.
[ ] R3 is required for critical dependencies and architecture.
[ ] Searches include contrary evidence and a stopping rule.

D. Licensing
[ ] Rights are assessed separately for access, storage, transformation,
    publication and commercial use.
[ ] Unknown rights block production reliance.
[ ] Specialist legal review is triggered where material.

E. Reproducibility
[ ] Queries, sources, versions, locators and hashes are retained.
[ ] Internal tests are registered as evidence.
[ ] Source bundles link research to specifications and code.

F. Updating
[ ] Every critical claim has a date or event trigger.
[ ] Corrections, retractions and supersession remain visible.
[ ] Stale blocking evidence stops production.

G. Implementation
[ ] Codex will implement schemas and validation.
[ ] Codex cannot upgrade confidence or infer legal rights.
[ ] No accepted architecture decision can contain an unresolved blocking claim.
