PURPOSE
-------
This document defines the shared language, identities, relationships and
temporal semantics of the entire DMF Pulse system.

Its job is to ensure that:

- "Mohamed Salah" in FPL;
- "M. Salah" in a bookmaker market;
- "Salah, Mohamed" in a sports-data feed;
- an exchange runner;
- a historical projection;
- a squad member;
- a price snapshot;
- a transfer decision;

can all be linked to the same real-world player without using a name as the
identity.

It also ensures that:

- a player transfer between clubs does not create a new player;
- two players with the same name do not collapse into one;
- a postponed match remains the same fixture when its kickoff moves;
- a fixture changing FPL Gameweek does not become a new fixture;
- a Double Gameweek is represented by fixture membership, not special-case code;
- provider market IDs remain scoped to their provider and market;
- historical FPL player IDs can change without breaking longitudinal data;
- every odds quote remains tied to the correct market, selection, source and
  observation time;
- every projection and decision can be reconstructed from the information
  actually available at the time.

The governing principle is:

    REAL-WORLD IDENTITY, PROVIDER IDENTITY, OBSERVATION AND MODEL OUTPUT
    ARE DIFFERENT THINGS AND MUST NEVER BE COLLAPSED INTO ONE TABLE OR KEY.

IMPORTANT TARGET-SEASON NOTE
----------------------------
The 2026/27 FPL game had not launched at the research cut-off.

Consequently:

1. Current FPL endpoint structures may be used as REFERENCE interfaces only.
2. Target-season FPL external IDs, positions, teams, Gameweeks and fixtures MUST
   be captured after launch.
3. FPL numeric IDs MUST be treated as provider- and season-scoped unless an
   official guarantee establishes a broader scope.
4. No FPL `element.id`, `team.id`, `event.id`, `fixture.id` or undocumented
   `code` field may become the DMF canonical identity.
5. The canonical model must survive a complete renumbering of the FPL catalogue
   between seasons.
6. Provider contracts and licensing determine which raw identifiers, payloads
   and mappings may be retained.

------------------
MUST / MUST NOT:
A non-negotiable requirement.

SHOULD / SHOULD NOT:

MAY:
Optional, provided no MUST requirement is violated.

0. EXECUTIVE CONTROL SUMMARY
======================================================================

0.1 THE DATA MODEL IN ONE SENTENCE
----------------------------------
DMF Pulse uses immutable internal UUID identities, explicit provider mappings,
bitemporal facts and append-only observations so that every player, fixture,
market, projection and decision remains unambiguous across providers, seasons
and corrections.

0.2 CONTROLLING ARCHITECTURAL DECISIONS
---------------------------------------
DD-03-01. Canonical IDs are internal.
Pulse generates and owns the canonical identifier for every core entity.
External IDs are attributes in mapping tables, never primary keys for the domain.

DD-03-02. Use UUIDv7 for new internal entity and event IDs.
RFC 9562 UUIDv7 provides 128-bit identifiers with a Unix-epoch time-ordered
field and no central registration requirement. Pulse will use one approved
implementation and PostgreSQL's native UUID type.

Caveats:
- UUID creation time is not business event time;
- do not infer ordering solely from UUID;
- do not expose time-ordered IDs publicly where this creates unwanted
  information leakage;
- external/public aliases may be separate opaque IDs.

DD-03-03. Identity is not a name.
Names, abbreviations, shirt names, transliterations and spelling variants are
aliases. They are never sufficient alone to merge canonical entities.

DD-03-04. Identity is not current team membership.
A player remains one player across transfers. Membership is an effective-dated
relationship between player and team.

DD-03-05. Identity is not a timestamp.
A fixture remains one fixture when kickoff time changes. Schedule details live
in versioned fixture revisions.

DD-03-06. FPL Gameweek is not calendar week.
A Gameweek is an FPL scoring period with an official deadline and explicit
fixture memberships. Membership can change. It must never be inferred solely
from date.

DD-03-07. Provider IDs are namespaced and temporal.
An external identifier is unique only inside its declared provider, product,
entity type, identifier namespace and, where necessary, effective interval.

DD-03-08. Market and selection identity are composite in semantics.
A market is tied to:
- operator;
- fixture;
- market definition;
- period;
- line/handicap;
- settlement profile;
- provider representation.

A selection is tied to its market and outcome semantics. An exchange runner
cannot be identified safely by `selectionId` alone.

DD-03-09. Raw observations are append-only.
Odds, prices, lineups, availability reports, projections and decisions are not
updated in place. New information creates a new observation or run.

DD-03-10. Mutable facts are bitemporal where material.
Pulse records:
- valid time: when the fact was true in the football/FPL world;
- system time: when Pulse knew or stored that version.

DD-03-11. Closed-open time intervals.
Effective ranges use:
    [valid_from, valid_until)
This allows adjacent facts to meet without overlap ambiguity.

DD-03-12. Canonical relational core, raw payload outside it.
Core searchable semantics use typed relational columns. Raw provider payloads
are preserved in licensed object storage with hashes and may be referenced by
JSONB metadata. JSONB is not a substitute for modelling core relationships.

DD-03-13. Provider and operator are different.
A provider is the technical feed/vendor through which data arrives.
A bookmaker or exchange is the economic market operator that originated or
hosts the price.

Example:
The Odds API may be the provider; a nested bookmaker is the operator.

DD-03-14. Observation and estimate are different.
An official injury flag, a journalist report and Pulse's probability of
availability are separate records.

DD-03-15. Projection identity is run-scoped.
A projection is identified by:
- projection run;
- entity;
- target fixture/Gameweek/horizon;
- metric;
- model version;
- ruleset;
- information cutoff.

DD-03-16. Decision records are immutable.
A revised recommendation creates a new decision run. The previous
recommendation remains queryable exactly as issued.

DD-03-17. Merges do not erase history.
If two canonical entities are discovered to be duplicates, one is superseded by
a survivor through an explicit merge record. Historical provenance is retained.

DD-03-18. Unknown is not null-by-accident.
Unknown, not applicable, not offered, not observed, unavailable and zero are
different states and require explicit semantics.

DD-03-19. Identity resolution uncertainty is stored.
Provider mappings have:
- method;
- confidence;
- evidence;
- reviewer;
- status;
- effective interval.

DD-03-20. No downstream module rematches names independently.
All modules consume canonical IDs or submit unresolved records to the central
entity-resolution workflow.

0.4 PRODUCTION SUCCESS CONDITION
--------------------------------
The canonical model is successful when any downstream record can answer:

- which real entity does this concern?
- which provider record did it come from?
- which version of that mapping was active?
- when was the fact true?
- when did Pulse know it?
- what raw source supports it?
- which season, Gameweek and fixture are involved?
- which model, ruleset and code version produced it?
- whether the mapping was certain, probabilistic or manually approved?
- whether later corrections altered the current view without rewriting history?

2. ONTOLOGY AND NAMING PRINCIPLES
======================================================================

2.1 ENTITY
----------
A thing with continuing identity.

Examples:
- player;
- club;
- fixture;
- market;
- model version.

2.2 RELATIONSHIP
----------------
A time-scoped connection between entities.

Examples:
- player registered to team;
- fixture assigned to Gameweek;
- selection refers to player;
- model version derived from dataset;
- decision uses projection run.

2.3 OBSERVATION
---------------
A source reports a value at a point in time.

Examples:
- bookmaker odds;
- FPL price;
- official availability flag;
- published lineup;
- team membership report.

2.4 STATE
---------
A derived or authoritative view valid for a period.

Examples:
- current player membership;
- aggregated availability state;
- current fixture schedule;
- current manager squad.

2.5 EVENT
---------
Something that changes state.

Examples:
- player transfer;
- fixture postponement;
- price rise;
- chip activation;
- decision approval.

2.6 RUN
-------
An immutable computational activity with fixed inputs and versioned code.

Examples:
- projection run;
- entity-resolution run;
- optimiser run;
- backtest run.

2.7 DEFINITION VERSUS INSTANCE
------------------------------
MarketDefinition:
The canonical concept "player anytime goalscorer, full time."

Market:
One operator's actual offered instance for one fixture.

ChipDefinition:
The rules/configured capability "Free Hit."

ChipInventory:
The manager's available copy.

ModelFamily:
Concept "minutes model."

ModelVersion:
Immutable trained/released artefact.

2.8 CURRENT VIEW VERSUS HISTORY
-------------------------------
A current view is a query over historical records. It is not a mutable row that
destroys previous truth.

Examples:
- `current_player_team`;
- `current_fixture_schedule`;
- `current_external_mapping`;
- `latest_price`;
- `latest_projection_before_deadline`.

2.9 CONTROLLED VOCABULARIES
---------------------------
Use explicit enums or reference tables for:

- entity type;
- provider type;
- operator type;
- fixture status;
- market scope;
- market period;
- selection outcome;
- availability state;
- mapping status;
- mapping method;
- projection metric;
- decision action;
- lifecycle status.

Do not use free text when the values control logic.

2.10 HUMAN LABELS
-----------------
Every entity may have:
- canonical display name;
- short display name;
- aliases;
- language;
- script;
- valid interval;
- source.

Labels are presentation metadata, not identity.

======================================================================
3. INTERNAL IDENTIFIER STRATEGY
======================================================================

3.1 UUID STANDARD
-----------------
Use RFC 9562 UUIDs.

Default:
UUIDv7 for new internal IDs.

Reasons:
- distributed generation;
- no central sequence service;
- PostgreSQL native UUID type;
- approximate creation-time locality;
- stable across database migrations;
- safe combination of data from multiple environments when generated correctly.

3.2 UUID GENERATION POLICY
--------------------------
- one approved library/implementation;
- cryptographically strong randomness according to implementation;
- application-generated or approved database function;
- generated exactly once;
- never reused;
- lowercase canonical textual representation;
- validate UUID version on insert where appropriate.

3.3 UUID IS NOT BUSINESS TIME
-----------------------------
Store explicit:
- created_at;
- observed_at;
- valid_from;
- kickoff_at;
- deadline_at;
- information_cutoff.

Never derive these from UUID bits for business logic.

3.4 PUBLIC IDENTIFIERS
----------------------
Internal UUIDv7 may reveal approximate creation time.

For future public APIs, Pulse MAY expose:
- random UUIDv4 public ID;
- opaque short alias;
- signed external token.

Public ID maps to internal canonical ID.

3.5 PRIMARY KEY NAMES
---------------------
Typed names:
- season_id;
- gameweek_id;
- team_id;
- player_id;
- fixture_id;
- market_id;
- selection_id;
- model_version_id;
- decision_run_id.

Avoid generic `id` in cross-table interfaces and exports.

3.6 NATURAL KEYS
----------------
Natural keys remain unique constraints where reliable, not primary identity.

Examples:
- Gameweek: season_id + number;
- TeamSeason: season_id + team_id;
- ModelVersion: model_family_id + version;
- one current player-team membership at one time;
- one active fixture assignment at one time.

3.7 ENTITY REGISTRY
-------------------
Recommended supertable:

canonical_entity
- entity_id UUID PRIMARY KEY;
- entity_type;
- created_at;
- lifecycle_status;
- superseded_by_entity_id nullable;
- provenance_source_id;
- notes.

Typed tables share the same key:

player.player_id
  FK -> canonical_entity.entity_id
  CHECK entity_type = PLAYER through trigger/constraint.

Benefits:
- generic aliases and external identifiers;
- generic merge relations;
- provenance graph;
- typed domain tables.

3.8 ENTITY TYPES
----------------
At minimum:
COMPETITION
SEASON
GAMEWEEK
TEAM
PLAYER
FIXTURE
DATA_PROVIDER
BETTING_OPERATOR
MARKET
SELECTION
MODEL_FAMILY
MODEL_VERSION
MANAGER_ACCOUNT
CHIP_DEFINITION

Run/snapshot rows may use UUIDv7 without joining the entity registry unless they
need aliases/external IDs.

3.9 IDENTIFIER NEVER CHANGES
----------------------------
A canonical ID remains stable even if:
- name changes;
- team changes;
- provider changes;
- FPL ID changes;
- fixture time changes;
- market display name changes;
- entity becomes inactive.

4.3 EXTERNAL IDS STORED AS TEXT
-------------------------------
Even apparently numeric IDs are stored as text in the mapping layer.

Reasons:
- leading zeros;
- prefixes;
- decimal-looking IDs such as Betfair market IDs;
- IDs larger than language integer limits;
- mixed formats;
- provider migration.

Typed adapter fields may parse numbers separately if the API contract requires.

4.5 UNIQUE CONSTRAINT
---------------------
A candidate uniqueness constraint:

(provider_id,
 provider_product,
 identifier_namespace,
 entity_type,
 external_id_text,
 valid_during)
WITHOUT OVERLAPS where supported.

Do not assume an external ID cannot be reused unless the provider guarantees it.

4.6 COMPOSITE PROVIDER IDENTITY
-------------------------------
Some provider entities require context.

Betfair runner identity:
- marketId;
- selectionId;
- handicap/line.

The official API requires marketId and selectionId, with handicap for relevant
Asian handicap markets.

Therefore:
- do not map Betfair selectionId globally to a Pulse Selection;
- store a provider runner mapping keyed by market context;
- treat line/handicap as an exact decimal or canonical line value.

4.7 PROVIDER MERGE MAPPINGS
---------------------------
Providers may merge duplicate teams, players or events.

Sportradar explicitly documents:
- competitor mappings;
- player mappings;
- competitor merge mappings;
- player merge mappings.

Pulse must:
- ingest provider merge information;
- preserve old external ID;
- link old mapping to replacement;
- evaluate whether the Pulse canonical entity also needs merging;
- never assume a provider merge is automatically correct for all contexts.

4.8 IDENTIFIER SCOPE
--------------------
Every namespace declares:

scope:
- GLOBAL_PROVIDER;
- PRODUCT;
- SPORT;
- COMPETITION;
- SEASON;
- EVENT;
- MARKET.

FPL element IDs are treated as SEASON scope until proven otherwise.

4.9 MAPPING STATUS
------------------
UNRESOLVED
CANDIDATE
AUTO_MATCHED
HUMAN_VERIFIED
CONFLICTED
REJECTED
SUPERSEDED
EXPIRED

Production use:
- BLOCKING entity mappings require HUMAN_VERIFIED or an approved deterministic

5. TEXT, NAMES AND ALIASES
======================================================================

5.1 PRESERVE RAW TEXT
---------------------
Store the provider's original text exactly, subject to licensing.

Examples:
- "Mohamed Salah";

- "Salah, Mohamed";

--------------------------

Alias types:
OFFICIAL_FULL
OFFICIAL_SHORT
FPL_WEB_NAME
SHIRT_NAME
BOOKMAKER_NAME
TRANSLITERATION
FORMER_NAME
ABBREVIATION
MISSPELLING

5.4 UNICODE NORMALISATION
-------------------------
Use Unicode Normalization Form C (NFC) for canonical stored comparison/display
normalisation.

For matching only, create additional derived keys using:
- Unicode casefold;
- punctuation standardisation;
- whitespace collapse;
- diacritic-insensitive key;
- token reordering where justified;
- optional transliteration.

Never replace raw text with the match key.

Unicode UAX #15 distinguishes canonical and compatibility normalization.
Compatibility normalisation can collapse meaningful distinctions, so NFKC/NFKD
may be used only in a separate candidate-generation feature with testing.

5.5 NAME MATCHING IS NOT IDENTITY
---------------------------------
Exact normalized-name equality can propose candidates.

It cannot auto-merge players when:
- duplicate names exist;
- initials are used;
- team is unknown;
- date of birth conflicts;
- provider market is ambiguous.

5.6 DUPLICATE NAMES
-------------------
Players may share:
- full name;
- surname;
- FPL web name;
- initials.

Disambiguating evidence:
- date of birth;
- current/historical team;
- competition/season;
- nationality;
- position;
- provider IDs;
- fixture participation;
- shirt number as weak temporal evidence;
- market fixture context.

A name-only match receives LOW confidence and remains unresolved.

5.7 TEAM NAMES
--------------
Do not strip:
- "United";
- "City";
- geographical terms;
- legal suffixes;
without preserving full source text.

Team abbreviation is contextual and nonunique.

5.8 PERSON NAME CHANGES
-----------------------
A player may:
- change preferred name;
- use mononym;
- change transliteration;
- have punctuation/diacritics corrected.

Add alias/version. Do not create new player unless identity evidence says so.

6. TEMPORAL AND PROVENANCE MODEL
======================================================================

6.1 FOUR IMPORTANT TIMES
------------------------
EVENT_TIME:
When the football/FPL event occurred.

VALID_TIME:
When a fact was true in the domain.

SOURCE_TIME:
When the provider says the record/quote was generated or updated.

SYSTEM_TIME:
When Pulse ingested or recorded it.

Additionally:
INFORMATION_CUTOFF:
Latest admissible information for a projection/decision/backtest.

6.2 BITEMPORAL FACT
-------------------
Example:
A fixture was scheduled for Saturday as known Monday, then moved to Sunday on
Wednesday.

Valid-time versions describe when the Sunday schedule became official.
System time records when Pulse learned each version.

6.3 CLOSED-OPEN INTERVALS
-------------------------
Use PostgreSQL `tstzrange` or dates with:
    [start, end)

Benefits:
- adjacent intervals do not overlap;
- exact point query is clear;
- exclusion constraints can prevent overlapping current facts.

6.4 REQUIRED BITEMPORAL TABLES
------------------------------
At minimum:
- external_identifier;
- entity_alias where aliases change;
- player_team_membership;
- team name/status;
- fixture_revision;
- fixture_gameweek_assignment;
- official availability state;
- provider market status where corrected;
- manager squad state if reconstructed historically.

6.5 APPEND-ONLY POINT OBSERVATIONS
---------------------------------
Use point timestamps rather than mutable ranges for:
- odds quote;
- price snapshot;
- lineup prediction;
- projection run;
- decision run;
- source snapshot.

Current/latest values are views.

6.6 PROVENANCE
--------------
Adapt W3C PROV concepts:

ENTITY:
- raw payload;
- canonical record;
- projection;
- decision.

ACTIVITY:
- ingestion;
- mapping;
- model run;
- optimiser run;
- backtest.

AGENT:
- provider;
- ChatGPT;
- Codex;
- Sebastian;
- model service.

6.7 SOURCE FIELDS
-----------------
Every ingested/derived record SHOULD carry:
- source_snapshot_id;
- source_bundle_id;
- created_by_activity_id;
- code_commit;
- schema_version;
- raw_payload_hash;
- ingestion_run_id.

6.8 NO DESTRUCTIVE CORRECTION
-----------------------------
Corrections create:
- new record;
- supersession link;
- new system interval;
- issue record;
- affected downstream invalidation.

7.1 COMPETITION
---------------
Represents the enduring competition concept.

Fields:
- competition_id;
- sport;
- canonical_name;
- organiser;
- country;
- competition_type;
- lifecycle_status.

Examples:
- Premier League;

7.2 SEASON
----------
Represents one edition of a competition/game.

Fields:
- season_id;
- competition_id;
- season_code "2026/27";
- start_date;
- end_date;
- official_label;
- ruleset_id;
- lifecycle_status;
- source.

Unique:
competition_id + season_code.

7.3 FPL SEASON VERSUS FOOTBALL SEASON
-------------------------------------
Recommended:
- `football_season` for Premier League competition edition;
- `fpl_season` for FPL game edition;
- explicit one-to-one or many-to-one link.

For MVP they may share a Season domain table with:
season_kind:
FOOTBALL_COMPETITION
FANTASY_GAME

and:
fpl_season.underlying_football_season_id.

This avoids assuming all future FPL rules map exactly to one competition season.

7.4 SEASON IMMUTABILITY
-----------------------
A season identity does not change when:
- dates are updated;
- ruleset is patched;
- provider season ID changes.

Ruleset versions and schedule revisions are separate.

7.5 HISTORICAL SEASONS
----------------------
Historical data MUST retain:
- original ruleset;
- original teams;
- original player-season position;
- original provider IDs;
- source rights.

Do not apply current-season position or club to historical points.

8. GAMEWEEK

8.1 DEFINITION
--------------
A Gameweek is an FPL scoring and decision period, not an ISO calendar week.

Fields:
- gameweek_id;
- fpl_season_id;
- gameweek_number;
- official_name;
- deadline_at_utc;
- lifecycle_status;
- is_current;
- is_next;
- points_finality_status;
- source.

Unique:
fpl_season_id + gameweek_number.

8.2 GAMEWEEK ID SCOPE
---------------------
FPL API `event.id` is stored as an external identifier scoped to FPL season.

The canonical Gameweek UUID persists independently.

8.3 GAMEWEEK STATUS
-------------------
PLANNED
OPEN
DEADLINE_PASSED
IN_PROGRESS
PROVISIONAL_COMPLETE
FINAL
CORRECTED
CANCELLED

8.4 GAMEWEEK AND FIXTURES
-------------------------
Gameweek does not own static fixture columns.

Use:
fixture_gameweek_assignment

A Gameweek can contain:
- zero fixtures for a club;
- one;
- multiple.

8.5 DOUBLE GAMEWEEK
-------------------
A Double Gameweek is a derived condition:

For a team T and Gameweek G:
    count(fixtures assigned to G involving T) >= 2

No separate "double_gameweek" entity is needed for core truth.
A derived materialised view may expose it.

8.6 BLANK GAMEWEEK
------------------
Derived:
    count == 0

8.7 GAMEWEEK DEADLINE HISTORY
-----------------------------
If an official deadline changes:
- append a new Gameweek revision or bitemporal deadline fact;
- preserve old known value;
- decision records retain the deadline known at decision time.

---------------------------

10.5 MULTIPLE MEMBERSHIPS

10.7 HISTORICAL PLAYER IDS

- unresolved records remain separate;
- later merge is explicit.

10.8 PLAYER RETIRES OR LEAVES LEAGUE
------------------------------------
Set lifecycle/season participation.
Never delete.

10.9 DUPLICATE PLAYER NAMES
---------------------------
Two distinct players:
- have distinct canonical UUIDs;
- can share exact aliases;
- external mapping requires context;
- user interfaces display disambiguators such as team, date of birth or position.

11.3 POSTPONEMENT
-----------------
When the same official match is postponed:
- same fixture_id;
- new revision with POSTPONED;
- later new scheduled revision;
- external provider ID mapping may persist or change.

11.4 PROVIDER DELETES AND RECREATES
-----------------------------------
If provider replaces event ID:
- preserve old external mapping;
- add new mapping to same fixture if evidence confirms;
- link provider records through supersession;
- do not create a new canonical fixture only because the external ID changed.

11.5 CANCELLATION/REPLAY
------------------------
If the competition officially treats the later match as a new/replayed fixture:
- create separate Fixture if identity is distinct;
- relation:
  REPLAY_OF
  REPLACES
  VOIDED_BY

This requires official evidence.

11.6 HOME/AWAY CHANGE
---------------------
If venue changes but designated home/away teams do not:
- revision.

If official fixture is replaced with reversed home/away:
- review whether new fixture identity is required;
- do not auto-match solely on teams and approximate date.

24. ENTITY RESOLUTION WORKFLOW
======================================================================

24.1 DEFINITION
--------------
Entity resolution links provider records that refer to the same real entity
when no shared reliable identifier exists.

24.2 ORDER OF METHODS
---------------------
1. Approved provider mapping API or explicit crosswalk.
2. Exact trusted external ID.
3. Deterministic multi-field match.
4. Probabilistic record linkage.
5. Human review.
6. Remain unresolved.

24.3 DETERMINISTIC PLAYER MATCH
-------------------------------
Strong examples:
- exact stable provider crosswalk;
- exact name + date of birth + team context;
- exact official FPL mapping plus verified external feed mapping.

Weak:
- exact web name only;
- surname + team;
- shirt number only.

24.4 PROBABILISTIC MATCHING
---------------------------
Fellegi–Sunter-style entity resolution provides a principled framework for
combining agreement/disagreement evidence when unique IDs are absent.

Candidate features:
- normalized full name;
- surname/given tokens;
- date of birth;
- team at event time;
- nationality;
- position;
- height;
- known provider aliases;
- fixture participation.

Model outputs:
- match probability;
- comparison vector;
- threshold result;
- uncertainty;
- model version.

24.5 BLOCKING
-------------
Candidate generation must reduce comparisons using context:
- same fixture/team roster;
- same season/competition;
- date-of-birth year;
- surname key.

Blocking improves scale but can hide true matches. Its recall must be evaluated.

24.6 TERM FREQUENCY
-------------------
Common names have less discriminating power than rare names.
If probabilistic linkage is used, term-frequency adjustments SHOULD be
considered and validated.

24.7 MANUAL REVIEW QUEUE
------------------------
Fields:
- candidate pair;
- probability;
- evidence;
- conflicts;
- source records;
- reviewer;
- decision;
- notes;
- time.

Actions:
MATCH
NO_MATCH
DEFER
REQUEST_EVIDENCE
CREATE_NEW_ENTITY

24.8 THRESHOLDS
---------------
Thresholds depend on risk.

For a player mapping that drives betting-market projection:
- false match is potentially catastrophic;
- uncertain cases should go to review.

For a noncritical display alias:
- lower threshold may be acceptable.

24.9 TRANSITIVITY
-----------------
If A maps to B and B maps to C, do not assume A=C blindly.

Check:
- entity type;
- time;
- source;
- conflicting identifiers;
- merge history.

24.10 NEGATIVE EVIDENCE
-----------------------
Store rejected pairs to prevent repeat proposals.

31. RESOLVING THE REQUIRED EDGE CASES
======================================================================

31.1 DIFFERENT PLAYER NAMES ACROSS PROVIDERS
--------------------------------------------
Resolution:
- raw alias per provider;
- Unicode-normalized match keys;
- provider context;
- external mappings;
- deterministic/probabilistic linkage;
- human verification for ambiguous cases.

Never:
join on lower(name).

31.2 TRANSFERS BETWEEN CLUBS
----------------------------
Resolution:
- Player remains stable;
- membership closes/opens;
- FPL season team assignment updates;
- market selection remains linked to Player;
- historical fixture links unchanged.

31.3 DUPLICATE NAMES
--------------------
Resolution:
- separate Player UUIDs;
- matching uses date of birth/team/season/provider context;
- UI shows disambiguation;
- name-only automatic merge prohibited.

31.4 POSTPONED FIXTURES
-----------------------
Resolution:
- same Fixture;
- new FixtureRevision;
- Gameweek assignment revised separately;
- external provider ID may be superseded/mapped;
- historical known schedule preserved.

31.5 DOUBLE GAMEWEEKS
---------------------
Resolution:
- multiple Fixtures assigned to one Gameweek for a team;
- no duplicate Player/Gameweek record identity issue;
- fixture projections aggregate to Gameweek projection;
- captaincy uses Gameweek appearance/points under ruleset.

31.6 PROVIDER-SPECIFIC MARKET IDS
---------------------------------
Resolution:
- internal Market UUID;
- provider_market mapping;
- operator and market semantics explicit;
- selection mapping market-scoped;
- external IDs stored text.

31.7 HISTORICAL PLAYER IDS
--------------------------
Resolution:
- FPL element ID scoped to season;
- provider crosswalks retained;
- Player UUID stable;
- alias/membership evidence supports linkage;
- merge/supersession explicit.

31.8 PROVIDER DUPLICATE ENTITY MERGE
------------------------------------
Resolution:
- ingest merge mapping;
- evaluate;
- close old external mapping;
- preserve old source records;
- route current resolution to survivor.

31.9 FIXTURE RECREATED BY PROVIDER
----------------------------------
Resolution:
- compare competition/teams/official event context;
- map new ID to same Fixture if confirmed;
- otherwise relation REPLACES/REPLAY_OF.

31.10 PLAYER WITH MULTIPLE MARKET NAMES IN SAME MATCH
-----------------------------------------------------
Resolution:
- separate provider selections;
- candidate to same Player;
- preserve raw names;
- flag if operator accidentally duplicates runner;
- do not average until selection equivalence is verified.

[ ] Manual review exists.
[ ] Merge/split preserve history.
[ ] Historical IDs can map across seasons.

F. Temporal/provenance
[ ] Valid time and system time are distinct.
[ ] Closed-open intervals are used.
[ ] Raw sources and canonical facts are linked.
[ ] Current view is query-derived.

G. Implementation
[ ] Relational core is preferred over JSONB.
[ ] Constraints and golden tests are mandatory.
[ ] Unresolved critical records are quarantined.
