PURPOSE
-------
This document defines the unified captaincy and chip decision engine.

It covers:

- ordinary captaincy;
- vice-captaincy;
- Triple Captain;
- Bench Boost;
- Free Hit;
- Wildcard;
- any future chip introduced by FPL;
- chip inventory and expiry;
- chip opportunity cost;
- future-opportunity forecasting;
- chip interactions;
- chip preparation;
- value of waiting;
- rolling re-optimisation;
- rank-aware extensions;
- decision explanations.

Captaincy and chips belong in one document because:

- Triple Captain modifies the ordinary captain decision;
- only one chip may normally be used in a Gameweek;
- Wildcard can prepare a Bench Boost;
- Free Hit can protect a Wildcard-built permanent squad;
- a Double Gameweek can be attractive for Triple Captain, Bench Boost or Free
  Hit, but only one can be selected;
- using a chip now removes a future opportunity;
- a future chip may last multiple Gameweeks or alter transfer/captain rules;
- captain/vice choices must be optimised inside every chip state.

The critical concept is OPPORTUNITY COST.

A chip is not valuable because it produces many total Gameweek points.

A chip is valuable when:

    THE BEST POLICY USING IT NOW
    OUTPERFORMS
    THE BEST POLICY THAT RETAINS IT FOR LATER.

GOVERNING PRINCIPLE
-------------------
Each chip is a scarce, perishable exercise right.

Its value depends on:

- the current opportunity;
- the distribution of future opportunities;
- the information likely to arrive;
- its expiry;
- other chips competing for the same Gameweeks;
- preparation and unwinding costs;
- the manager's current squad;
- transfers, bank and purchase prices;
- rank objective;
- uncertainty and robustness;
- the ability to re-optimise later.

Pulse therefore solves a finite-horizon, multiple-stopping and stochastic
control problem.

It MUST NOT use rules such as:

- "Always Triple Captain in a Double Gameweek";
- "Bench Boost immediately after Wildcard";
- "Free Hit the biggest Blank Gameweek";
- "Wildcard after six bad players";
- "Captain the highest expected-points player and vice the second highest";
- "Use every chip as late as possible";
- "Use every first-half chip quickly because it expires";
- "A chip scoring 20 points was a good decision."

All such statements can be true in a particular state and false in another.

======================================================================
0. EXECUTIVE POLICY DECISIONS
======================================================================

0.1 SELECTED ARCHITECTURE
-------------------------
The production engine combines:

A. EXACT CURRENT-GAMEWEEK TACTICAL EVALUATION
From DMFP-12:

- legal XI;
- bench order;
- captain;
- vice;
- automatic substitutions;
- normal-chip scoring transformation.

B. MULTISTAGE STOCHASTIC CHIP SCHEDULING
At every future decision node:

- use no chip; or
- activate one eligible chip;
- choose the chip-specific optimal squad/transfers/lineup/captain;
- retain future chip inventory and recourse.

C. ROLLING-HORIZON MODEL PREDICTIVE CONTROL
Solve the chip schedule over an adaptive horizon, execute only the current
activation/nonactivation, then update and re-solve.

D. MULTIPLE-STOPPING VALUE FUNCTION
Every unused chip has a continuation value.
Multiple copies and expiry windows are treated as multiple exercise rights.

E. ROBUST/INFORMATION-AWARE POLICY
Use DMFP-10:

- robust regret;
- value of information;
- structural scenarios;
- rank utility later;
- confidence and action stability.

0.2 CHIP EXERCISE RULE
----------------------
For chip c at current node n:

    exercise_advantage[c,n]
      =
      Q(use c now | state n)
      -
      Q(hold c | state n)

Use c now only when:

- exercise advantage is positive under the selected objective;
- robust regret is acceptable;
- the chip is legally activatable;
- no competing chip has higher policy value;
- source/rules/solver state passes;
- execution timing is safe.

0.3 CURRENT GAIN VERSUS POLICY VALUE
------------------------------------
Define:

GROSS_CURRENT_GAIN:
Current Gameweek manager-score difference from the same-state, optimised no-chip
baseline.

NET_POLICY_VALUE:
Remaining-season utility difference after including:

- future chip availability;
- preparation/unwinding;
- transfers/hits;
- price paths;
- future information;
- future recourse;
- chip conflicts;
- terminal inventory/expiry.

A chip can have:

- high gross current gain but low/negative net policy value;
- low current gain but positive long-term policy value, especially Wildcard;
- positive value while producing a poor realised score.

0.4 MULTIPLE-STOPPING INTERPRETATION
------------------------------------
A set of chips resembles a finite collection of exercise rights.

Research on swing options and multiple stopping formalises decisions where a
holder chooses when to exercise several finite rights.

The analogy is useful because FPL chips have:

- finite inventory;
- expiry;
- stochastic rewards;
- mutually exclusive activation times;
- information arrival;
- state-dependent future value.

Pulse does not import financial assumptions such as tradability or market
completeness. It uses the dynamic-programming structure.

0.5 ORDINARY CAPTAINCY
----------------------
Ordinary captaincy is available every Gameweek and is not a finite inventory.

The optimal captain/vice pair is selected jointly from the starting XI using
scenario-level appearance and points.

The vice-captain is the best conditional fallback, not necessarily the player
with the second-highest unconditional expected points.

0.6 TRIPLE CAPTAIN
------------------
The incremental normal-points value of Triple Captain is exactly one additional
copy of the effective captain's raw Gameweek score.

Under reference rules:

- if captain appears, that player receives triple instead of double;
- if captain plays no minutes in the Gameweek, the triple multiplier passes to
  the vice-captain;
- if neither appears, the extra is lost and the chip is not returned.

Therefore the Triple Captain opportunity distribution is the effective
captain-score distribution after fallback, not the named captain's mean alone.

0.7 BENCH BOOST
----------------
The immediate incremental Bench Boost value is:

    all bench player points
    -
    points those bench players would already have contributed through normal
    automatic substitutions.

The full policy value also includes:

- budget held on the bench;
- transfer cost to prepare;
- starting-XI points sacrificed before/after;
- injury/rotation insurance;
- Wildcard interaction;
- future chip opportunity cost.

0.8 FREE HIT
------------
Free Hit value is:

    best temporary-squad policy
    -
    best policy without Free Hit

including:

- current Gameweek point uplift;
- transfer hits avoided;
- permanent squad damage avoided;
- free-transfer state;
- captain/bench effects;
- opportunity cost of future Free Hit use.

It is not simply:

    Free Hit team points - original XI expected points.

0.9 WILDCARD
------------
Wildcard value is a permanent-state policy difference.

Compare:

- normal transfers now;
- immediate Wildcard;
- delayed Wildcard after information;
- Free Hit bridge;
- alternative future Wildcard dates.

Wildcard is an irreversible reset option. Waiting can have value because it
preserves the ability to rebuild after more information, but waiting can also
lose points, prices and chip synergies.

0.10 FUTURE CHIP
----------------
Every future chip is defined by a declarative effect grammar.

No chip-specific production code is accepted until the generic representation
proves insufficient and an architecture decision records the exception.

0.11 SELECTED METHODS
---------------------
Primary:

- finite-horizon stochastic dynamic programming over chip inventory;
- rolling-horizon scenario-based optimisation;
- DMFP-12 mixed-integer squad/transfer subproblems;
- exact tactical scenario evaluation;
- approximate value functions for opportunities outside the explicit horizon.

Challengers:

- regression Monte Carlo optimal stopping;
- approximate dynamic programming;
- Monte Carlo tree search;
- robust multiple-stopping algorithms;
- learned chip policies.

1. REFERENCE CHIP RULES
======================================================================

1.1 REFERENCE SEASON
--------------------
Official 2025/26 guidance stated:

- eight chips across the season;
- two each of Wildcard, Free Hit, Triple Captain and Bench Boost;
- one copy of each for the first half;
- one copy of each for the second half;
- only one chip can be played in a Gameweek;
- first-half chips expire at the Gameweek 19 deadline;
- second-half copies are available from Gameweek 20;
- unused chips do not carry between halves.

These are not target-season assumptions.

1.2 REFERENCE BENCH BOOST
-------------------------
All bench-player points are included in the Gameweek total.

It could be cancelled before the deadline.

1.3 REFERENCE FREE HIT
----------------------
- unlimited free transfers for one Gameweek;
- permanent squad restored at the next deadline;
- unavailable in Gameweek 1;
- cannot be played in both Gameweek 19 and 20;
- irreversible after confirmation;
- banked free transfers were retained under the reference rules.

1.4 REFERENCE TRIPLE CAPTAIN
----------------------------
- captain scores triple instead of double;
- cancellable before the deadline;
- if captain records no minutes, vice receives triple;
- if neither records minutes, multiplier is lost and chip is not returned.

1.5 REFERENCE WILDCARD
----------------------
- all permanent transfers in the Gameweek are free;
- unavailable in Gameweek 1;
- irreversible after activation;
- banked transfers were retained under reference rules.

1.6 ONE-CHIP RULE
-----------------
One active chip per Gameweek under reference rules.

The optimiser treats this as configurable mutual exclusion.

1.7 BLANK AND DOUBLE GAMEWEEKS
------------------------------
Official guidance says Blank and Double Gameweeks are often caused by domestic
cup rescheduling and chips can help navigate/capitalise on them.

This is a strategic observation, not proof that the largest blank/double is
always the optimal chip week.

======================================================================
2. CHIP STATE AND INVENTORY
======================================================================

2.1 CHIP TOKEN
--------------
Each usable copy is an individual token:

- chip_inventory_id;
- chip_definition_id;
- acquired_at;
- activation_start;
- activation_end/expiry;
- duration;
- status;
- used_at;
- cancelled_at;
- source/ruleset.

2.2 INVENTORY STATE
-------------------
At node n:

    I_n[c,j] in {AVAILABLE, ACTIVATED, USED, EXPIRED, UNAVAILABLE}

where j indexes copies.

2.3 EXPIRY
----------
At expiry:

- unused token value becomes zero;
- inventory transitions to EXPIRED;
- no carryover unless rules explicitly permit it.

2.4 FUTURE INVENTORY
--------------------
A later token not yet available can be represented as scheduled inventory.

2.5 MUTUAL EXCLUSION
--------------------
For Gameweek t:

    sum_c activate[c,t] <= chip_concurrency_limit[t]

Reference:
limit one.

2.6 DURATION
------------
A chip may affect:

- one Gameweek;
- several consecutive Gameweeks;
- one deadline action;
- permanent future state.

The token remains active for configured duration.

2.7 CANCELLABILITY
------------------
Activation state machine:

NOT_ACTIVATED
PENDING_CANCELLABLE
CONFIRMED_IRREVERSIBLE
ACTIVE
COMPLETED
CANCELLED

Chip-specific rules control transitions.

======================================================================
4. FINITE-HORIZON VALUE FUNCTION
======================================================================

4.1 STATE
---------
At decision node n:

    S_n =
      manager state
      + belief state
      + fixture/price state
      + rank mode
      + chip inventory.

4.2 ACTION
----------
    a_n =
      transfers
      + lineup/bench
      + captain/vice
      + chip activation or no chip.

4.3 BELLMAN RECURSION
---------------------
Conceptually:

    V_n(S_n, I_n)
      =
      max_{a in legal(S_n,I_n)}
      E[
        R_n(S_n,a,omega)
        +
        V_next(S_next, I_next)
      ]

where future actions adapt only to revealed information.

4.4 USE VERSUS HOLD
-------------------
For chip c:

    Q_use(c,n)
      =
      best expected/robust utility with c activated now.

    Q_hold(c,n)
      =
      best expected/robust utility with c retained now.

    exercise_advantage
      =
      Q_use - Q_hold.

4.5 OPPORTUNITY COST
--------------------
The opportunity cost of using c now is the reduction in optimal future value
caused by removing the token:

    OC_c,n
      =
      E[
        V_next(S_next, I_n)
        -
        V_next(S_next, I_n \ {c})
      ]

conditional on the rest of the current action.

It is not the expected points of the best currently forecast future Gameweek
alone.

4.6 EXPIRY PRESSURE
-------------------
As expiry approaches:

- fewer future exercise dates remain;
- continuation value may fall;
- current exercise threshold falls;
- conflicts with other unused chips can force suboptimal use or expiry.

4.7 MULTIPLE RIGHTS
-------------------
With multiple copies, the value of one token depends on remaining copies.

The first of two copies can be used at a lower opportunity threshold than a
single unique season-long token, all else equal.

4.8 CHIP INTERACTION
--------------------
The marginal value of one chip depends on the inventory of others.

Example:

- Free Hit can make a Wildcard/Bench Boost route more valuable;
- using Triple Captain in a Double Gameweek frees Bench Boost for another;
- Wildcard preparation changes Bench Boost opportunities.

======================================================================
5. OPPORTUNITY SET AND FUTURE OPPORTUNITY MODEL
======================================================================

5.1 OPPORTUNITY
---------------
For chip c and future Gameweek t:

    O_c,t =
      incremental policy value if c is exercised at t,
      conditional on information and prior decisions.

5.2 FORECAST DISTRIBUTION
-------------------------
O is random because:

- fixtures may move;
- player roles/injuries change;
- prices change;
- squad changes;
- points distributions update;
- rank objective changes;
- another chip may occupy t.

5.3 NO "MAX OF FORECAST MEANS" RULE
-----------------------------------
Taking the maximum of current forecast means across future weeks:

- assumes current forecasts remain correct;
- ignores information updates;
- ignores chip conflicts;
- overstates selection value;
- ignores actions needed to prepare.

It is a diagnostic, not the policy value.

5.4 PERFECT-INFORMATION UPPER BOUND
-----------------------------------
A clairvoyant future best opportunity provides an upper bound.

It is not achievable and must not be compared directly to current exercise as
though it were.

5.5 POLICY OPPORTUNITY VALUE
----------------------------
The correct future value allows decisions at future nodes using information
available then.

5.6 OPPORTUNITY QUANTILES
-------------------------
For each chip:

- current gross gain;
- current net policy value;
- percentile of current opportunity among future policy opportunities;
- P(future opportunity exceeds current by delta);
- expected best future exercise value;
- expiry-adjusted continuation value.

5.7 FORECAST BIAS
-----------------
The best-looking future opportunity is subject to winner's-curse/forecast error.

Use model uncertainty and out-of-time calibration.

======================================================================
6. ORDINARY CAPTAINCY
======================================================================

6.1 EFFECTIVE CAPTAIN SCORE
---------------------------
For scenario s, captain c and vice v:

    C_eff(c,v,s)
      =
      X_c,s          if captain records any minutes in the Gameweek;
      X_v,s          if captain records zero minutes and vice records any;
      0              otherwise.

`X` is raw DMFP-09 Gameweek points.

6.2 NORMAL MANAGER EXTRA
------------------------
Normal captaincy adds:

    C_eff(c,v,s)

because the raw points already include one copy.

6.3 PAIR OBJECTIVE
------------------
Under expected-points mode:

    max_{c != v in XI}
      E[C_eff(c,v)]

subject to exact XI/tactical selection.

6.4 VICE VALUE
--------------
Vice value is:

    E[
      X_v
      x 1[captain zero, vice appears]
    ]

not:

    E[X_v].

6.5 CORRELATION
---------------
Captain and vice can share nonappearance risk:

- same postponed fixture;
- same team illness/rotation;
- same schedule.

A lower-mean vice from a different risk source may be better conditionally.

6.6 DOUBLE GAMEWEEK
-------------------
Under the rule "captain plays no minutes in the Gameweek":

- appearing in one of two fixtures prevents vice fallback;
- the captain retains multiplier on total Gameweek points.

Load exact target-season rule.

6.7 CAPTAIN DISTRIBUTION
-----------------------
Output:

- expected effective captain score;
- median/variance;
- P(0);
- P(5+/10+/15+/20+);
- vice fallback probability;
- conditional vice gain;
- captain/vice nonappearance joint probability.

6.8 RANK-AWARE CAPTAINCY
------------------------
DMFP-14 may replace absolute points utility with rank/relative utility.

Ownership is not used in normal points mode.

6.9 HIGH UPSIDE
----------------
Do not select a captain merely for variance.

Use:

- P(beat baseline/rival threshold);
- upper-tail incremental score;
- expected-utility floor;
- appearance constraints.

======================================================================
7. TRIPLE CAPTAIN
======================================================================

7.1 SCORING TRANSFORM
---------------------
Normal manager extra:

    C_eff.

Triple Captain manager extra:

    2 x C_eff.

Incremental TC gain over normal captaincy:

    Delta_TC(s)
      =
      C_eff(c_TC,v_TC,s)
      + any change from selecting a different optimal captain/vice pair under
        the chip objective.

The chip and captain pair are optimised jointly.

7.2 RAW CURRENT TC VALUE
------------------------
    gross_TC_t
      =
      E[manager points with TC]
      -
      E[best normal manager points].

7.3 POLICY VALUE
----------------
    net_TC_t
      =
      Q_use(TC,t)
      -
      Q_hold(TC,t).

7.4 FUTURE COMPARISON
---------------------
Compare against plausible future:

- Double Gameweeks;
- strong single fixture;
- multiple premium candidates;
- fixture certainty;
- player p_start/minutes;
- market confidence;
- rank utility;
- chip conflict.

7.5 DOUBLE GAMEWEEK
-------------------
Two fixtures can increase expected score but also introduce:

- rotation;
- injury between fixtures;
- one-fixture appearance;
- fixture cancellation;
- captaincy competition;
- correlated team performance.

Use joint Gameweek scenarios.

7.6 SINGLE GAMEWEEK
-------------------
A strong single fixture can dominate a weak/uncertain double.

No DGW requirement.

7.7 DISTRIBUTION
----------------
Output incremental chip PMF:

- mean;
- median;
- variance;
- P(0);
- P(5+/10+/15+ incremental);
- downside;
- upper tail;
- robust regret.

7.8 APPEARANCE RISK
-------------------
A chip does not return after captain/vice failure under reference rules.

Include:

- p captain zero;
- p vice fallback;
- p chip produces zero extra;
- lineup/news value.

7.9 EXERCISE THRESHOLD
----------------------
Use current TC when:

- current net policy value positive;
- current opportunity exceeds continuation under selected robust policy;
- future high-value opportunities are absent/less likely or chip expiry
  approaches;
- captain/vice distribution passes confidence.

7.10 OPPORTUNITY-COST OUTPUT
----------------------------
- gross current incremental points;
- continuation value;
- exercise advantage;
- P(now is optimal under scenario worlds);
- expected future best policy gain;
- future opportunity quantiles;
- expiry pressure;
- competing chip value.

======================================================================
8. BENCH BOOST
======================================================================

8.1 SCORING TRANSFORM
---------------------
Under Bench Boost:

- all 15 permanent-squad player points count according to active rules;
- normal automatic-substitution treatment is replaced/modified by chip rules.

Implement through configuration.

8.2 INCREMENTAL CURRENT VALUE
-----------------------------
For scenario s:

    Delta_BB(s)
      =
      score_BB(s)
      -
      score_best_normal_tactics(s under the saved no-chip action).

Conceptually:

    bench total
    -
    bench points already realised through normal auto substitutions
    + any tactical/captain differences.

8.3 DO NOT USE BENCH SUM ALONE
------------------------------
If a starting player misses and a bench player would have auto-subbed normally,
that bench score is not entirely incremental chip value.

8.4 POLICY VALUE
----------------
Bench Boost policy includes squad preparation.

Compare:

A. no chip route;
B. immediate BB with current squad;
C. prepare through normal transfers then BB;
D. Wildcard then BB;
E. delay to future fixture state.

8.5 BENCH CAPITAL COST
----------------------
Define:

    bench_investment_cost
      =
      optimal expected policy value with unconstrained ordinary squad
      -
      optimal expected policy value under the squad preparation needed for BB,
        excluding BB scoring benefit.

This captures:

- money shifted from XI;
- weaker captain/premium;
- transfers;
- price locks;
- post-BB unwinding;
- opportunity cost before/after.

8.6 NET BENCH BOOST VALUE
------------------------
    net_BB_policy_value
      =
      incremental BB scoring
      - preparation cost
      - transfer/hit cost
      - post-chip lock-in cost
      - chip continuation value foregone
      + insurance/flexibility benefits.

8.7 BENCH BOOST WITHOUT PREPARATION
-----------------------------------
Sometimes the current squad naturally has:

- 15 likely starters;
- good fixtures;
- low opportunity cost.

A "natural" BB can beat a higher-gross, heavily engineered future BB.

8.8 DOUBLE GAMEWEEK
-------------------
A Double Gameweek can create up to two fixtures for each player, but fixture
count is not value.

Use:

- joint points;
- p_start/minutes each fixture;
- rotation/injury;
- postponement;
- bench capital cost;
- other chip conflict.

8.9 WILDCARD PAIRING
--------------------
Wildcard-BB synergy:

    synergy(WC_t, BB_u)
      =
      value(joint schedule)
      - value(best policy using WC without planned BB)
      - value(best policy using BB without WC preparation)
      + value(no relevant chips baseline).

Compute by policy re-solves.

Do not assume synergy is positive.

8.10 POST-BB SQUAD
------------------
A BB-prepared squad may contain excess bench budget afterward.

Future value includes:

- keeping useful depth;
- transfers to release money;
- opportunity cost.

8.11 CURRENT OUTPUT
-------------------
- expected BB incremental points;
- bench player component;
- auto-sub overlap;
- P(all 15 appear);
- P(at least 14/13 appear);
- preparation cost;
- bench capital;
- WC synergy;
- continuation/opportunity cost;
- net policy value;
- confidence.

======================================================================
9. FREE HIT
======================================================================

9.1 STATE TRANSFORM
-------------------
Under reference rules:

- create a temporary 15-player squad;
- permit unlimited free temporary transfers;
- optimise temporary XI/bench/captain/vice;
- permanent squad returns after the Gameweek;
- FT transition follows active rules.

9.2 CORRECT BASELINE
--------------------
No-FH baseline must optimise:

- normal permanent transfers;
- transfer hits;
- lineup;
- bench;
- captain;
- future permanent squad.

9.3 GROSS CURRENT VALUE
-----------------------
    gross_FH_t
      =
      E[best temporary FH manager points]
      -
      E[best no-FH manager points this Gameweek].

9.4 NET POLICY VALUE
--------------------
Includes:

- one-week point uplift;
- avoided hits;
- permanent squad damage avoided;
- transfer inventory effects;
- future squad retained;
- future FH opportunity foregone;
- chip conflicts;
- price/timing uncertainty.

9.5 LONG-TERM DAMAGE AVOIDED
----------------------------
Without FH, a manager may permanently transfer out good long-term players to
field the current week.

Define:

    preserved_squad_value
      =
      best future utility with original permanent squad retained
      -
      best future utility after necessary no-FH permanent changes,

net of later recovery transfers.

9.6 BLANK GAMEWEEK
------------------
FH value depends on:

- current squad players blanking;
- bench;
- normal transfers available;
- hit cost;
- quality of temporary fixtures;
- permanent players desired immediately afterward.

Large blank != automatically high FH value.

9.7 DOUBLE GAMEWEEK
-------------------
FH can attack a double without permanently owning its players.

Compare to:

- TC;
- BB;
- normal transfers;
- Wildcard.

9.8 DEFENSIVE VERSUS OFFENSIVE
------------------------------
Defensive FH:
avoid a structurally bad week/forced damage.

Offensive FH:
exploit unusually strong one-week fixtures.

Both are valid.

9.9 PRICE STATE
----------------
Use target rules for:

- temporary buying prices;
- bank;
- restored ownership spells;
- price changes to permanent players;
- free transfers.

Do not infer from reference season.

9.10 OUTPUT
-----------
- temporary squad/tactics;
- gross one-week uplift;
- hit avoidance;
- permanent squad preservation;
- FT effect;
- future opportunity cost;
- net policy value;
- P(FH optimal);
- blank/double decomposition;
- confidence.

======================================================================
10. WILDCARD
======================================================================

10.1 STATE TRANSFORM
--------------------
Wildcard permanently rebuilds the squad under active rules with no transfer-hit
cost.

It creates new purchase-price ownership spells for incoming players.

10.2 NO DIRECT CHIP POINTS
--------------------------
Wildcard itself does not add a scoring multiplier.

Its value appears through:

- current and future squad points;
- hits saved;
- price routes;
- flexibility;
- chip synergies;
- fewer forced transfers.

10.3 COMPARATORS
----------------
For each possible activation date:

A. normal transfers;
B. immediate WC;
C. wait and WC after information;
D. FH bridge then WC;
E. normal transfers then delayed WC;
F. no WC before expiry.

10.4 IMMEDIATE WILDCARD VALUE
-----------------------------
    Q_WC_now
      =
      optimal rolling policy with permanent reset now.

10.5 HOLD VALUE
---------------
    Q_WC_hold
      =
      optimal current normal action
      + adaptive future policy retaining WC.

10.6 EXERCISE ADVANTAGE
-----------------------
    exercise_advantage_WC
      =
      Q_WC_now - Q_WC_hold.

10.7 SQUAD MISMATCH
-------------------
Metrics:

- number of desired changes;
- expected points lost under current squad;
- hit-equivalent repair cost;
- forced-transfer risk;
- captain/price bracket mismatch;
- blank/double route mismatch;
- bench fragility.

No fixed "five transfers means Wildcard" rule.

10.8 DELAYED WILDCARD
---------------------
Waiting can reveal:

- new manager roles;
- injuries;
- fixture/rescheduling;
- player prices;
- set pieces;
- markets;
- tournament return.

Value of waiting from DMFP-10 is included.

10.9 COST OF WAITING
--------------------
- current points foregone;
- hits or fielding weakness;
- price route loss;
- expiring chip window;
- lost BB/FH timing;
- execution risk.

10.10 REAL-OPTION INTERPRETATION
-------------------------------
Wildcard is an irreversible reconfiguration right.

Real-options research highlights the value of waiting when an irreversible
decision can be delayed for information.

The analogy is used only for decision timing; FPL has finite deadlines and no
tradable option market.

10.11 FUTURE INFORMATION
------------------------
The optimiser must represent the actual information event.

"Wait for more data" is invalid if no meaningful information will arrive before
the next relevant decision.

10.12 WILDCARD SQUAD ROBUSTNESS
-------------------------------
The squad should be optimised over future scenarios, not one deterministic
fixture ticker.

Include:

- lineup certainty;
- price paths;
- future transfers;
- bench;
- chip route;
- blanks/doubles;
- team/player correlation.

10.13 EARLY WILDCARD
--------------------
Can be optimal when:

- opening priors were materially wrong;
- roles/players emerge;
- current squad mismatch is expensive;
- long runway amplifies squad quality;
- delay information value is low.

Can be poor when it overreacts to one noisy Gameweek or destroys a valuable
future reset.

10.14 LATE WILDCARD
-------------------
Can build a precise remaining-season/BB route.
Opportunity cost declines near expiry, but fewer future weeks also reduce
benefit.

10.15 OUTPUT
-----------
- immediate WC squad and policy;
- normal-transfer comparator;
- delayed activation alternatives;
- current/future utility;
- hits saved;
- price effect;
- flexibility;
- BB/FH/TC synergy;
- information value;
- exercise advantage;
- squad mismatch;
- confidence.

======================================================================
11. CHIP INTERACTIONS
======================================================================

11.1 ONE GAMEWEEK COMPETITION
-----------------------------
At one Gameweek, only one eligible chip can activate under reference rules.

For a strong Double Gameweek compare:

- TC;
- BB;
- FH;
- WC;
- no chip.

11.2 SEQUENCE INTERACTION
-------------------------
Examples:

- WC -> BB;
- WC -> normal week -> BB;
- WC -> BB -> FH blank;
- FH blank -> WC -> BB double;
- TC double while preserving BB;
- early BB removes bench-investment pressure later.

11.3 SYNERGY DEFINITION
-----------------------
For chips c,d on schedule sigma:

    synergy(c,d)
      =
      V(policy with c and d)
      - V(policy with c only)
      - V(policy with d only)
      + V(no c,d).

11.4 CANNIBALISATION
--------------------
Two chips may compete for the same high-value week.

The scheduler measures:

- best joint dates;
- value lost because both cannot be used together;
- alternative opportunity quality.

11.5 PREPARATION DEPENDENCE
--------------------------
A chip schedule affects prior transfers/squad.

The chip scheduler and squad optimiser cannot be separated into:

1. choose chip dates;
2. optimise squad afterward.

They must iterate or solve jointly.

11.6 COORDINATION ALGORITHM
---------------------------
V1:

1. generate candidate chip schedules;
2. call DMFP-12 for each schedule;
3. evaluate on common scenarios;
4. refine around top schedules;
5. return Pareto set.

V2:
integrated multistage mixed-integer model with chip activation variables.

Enumerating all combinations can be large, especially with multiple copies and
future chips.

12.2 REQUIRED DATES
-------------------
Always include:

- high bench opportunity;

12.3 UPPER BOUNDS
-----------------
Per chip/Gameweek upper bound:

- TC: upper effective-captain extra;
- BB: upper bench incremental;
- FH: temporary optimal minus relaxed baseline;
- WC: relaxed future squad/hit bound.

Dates with bounds below incumbent opportunity can be pruned if safe.

12.4 BEAM SEARCH
----------------
V1 may maintain top partial schedules by:

- expected upper value;

12.5 EXACT SMALL CASE

For small chip/date sets, enumerate every legal schedule.

12.6 FUTURE CHIP
----------------
Candidate generation uses effect type/duration, not name.

13. MULTIPLE-STOPPING SOLUTION METHODS
======================================================================

13.1 EXACT BACKWARD INDUCTION
-----------------------------
For reduced state/date opportunity process:

- recursively solve value with k rights remaining;
- use Bellman exercise/continue comparison.

Selected for:

- toy/golden validation;
- TC-only/BB-only simplified timing;
- expiry-window opportunity model.

13.2 REGRESSION MONTE CARLO
---------------------------
Estimate continuation value from simulated future states.

Use:

- basis/gradient boosting;
- cross-fitting;
- out-of-sample lower-bound policy;
- dual/perfect-information upper bounds where feasible.

13.3 SWING-OPTION RESEARCH
--------------------------
Carmona and Touzi formalise multiple optimal stopping for several exercise
rights.

Robust multiple-stopping research provides upper/lower-bound approaches under
model uncertainty.

Pulse uses these as methodological references, not financial pricing models.

13.4 APPROXIMATE DYNAMIC PROGRAMMING
------------------------------------
Learn continuation value:

    V_hat(state, inventory, time).

Features:

- current opportunities;
- future fixture structure;
- squad mismatch;
- chip inventory;
- expiry;
- rank;
- uncertainty;
- information schedule.

13.5 MCTS
---------
Challenger for future-chip complexity.

Requires:

- legal schedule action generator;
- progressive widening;
- DMFP-12 rollout policy;
- reproducible budgets;
- comparison with DP/MILP bounds.

13.6 SELECTED V1
----------------
Scenario schedule enumeration/beam search plus DMFP-12 rolling optimisation,
supported by approximate continuation values.

Why:

- transparent;
- auditable;
- reuses central engine;
- handles current FPL dimensions;
- produces alternatives.

======================================================================
14. CAPTAIN/CHIP OBJECTIVES
======================================================================

14.1 POINTS MODE
----------------
Maximise expected remaining-season FPL points.

14.2 ROBUST MODE
----------------
Apply DMFP-10 robust regret to model/lineup/fixture worlds.

Do not penalise ordinary haul variance blindly.

14.3 CONSERVATIVE CHIP PLAN
--------------------------
Within an expected-utility epsilon:

- minimise robust regret;
- reduce appearance/fixture failure;
- preserve future flexibility;
- avoid heavy preparation.

14.4 HIGH-UPSIDE CHIP PLAN
-------------------------
Within expected-utility and fragility constraints:

- maximise P(beat baseline by K);
- upper-tail expected gain;
- rank utility via DMFP-14.

Do not use raw chip variance.

14.5 RANK MODE
--------------
DMFP-14 supplies:

- effective ownership;
- rival captain/chip scenarios;
- rank utility.

Ownership is not used in normal points mode.

14.6 CHIP EXPIRY
----------------
Use-it-or-lose-it is reflected in inventory terminal value zero.
It does not justify a chip that displaces a much better competing chip.

24. CONSERVATIVE AND HIGH-UPSIDES MODES
