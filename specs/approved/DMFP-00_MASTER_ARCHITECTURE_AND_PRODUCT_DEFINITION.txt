DOCUMENT PURPOSE
----------------
This is the controlling document for DMF Pulse: the system intended to help
Sebastian make the strongest possible sequence of decisions in the 2026/27
Fantasy Premier League season.

It defines:

- the product mission;
- what "win FPL" means as a mathematical and operational objective;
- the boundary between market information and proprietary modelling;
- the system modules and their dependencies;
- the canonical decision lifecycle;
- the treatment of uncertainty;
- the separation between projections and strategy;
- the required evidence, auditability and backtesting standards;
- the minimum viable system to be ready before Gameweek 1;
- the conditions under which Codex is allowed to implement modules;
- acceptance criteria for a trustworthy decision engine.

This document does not fully specify each mathematical model. Those decisions
belong in the numbered module specifications. It does, however, constrain those
documents so that the overall system remains coherent.

======================================================================
0. EXECUTIVE DECISION SUMMARY
======================================================================

0.1 THE PRODUCT IN ONE SENTENCE
-------------------------------
DMF Pulse is a market-informed, probabilistic, rolling-horizon FPL decision
engine that converts football betting markets and other time-stamped information
into player point distributions, then chooses transfers, squads, line-ups,
captains and chips to maximise Sebastian's season objective.

0.2 CORE STRATEGIC THESIS
-------------------------
The engine will not begin by trying to recreate the entire forecasting operation
of professional betting markets.

Instead:

    betting markets estimate football outcomes;
    DMF Pulse translates those outcomes into FPL decisions.

Markets are the primary external information aggregator for football-event
probabilities. They are not accepted blindly. Pulse will:

- remove bookmaker margin;
- detect stale or anomalous prices;
- combine providers;
- reconcile contradictory markets;
- correct systematic calibration bias where evidence supports it;
- understand settlement conditions;
- supplement missing player markets with transparent models;
- model FPL-specific variables that sportsbooks do not directly price.

The proprietary advantage is expected to come primarily from:

1. player starting and minutes distributions;
2. coherent translation from betting markets to FPL scoring distributions;
3. price-change and team-value decisions;
4. multi-Gameweek squad and transfer optimisation;
5. captaincy and chip opportunity-cost modelling;
6. rank-aware risk strategy;
7. disciplined use of information available at each deadline;
8. calibration, decision logging and continual improvement.

0.3 NON-GUARANTEE
-----------------
No system can guarantee first place in a competition affected by football
outcomes, injuries, rotation, ownership, schedule changes and millions of
opponents.

"Build an engine that will win FPL" is therefore interpreted as:

    build the most rigorous engine practicable for maximising the probability
    of an exceptional overall finish, including the probability of overall rank
    1, without pretending that uncertainty can be eliminated.

A poor final rank does not automatically prove the engine was poor, and a strong
rank does not automatically prove it was good. Evaluation MUST distinguish
decision quality from realised luck.

0.4 CONTROLLING ARCHITECTURAL DECISIONS
---------------------------------------
AD-00-01. Market-first, not market-only.
Betting markets are the initial anchor for football probabilities. Pulse retains
proprietary models where markets are absent, thin, incoherent or not directly
mapped to FPL.

AD-00-02. Distributions, not point estimates.
The core product is a joint distribution of plausible FPL outcomes. Expected
points are one summary, not the model.

AD-00-03. Projection is separate from preference.
A player's football and FPL outcome distribution MUST NOT change because
Sebastian is chasing rank. Risk preference belongs in the decision layer.

AD-00-04. Rolling horizon.
Pulse will repeatedly optimise a finite future horizon, implement only the
current decision, then re-optimise when new information arrives.

AD-00-05. Time-travel-safe data.
Every recommendation MUST be reconstructible from the information actually
available at that moment. Backtests MUST NOT use post-deadline odds, line-ups,
injury news, prices or outcomes.

AD-00-06. Rules are data.
FPL rules, squad constraints, chips, deadlines and scoring MUST be stored in a
versioned season configuration and MUST NOT be scattered through code.

AD-00-07. Human-in-the-loop.
For the first season, Pulse recommends and records; Sebastian confirms and
executes. No autonomous transfer, captain or chip submission.

AD-00-08. Explainable recommendations.
Every decision MUST include alternatives, assumptions, uncertainty, marginal
value, failure scenarios and the reason it changed.

AD-00-09. Fail closed.
When critical data is stale, missing, unlicensed or internally inconsistent,
Pulse MUST degrade visibly or refuse to issue a high-confidence recommendation.

AD-00-10. LLMs are outside the numerical truth path.
Large language models MAY help research, summarise news and draft explanations.
They MUST NOT silently invent probabilities, odds, player identities, rules or
solver outputs.

AD-00-11. The FPL engine is upstream of DMF content.
Content automation consumes approved Pulse outputs. Content performance must
never influence player projections unless a separately justified variable is
introduced.

AD-00-12. One validated vertical slice before breadth.
The first implementation target is:

    time-stamped pre-deadline data
    -> market probabilities
    -> player point distributions
    -> legal squad/captain decision
    -> reproducible decision package
    -> ex-ante evaluation

Price rises, chips and rank-aware strategy are added after this slice works.

1. PRODUCT MISSION, VISION AND DEFINITION OF SUCCESS
======================================================================

1.1 MISSION
-----------
At every FPL decision point, produce the action with the best defensible
probability-weighted value given:

- the current squad;
- money in the bank;
- purchase and selling prices;
- free transfers;
- chip inventory;
- fixtures and deadlines;
- market-implied football probabilities;
- player availability and minutes uncertainty;
- future price changes;
- future information likely to arrive;
- Sebastian's current rank and season objective;
- the decisions available in later Gameweeks.

1.2 VISION
----------
By the end of the 2026/27 season, Pulse should function as a decision journal and
learning system rather than a one-off optimiser.

It should be able to answer:

- What did we know at the deadline?
- What probability distribution did we assign?
- What were the best legal alternatives?
- Why was one action selected?
- What assumptions dominated the result?
- What new information changed the recommendation?
- Was the forecast calibrated?
- Was the decision correct ex ante, even if the outcome was poor?
- Where is the engine systematically wrong?
- Which modules genuinely add value over simple baselines?

1.3 PRIMARY USER
----------------
The primary user is Sebastian Greenhalgh operating one official FPL team.

The system is initially optimised for:

- a technically sophisticated user;
- willingness to inspect uncertainty and assumptions;
- manual final execution;
- a high ambition for overall rank;
- a preference for rigorous, non-hype explanations;
- acceptance that some recommendations will be counterintuitive;
- acceptance that an optimal policy can still lose.

1.4 SECONDARY USERS
-------------------
Not in the initial build, but supported by the architecture:

- a public DMF projection dashboard;
- subscribers with their own squads;
- educational notebooks;
- content-generation systems;
- research papers;
- simulation of alternative manager profiles;
- a future API or product.

The private engine MUST be correct and auditable before public productisation.

1.5 SUCCESS HIERARCHY
---------------------
Success is assessed at five levels.

Level 1 — System integrity
- correct rules;
- correct identities;
- fresh data;
- reproducible decisions;
- no leakage;
- legal FPL actions;
- reliable deadline operation.

Level 2 — Forecast quality
- calibrated event probabilities;
- calibrated starting/minutes probabilities;
- well-covered FPL point intervals;
- useful joint simulations;
- improvement over transparent baselines.

Level 3 — Decision quality
- higher expected decision value than no-action and simple heuristic policies;
- sensible marginal transfer and chip values;
- low avoidable regret;
- robust behaviour under model uncertainty;
- coherent treatment of future options.

Level 4 — Season performance
- strong points total;
- strong overall rank;
- effective chips;
- preserved team value where useful;
- few unforced decision errors.

Level 5 — Aspirational outcome
- contend for and maximise the probability of overall rank 1.

Level 5 is the mission aspiration. Levels 1–4 are the engineering and scientific
standards that can be controlled.

2. THE DECISION PROBLEM
======================================================================

2.1 FORMAL VIEW
---------------
FPL is a sequential decision problem under partial information.

At time t:

- S_t is the manager state:
  squad, purchase prices, selling prices, bank, free transfers, chips, rank and
  relevant league state.

- I_t is the information set:
  all observations legally and actually available at time t, including odds,
  fixtures, injuries, predicted line-ups, press information, ownership and
  model state.

- A(S_t) is the set of legal actions:
  transfers, chip choices, starting XI, bench order, captain and vice-captain.

- Y_(t:T) is the uncertain future:
  football events, minutes, injuries, postponements, price changes, ownership
  changes and opponent decisions.

- U is the terminal utility:
  the value assigned to final points, final rank, target-rank probability or a
  combination.

The ideal policy is:

    a*_t = argmax over a in A(S_t)
           E[ U(S_T, R_T) | S_t, I_t, action a, future policy ]

The exact solution across an entire season is computationally and statistically
intractable. Pulse will use a rolling-horizon approximation, scenario simulation
and explicit terminal-value approximations.

2.2 WHY EXPECTED POINTS ALONE ARE INCOMPLETE
--------------------------------------------
Expected points are indispensable but not sufficient because:

- transfers alter future squads;
- purchase prices affect later affordability;
- free transfers have option value;
- chips are scarce and have opportunity cost;
- bench points matter differently under Bench Boost;
- players and matches are correlated;
- future information has value;
- final rank has a nonlinear relationship with points;
- a late-season manager chasing rank may rationally prefer a different risk
  profile from a manager defending rank.

The engine MUST nevertheless keep the football projection objective and the
manager utility objective separate.

2.3 OPERATING OBJECTIVE BY SEASON STAGE
---------------------------------------
The default policy will be stage-adaptive, subject to later research in
DMFP-14.

Preseason and early season:
- maximise robust expected points;
- heavily value minutes security and information;
- avoid overreacting to tiny samples;
- value squad flexibility and routes to high-upside players;
- use stronger priors and wider uncertainty;
- avoid variance for its own sake.

Middle season:
- maximise rolling expected points over a medium horizon;
- account for fixture swings, chips and price effects;
- introduce rank and effective-ownership scenarios without allowing them to
  distort base projections.

Late season:
- increasingly optimise probability of a chosen rank target;
- model competitors and effective ownership;
- select variance and correlation intentionally;
- distinguish protecting a lead from chasing a deficit.

Final Gameweeks:
- use explicit target-rank or opponent-beating probability;
- shorten the horizon;
- value tactical leverage;
- include correlated captaincy and transfer decisions.

These are default strategic regimes, not fixed Gameweek cut-offs. The transition
SHOULD depend on rank, uncertainty, chip state and the remaining opportunity set.

2.4 CHAMPION UTILITY
--------------------
A literal "probability of rank 1" objective is too sparse and unstable early in
the season. Pulse SHOULD use a smooth surrogate during most of the season, then
increase weight on target-rank probabilities as the outcome becomes estimable.

Candidate components include:

- expected final points;
- expected negative log rank;
- probability of top 100k, 10k, 1k, 100, 10 and 1;
- probability of beating selected elite-manager or mini-league benchmarks;
- lower-tail risk;
- option value;
- model uncertainty penalty.

A possible research form is:

    Utility =
        w_points(t) * normalised expected final points
      + w_rank(t)   * E[-log(final rank)]
      + w_target(t) * P(final rank <= target)
      - w_tail(t)   * downside risk
      + w_option(t) * future flexibility

No weights are authorised by this document. DMFP-14 must research and validate
them.

2.5 VALUE OF INFORMATION
------------------------
Pulse MUST recognise that waiting can be an action.

Examples:

- delaying a transfer may reveal a press conference, training update or European
  line-up;
- transferring early may avoid a price rise;
- holding a Wildcard may allow future fixture confirmation;
- choosing a flexible squad may reduce the cost of future uncertainty.

For a decision d, Pulse SHOULD estimate:

    value of acting now
    versus
    value of waiting for expected information
    minus
    expected cost of waiting.

This is particularly important for price decisions and early-season uncertainty.

3. 2026/27 OPERATING CONTEXT
======================================================================

3.1 CALENDAR
------------
As of the research cut-off:

- the first scheduled Premier League match is Arsenal v Coventry City on
  Friday 21 August 2026;
- the final match round is Sunday 30 May 2027;
- the season contains 33 weekends and five midweek match rounds;
- fixture dates remain subject to television, cup and European amendments;
- nine Premier League clubs are involved in UEFA competitions.

Pulse MUST ingest official deadlines and fixture times from current data. It
MUST NOT infer a deadline from a generic rule such as "90 minutes before the
first kick-off" unless that rule is confirmed for 2026/27.

3.2 UNUSUALLY HIGH PRESEASON UNCERTAINTY
----------------------------------------
The official Premier League's 16 July preseason analysis identified:

- eight new Premier League managers;
- late returns for players involved deep into the 2026 FIFA World Cup;
- new signings and tactical changes;
- less than five weeks between the World Cup final weekend and the opening
  league fixtures for some players.

Consequences for Pulse:

- historical team-style priors must be down-weighted for changed managers;
- start and minutes uncertainty must be wider;
- pre-season line-ups must be treated as noisy evidence, not ground truth;
- World Cup participation and return date must be explicit covariates;
- promoted clubs need hierarchical priors and cross-competition uncertainty;
- market prices may update faster than public statistical models;
- initial squad optimisation should reward transfer-route flexibility;
- the engine should avoid false precision.

3.3 RULE UNCERTAINTY
--------------------
At the cut-off, the 2026/27 FPL game was not live. The prior 2025/26 game had
material changes including defensive-contribution points, revised assists, two
sets of major chips and BPS changes.

Pulse MUST assume that rule changes can materially alter:

- player position value;
- defensive-midfielder value;
- bench value;
- chip opportunity;
- free-transfer strategy;
- price-change data;
- optimisation constraints.

The launch-diff process in DMFP-02 is therefore a critical-path task.

5. DESIGN PRINCIPLES
======================================================================

5.1 PROBABILISTIC FIRST
-----------------------
Every material unknown SHOULD be represented probabilistically where feasible.

Examples:
- P(start);
- minutes distribution;
- P(clean sheet);
- goals distribution;
- points distribution;
- P(price rise tonight);
- P(fixture moves);
- P(player is available);
- chip-value distribution.

"Likely to start" without a probability is insufficient for the core engine.

5.2 CALIBRATION BEFORE COMPLEXITY
---------------------------------
A simpler well-calibrated model is preferred to a more accurate-looking but
overconfident model.

Model selection MUST consider:

- log score or log loss;
- Brier score for binary outcomes;
- continuous ranked probability score for scalar distributions;
- interval coverage;
- reliability diagrams;
- calibration intercept and slope;
- sharpness conditional on calibration.

Mean absolute error alone is not enough.

5.3 MARKET INFORMATION IS EVIDENCE, NOT TRUTH
----------------------------------------------
Market odds include:

- bookmaker margin;
- favourite-longshot effects;
- provider-specific pricing;
- stale prices;
- low-liquidity noise;
- settlement-rule differences;
- correlated or derivative markets;
- possible limits on player-prop coverage.

Pulse MUST preserve the raw quote and the transformed probability so every
adjustment can be audited.

5.4 TEMPORAL INTEGRITY
----------------------
Every row that can change MUST carry, as applicable:

- event time;
- provider publication time;
- ingestion time;
- effective-from time;
- effective-to time;
- source version;
- raw payload hash.

The system MUST be able to answer "what did Pulse know at 17:42 UTC?"

5.5 COHERENCE
-------------
The football simulation must be internally coherent.

Examples:
- expected player goals cannot materially exceed expected team goals without a
  flagged reconciliation issue;
- a player cannot keep a clean sheet when his team does not;
- a player cannot receive 60-minute clean-sheet points if simulated below the
  threshold;
- assists and goals must arise from compatible match events;
- bonus is allocated among players in the same simulated match;
- captaincy applies to the simulated player total, not separately to components;
- double-Gameweek totals preserve match-level dependence.

5.6 MODULARITY
--------------
Data acquisition, probability transformation, football simulation, FPL scoring
and decision utility MUST be separate modules.

A new odds provider should not require rewriting the optimiser.
A scoring change should not require rewriting football probabilities.
A rank objective should not alter player goal probabilities.

5.7 REPRODUCIBILITY
-------------------
Given:

- code commit;
- configuration version;
- model versions;
- raw-data snapshot hashes;
- random seed;
- decision time;

Pulse MUST reproduce the same recommendation and simulation summaries, subject
only to documented solver nondeterminism.

5.8 ROBUSTNESS
--------------
Pulse SHOULD avoid plans that are optimal only under one brittle estimate.

Robustness may include:

- scenario optimisation;
- uncertainty sets;
- chance constraints;
- downside penalties;
- multiple projection ensembles;
- sensitivity analysis;
- safe fallback actions;
- explicit price of robustness.

Robustness must not become unjustified conservatism.

5.9 OPTION VALUE
----------------
A legal future action has value.

Examples:
- a banked free transfer;
- money in the bank;
- a flexible player price point;
- an unused chip;
- avoiding a player whose minutes depend on unknown information;
- preserving a route to a future premium player.

The optimiser MUST NOT treat future flexibility as zero merely because it does
not score this Gameweek.

5.10 TRANSPARENCY
-----------------
Every recommendation MUST reveal:

- expected gain;
- distribution of gain;
- horizon;
- main assumptions;
- uncertainty;
- alternative actions;
- sensitivity;
- source freshness;
- model versions;
- constraints;
- why it changed.

5.11 GRACEFUL DEGRADATION
-------------------------
The system must classify inputs as:

- HEALTHY;
- DEGRADED;
- BLOCKING.

Examples:
- one missing bookmaker may be DEGRADED;
- no fresh player availability data may be DEGRADED;
- an unconfirmed FPL rules file is BLOCKING;
- unresolved duplicate player identities are BLOCKING;
- a stale deadline is BLOCKING.

5.12 KEEP THE NUMERICAL CORE SMALL
----------------------------------
For the preseason MVP:

- prefer a reliable CLI over a complex web application;
- prefer Postgres plus Parquet over many databases;
- prefer one proven optimiser over several experimental solvers;
- prefer manual high-quality availability inputs over unreliable automated NLP;
- prefer tested deterministic transformations over premature microservices.

Purpose:

- median;

9. DATA SOURCE STRATEGY
======================================================================

9.1 SOURCE CLASSES
------------------
Tier A — Authoritative game state
- official FPL game data;
- official Premier League fixtures and amendments;
- official rules;
- official club/player availability announcements where available.

Tier B — Market evidence
- betting exchange prices and volume;
- regulated bookmaker odds;
- odds aggregators with clear provenance;
- historical snapshots.

Tier C — Football context
- line-up providers;
- injuries and suspensions;
- press conferences;
- match statistics;
- European and cup schedules;
- travel and rest.

Tier D — FPL strategic context
- ownership;
- transfers in/out;
- price history;
- effective ownership;
- elite-manager ownership where lawful and technically available.

Tier E — Manual evidence
- Sebastian's verified observations;
- manual line-up overrides;
- provider outage corrections;
- explicitly documented assumptions.

9.2 FPL DATA ACCESS
-------------------
The FPL web application exposes JSON endpoints, including the widely used
bootstrap-static and fixtures endpoints. They are useful but publicly accessible
does not mean contractually stable or formally supported.

Therefore:
- adapters MUST tolerate schema change;
- raw payloads MUST be retained;
- endpoint availability MUST be monitored;
- no endpoint is assumed permanent;
- authenticated endpoints require separate security review;
- rules and deadlines are verified against the official game interface.

9.3 ODDS PROVIDER ABSTRACTION
-----------------------------
Current research shows a fragmented market:

- Betfair provides an Exchange API with odds and volume access plus a historical
  data service.
- The Odds API provides historical snapshots and EPL player props, but its
  documented soccer player-prop coverage is limited to selected US bookmakers,
  and historical event odds require paid access.
- Sportradar documents player props for top soccer leagues and enterprise-grade
  odds feeds.
- OpticOdds documents historical odds, player props, injuries, rosters and
  starting line-ups.
- Provider coverage, price, retention rights and geographical access must be
  verified by proof-of-access.

Architectural consequence:
- define one canonical market schema;
- implement provider adapters;
- never let provider-specific IDs reach projection or optimisation code;
- support at least one fallback;
- make provider coverage visible by fixture and player;
- store provider terms/version metadata;
- block commercial output if rights are unclear.

9.4 MINIMUM MARKET SET
----------------------
Preferred markets, in descending architectural value:

Core team markets:
- match 1X2;
- Asian handicap;
- total goals;
- team total goals;
- both teams to score;
- clean sheet or opponent to score zero;
- correct score where useful for reconciliation.

Core player markets:
- anytime goalscorer;
- two-or-more goals or player goal line;
- assist;
- shots;
- shots on target;
- goalkeeper saves;
- cards;
- player-specific lines where relevant.

Supporting markets:
- penalty markets;
- first goalscorer;
- player to score or assist;
- same-game combinations only if dependencies and margin can be handled.

The MVP is viable with team totals, clean-sheet probabilities, anytime scorer
markets and a strong minutes layer. Assist and bonus estimates can initially be
hybrid models.

9.5 SETTLEMENT RULE PROBLEM
---------------------------
Player-market odds cannot be interpreted until settlement rules are understood.

Examples of possible differences:
- bet void if player does not start;
- bet active if player appears at any time;
- dead-heat or special settlement;
- extra time excluded;
- named-player replacement rules;
- abandoned match rules.

Pulse MUST attach a settlement-rule profile to every player market and transform
the probability consistently with the minutes model.

9.6 DATA RETENTION
------------------
Pulse requires historical snapshots to backtest honestly.

Preferred retention:
- all raw FPL payloads at each scheduled ingest;
- every odds update allowed by license;
- provider timestamps;
- all transformations;
- every pre-deadline source bundle;
- every recommendation;
- all actual outcomes.

If a license forbids storing raw odds:
- store only permitted derived values;
- record the restriction;
- preserve reproducibility to the maximum lawful extent;
- do not claim complete historical reproducibility.

10. MARKET-INFORMED FORECASTING POLICY
======================================================================

10.1 RESEARCH BASIS
-------------------
Research supports using betting prices as a strong forecasting benchmark and as
an input to statistical football models. Published and emerging work has shown:

- hierarchical football models can blend historical data with bookmaker odds;
- market calibration can dominate purely model-based parameters;
- odds-to-probability conversion method matters;
- margin removal alone may not remove favourite-longshot bias;
- calibration is essential when probabilities feed decisions.

Pulse treats these findings as a design hypothesis to validate, not as proof
that every market is perfectly efficient.

10.2 MARKET-FIRST HIERARCHY
---------------------------
For a football quantity q:

1. Use a fresh, liquid, well-defined market consensus where available.
2. Reconcile multiple related markets into a coherent distribution.
3. Add player availability/minutes treatment.
4. Use proprietary models to fill missing components.
5. Shrink weak proprietary estimates toward market or hierarchical priors.
6. Expose residual disagreement and uncertainty.

10.3 AVOIDING DOUBLE COUNTING
-----------------------------
Do not independently combine:

- a team total goal market;
- player anytime goalscorer markets;
- a historical player xG model;

as though they were independent pieces of evidence.

DMFP-08 must define a reconciliation procedure. Candidate approaches include:

- constrained optimisation so player intensities sum to team intensity;
- Bayesian calibration with the market as prior or likelihood;
- latent match-intensity model jointly fitted to market probabilities;
- iterative proportional fitting;
- simulation weighting.

10.4 MARGIN REMOVAL
-------------------
The first implementation may include:

- multiplicative normalisation;
- power method;
- Shin method where applicable;
- exchange back/lay midpoint with commission treatment;
- a learned favourite-longshot correction.

No method is selected globally by this file. DMFP-06 must compare them
out-of-sample by market type and provider.

10.5 MARKET UNCERTAINTY
-----------------------
Uncertainty should increase when:

- few providers quote the market;
- books disagree;
- liquidity is low;
- prices are stale;
- line-ups are unknown;
- settlement rules differ;
- the quote moved sharply;
- the market is derivative or highly margined;
- provider mappings are uncertain.

Market consensus MUST produce an uncertainty measure, not only a mean.

10.6 MARKET MISSINGNESS
-----------------------
Missing a player prop is informative only after checking provider coverage. It
may mean:

- player unlikely to start;
- player not offered due low demand;
- provider omitted the market;
- feed is incomplete;
- player identity failed;
- market not yet open.

Pulse MUST NOT convert market absence directly into zero probability.

======================================================================
11. UNCERTAINTY ARCHITECTURE
======================================================================

11.1 UNCERTAINTY TYPES
----------------------
Aleatoric uncertainty:
Irreducible football randomness.

Epistemic uncertainty:
Uncertainty because the model or data lacks knowledge.

Market uncertainty:
Margin, disagreement, liquidity, staleness and coverage.

Availability uncertainty:
Injury, selection, rotation and substitutions.

Structural uncertainty:
Wrong model family, changed manager, changed role or changed rules.

Operational uncertainty:
API outages, mapping failures, delayed data and solver failures.

Strategic uncertainty:
Future ownership, rival actions, fixture rearrangements and chip usage.

These MUST be distinguishable in diagnostics where practicable.

11.2 EARLY-SEASON POLICY
------------------------
The opening Gameweeks require wider epistemic uncertainty.

Pulse SHOULD:

- use stronger hierarchical shrinkage;
- widen player-role and minutes distributions;
- increase promoted-team uncertainty;
- down-weight old-manager tactical data after a managerial change;
- model World Cup return and preseason load;
- value players with secure roles;
- reward flexible price structures;
- avoid aggressive long-horizon certainty;
- update rapidly after each line-up and match;
- track whether the market had already incorporated new information.

11.3 ROBUST DECISIONS
---------------------
The optimiser SHOULD produce at least:

- expected-value plan;
- robust plan;
- high-upside plan;
- no-action baseline.

The robust plan may optimise:

- expected value minus uncertainty penalty;
- conditional value at risk;
- worst-case value over a calibrated scenario set;
- chance constraints;
- regret across model ensembles.

The selected method must state the price paid in nominal expected points for
robustness.

11.4 SENSITIVITY
----------------
Every material decision should be stress-tested against:

- start probability;
- minutes;
- one key market probability;
- price-change probability;
- planning horizon;
- terminal transfer value;
- alternative de-vigging method;
- fixture status;
- ownership estimate;
- chip opportunity assumptions.

A recommendation that reverses under a tiny plausible change is fragile and
must be labelled.

======================================================================
12. DECISION ENGINE ARCHITECTURE
======================================================================

12.1 SEPARATION OF LAYERS
-------------------------
Projection layer:
"What can happen, with what probability?"

Decision layer:
"What should Sebastian do, given preferences and constraints?"

Explanation layer:
"Why, compared with what?"

This separation is mandatory.

12.2 INITIAL SQUAD
------------------
The initial-squad optimiser must consider:

- first-horizon expected points;
- captaincy coverage;
- player minutes security;
- bench viability;
- price structure;
- likely early transfers;
- fixture rotations;
- club concentrations;
- high-upside routes;
- uncertainty;
- expected price changes;
- the value of waiting for information;
- potential early Wildcard policy if rules allow.

The initial team SHOULD not be selected by summing six Gameweeks of expected
points with no future-transfer model.

12.3 ROLLING TRANSFER HORIZON
-----------------------------
Recommended initial planning horizon:
- six Gameweeks for normal transfer planning;
- longer lightweight terminal value;
- shorter horizon near blank/double Gameweeks or season end.

At each decision:
1. optimise a plan over the horizon;
2. implement only the immediate action;
3. retain future steps as contingent guidance;
4. update when information changes.

12.4 FREE TRANSFER VALUE
------------------------
A free transfer has nonzero option value.

The optimiser should account for:
- ability to react to injury;
- ability to attack a future fixture swing;
- ability to execute a two-transfer structure;
- chip preparation;
- price movement;
- uncertainty resolution.

Rolling a transfer may be optimal even if a small immediate expected-points gain
exists.

12.5 TRANSFER HITS
------------------
A hit is not evaluated as simply:

    projected points in - projected points out > hit cost.

It must include:
- horizon points;
- captaincy effects;
- bench effects;
- future transfers avoided;
- price and affordability;
- injury risk;
- option value;
- chip interaction;
- rank utility;
- uncertainty.

12.6 CAPTAINCY
--------------
Captaincy must use the full joint points distribution and start risk.

The decision package should show:
- expected captain points;
- P(blank);
- P(10+ raw points);
- upside quantiles;
- vice-captain activation probability;
- correlation with the rest of the squad;
- effective ownership where relevant;
- expected rank impact by scenario.

12.7 CHIP OPPORTUNITY COST
--------------------------
A chip's value is incremental relative to the best policy without using it now.

Triple Captain:
Value of the extra captain multiplier now versus future opportunities.

Bench Boost:
Bench points gained now, minus squad-structure and transfer costs, versus future
opportunities.

Free Hit:
Temporary optimal squad value minus best non-Free-Hit policy, including hits and
long-term squad damage avoided.

Wildcard:
Value of immediate squad reset over the future horizon versus normal transfers,
including information value and future Wildcard opportunities.

The number, timing and rules of chips remain provisional until 2026/27 launch.

12.8 PRICE DECISIONS
--------------------
Pulse must distinguish:

- predicting a price change;
- valuing the price change;
- recommending an early transfer.

An early transfer is justified only if:

    expected affordability/team-value benefit
    + expected points benefit of acting
    >
    expected cost of injury, rotation, new information and lost flexibility.

12.9 RANK-AWARE STRATEGY
------------------------
Rank awareness begins with a field model, not slogans such as "take more risks."

Pulse should estimate:
- effective ownership;
- captaincy effective ownership;
- score distribution of nearby ranks;
- rank gain/loss by player scenario;
- correlations;
- target-rank probability.

A differential is valuable only when its expected utility exceeds the
alternative. Low ownership is not intrinsically good.

- test provider outage fallback;
- produce initial squad families;

- freeze and record final decision package.

13.2 NORMAL GAMEWEEK CYCLE
--------------------------
After deadline:
- freeze source bundle and recommendation;
- record chosen action;
- do not alter original decision;
- ingest actual line-ups and results for evaluation.

After matches:
- reconcile FPL points;
- update minutes and role evidence;
- evaluate forecasts;
- update price model;
- identify data-quality issues;
- produce model-error decomposition.

Early week:
- update fixture schedule;
- generate base projections;
- identify transfer candidates;
- calculate price risks;
- update chip plan.

Midweek:
- ingest European/cup outcomes;
- update injuries and minutes;
- run alternative transfer paths;
- monitor odds coverage.

Deadline day:
- increase ingest frequency;
- check freshness;
- update start/minutes;
- run optimiser;
- compare against previous recommendation;
- show change reasons;
- perform human review;
- record final action.

13.3 DEADLINE-DAY SERVICE LEVEL
-------------------------------
Target for the MVP:
- complete fresh data build within 10 minutes of scheduled run;
- optimiser result within 2 minutes on standard hardware;
- final decision report generated within 15 minutes;
- explicit data-health banner;
- deterministic rerun;
- fallback report if one provider fails.

These are engineering targets, not guarantees of source availability.

13.4 MATERIAL CHANGE ALERT
--------------------------
A recommendation-change alert should trigger when:

- recommended transfer changes;
- captain changes;
- chip decision changes;
- expected advantage changes beyond a configured threshold;
- a player start probability changes materially;
- a fixture changes;
- critical odds become stale;
- a price change becomes likely enough to affect action;
- data health becomes DEGRADED or BLOCKING.

14. EVALUATION AND SCIENTIFIC VALIDITY
======================================================================

14.1 INFORMATION-SET DISCIPLINE
-------------------------------
Every evaluation must specify an information cut-off.

For a Gameweek deadline D:
- only data with effective availability before D may be used;
- provider timestamps must be interpreted correctly;
- post-deadline line-ups are prohibited;
- closing odds after D are prohibited;
- retrospective injury reports are prohibited;
- corrected FPL points may be used only for outcome labels, not inputs.

14.2 FORECAST EVALUATION
------------------------
Binary events:
- Brier score;
- log loss;
- reliability diagrams;
- calibration slope/intercept;
- expected calibration error with caution.

Count and continuous distributions:
- log score where valid;
- continuous ranked probability score;
- prediction interval coverage;
- probability integral transform diagnostics;
- MAE/RMSE as secondary summaries.

Minutes:
- Brier/log loss for start and 60+;
- CRPS or discrete distribution score;
- calibration by predicted-probability bin;
- error by team, manager and congestion state.

FPL points:
- CRPS;
- interval coverage;
- expected-points bias;
- tail-event calibration;
- component-level error;
- position and price-band diagnostics.

14.3 DECISION EVALUATION
------------------------
Evaluate against:

- no-transfer baseline;
- simple recent-points heuristic;
- official FPL form/FDR heuristic where reproducible;
- market-only projection;
- market plus minutes;
- complete Pulse projection;
- deterministic expected-points optimiser;
- robust optimiser;
- actual chosen action;
- perfect hindsight, labelled only as an unattainable upper bound.

Metrics:
- points versus baseline;
- ex-ante expected gain;
- realised gain distribution;
- transfer regret;
- hit value;
- chip incremental value;
- team-value effect;
- rank effect;
- recommendation stability;
- decision sensitivity.

14.4 ABLATION
-------------
Pulse MUST be able to remove modules to measure contribution.

Examples:
- without market consensus;
- without proprietary minutes;
- without price model;
- without robust optimisation;
- without rank model;
- without chip opportunity cost;
- without ownership.

A module that adds complexity but no stable out-of-sample value should be
removed or simplified.

14.5 MODEL PROMOTION
--------------------
A model version is promoted only when:

- code and data are versioned;
- tests pass;
- no leakage is found;
- calibration is acceptable;
- performance is non-inferior to baseline;
- decision impact is understood;
- failures are documented;
- rollback exists.

A model is not promoted because it fits historical points better in-sample.

14.6 SMALL-SAMPLE CAUTION
-------------------------
One FPL season provides few independent chip decisions and only 38 Gameweeks.

Therefore:
- use multi-season components where rules permit;
- evaluate event-level probabilities;
- use bootstrap or appropriate uncertainty intervals;
- avoid overclaiming chip-model performance;
- maintain prospective evidence;
- keep manual decision journal;
- distinguish model revision from outcome chasing.

15.2 MANUAL OVERRIDES
---------------------
Sebastian may override:
- injury status;
- likely start;
- expected minutes;
- player role;
- market exclusion;
- fixture status;
- risk mode;
- action.

Every override must include:
- author;
- time;
- value;
- reason;
- evidence;
- expiry;
- effect on recommendation.

15.3 DISAGREEMENT
-----------------
When Sebastian rejects a recommendation, the engine should record the reason,
not force agreement.

Useful categories:
- external information not represented;
- preference/risk disagreement;
- trust/data-quality issue;
- usability issue;
- model appears wrong;
- strategic choice;
- execution error.

This creates training data for improving the system and the user interface.

16. RELIABILITY, SECURITY AND COMPLIANCE

16.1 SOURCE RIGHTS
------------------
No provider becomes production-critical until DMFP-04 records:

- access method;
- permitted purpose;
- storage rights;
- derivative-use rights;
- public-display rights;
- rate limits;
- attribution;
- geographic limitations;
- termination risk;
- cost.

16.2 NO UNAUTHORISED SCRAPING
-----------------------------
Pulse MUST use:
- official APIs;
- licensed feeds;
- public endpoints where use is permitted;
- manual imports.

It MUST NOT bypass:
- authentication;
- anti-bot controls;
- paywalls;
- access limits;
- contractual restrictions.

16.3 SECRETS
------------
API keys and FPL session material:
- never committed to Git;
- stored in an encrypted secret manager or protected environment file;
- scoped minimally;
- rotated;
- excluded from logs;
- separated between development and production.

16.4 FPL ACCOUNT SAFETY
-----------------------
The MVP should avoid automated account actions.

If private squad data requires authentication:
- prefer a safe export or read-only route;
- do not store a password;
- document session-token handling;
- add revocation;
- block any write request by default.

16.5 BACKUPS
------------
Required:
- database backup;
- raw object-store backup;
- configuration backup;
- model artefact backup;
- restore test;
- exportable decision journal.

16.6 INCIDENT CLASSES
---------------------
P0:
Wrong rules, wrong deadline, corrupt manager state, or recommendation generated
with future information.

P1:
Critical provider outage, identity mapping failure, or optimizer returning an
illegal action.

P2:
Noncritical missing provider, stale secondary input, slow job.

P3:
UI defect or nonmaterial explanation issue.

P0 and P1 incidents require the decision engine to fail closed.

Status:
Proposed.

Status:
Proposed.

Status:
Proposed.

26. GLOSSARY
======================================================================

Action:
A legal FPL decision: transfers, chip, line-up, bench and captaincy.

Aleatoric uncertainty:
Randomness intrinsic to outcomes.

Backtest:
Historical evaluation using only information available at the simulated time.

Brier score:
A proper score for probabilistic binary forecasts.

Calibration:
Agreement between predicted probabilities and observed frequencies.

Canonical ID:
Pulse's stable identity independent of provider.

Champion mode:
A rank-targeted utility mode intended to increase probability of an exceptional
finish, especially late in the season.

Common random numbers:
Using the same simulated scenarios to compare actions, reducing noise in the
difference.

CRPS:
Continuous ranked probability score for distribution forecasts.

Decision bundle:
All inputs, models, configuration and outputs needed to reproduce a decision.

De-vig:
Remove bookmaker margin from quoted odds.

Effective ownership:
Ownership adjusted for captaincy and chip multipliers in a relevant population.

Epistemic uncertainty:
Uncertainty due to incomplete knowledge or model uncertainty.

Expected points:
The probability-weighted mean of FPL points.

Fail closed:
Stop or lower confidence rather than issue an untrustworthy decision.

Information cut-off:
Latest time at which data may enter a decision.

Market consensus:
Combined fair probability from one or more market sources after processing.

Market-first:
Markets anchor football probabilities; proprietary models supplement and
translate them.

Model predictive control / rolling horizon:
Optimise a future horizon, implement the first action, then repeat.

Option value:
Value of preserving future choices.

Prequential evaluation:
Sequentially evaluating forecasts as they are issued and outcomes arrive.

Proper scoring rule:
A score that rewards reporting honest probabilities.

Robust optimisation:
Optimisation designed to perform acceptably across uncertainty, not only at a
single estimate.

Sharpness:
Concentration of a predictive distribution, desirable subject to calibration.

Source bundle:
Immutable set of data snapshots used by a model run.

Temporal integrity:
Correct representation of what was known and when.

Utility:
Numerical representation of the manager's objective.

The value of market odds is not merely expected goals. It is a time-stamped
information aggregation layer.

distribution, multi-period actions, prices, chips and rank.

Conclusion 6:
Backtesting must be designed before modelling to prevent leakage.

Conclusion 7:
A probabilistic engine must be evaluated with proper scoring rules.

Conclusion 8:
A winning objective requires rank-aware utility eventually, but introducing
variance too early without positive expected target utility is not a strategy.

Conclusion 9:
Rolling-horizon optimisation is the natural operational form.

Conclusion 10:
The immediate build is a thin, complete vertical slice, not twenty incomplete
models.
