PURPOSE
-------
This document defines when DMF Pulse is trying to:

- maximise expected FPL points;
- protect an overall rank;
- reach a target overall rank;
- win or place in a mini-league;
- beat one or more named rivals;
- maximise the probability of a rank-band or prize outcome;
- pursue measured leverage;
- deliberately accept or reduce relative-score variance.

It researches and specifies:

- effective ownership;
- starting ownership;
- captaincy and Triple Captain ownership;
- Bench Boost and automatic-substitution effects;
- differential value;
- player and manager-score correlations;
- template exposure;
- rank protection;
- rank chasing;
- rank and target utility functions;
- probability of reaching a target rank;
- overall-rank cohort modelling;
- mini-league opponent modelling;
- hidden rival transfers and chips;
- season-stage risk preference;
- final-week target optimisation.

The central separation is non-negotiable:

    RAW FOOTBALL AND FPL PROJECTIONS DO NOT CHANGE
    BECAUSE SEBASTIAN IS AHEAD OR BEHIND.

Only the decision utility changes.

A player's:

- expected points;
- minutes distribution;
- goal probability;
- clean-sheet probability;
- price distribution;

must be identical in pure-points and rank-aware modes when they use the same
information/model version.

The rank module consumes those unchanged distributions and asks:

    "How does this action perform relative to the managers and rank target that
    matter?"

GOVERNING PRINCIPLE
-------------------
Rank is a relative outcome generated by:

- Sebastian's current points and tie-break state;
- Sebastian's future actions;
- rivals' current states and future actions;
- the same shared football outcomes;
- player overlap and captain multipliers;
- chips;
- transfer hits;
- rank tie rules;
- uncertainty in the wider field.

Effective ownership is a useful local sensitivity statistic.

It is not a complete rank model.

The selected architecture is:

    UNCHANGED JOINT PLAYER-POINT SCENARIOS
    + COHORT-SPECIFIC MANAGER MULTIPLIERS
    + OPPONENT ACTION MODELS
    + SHARED-SCENARIO RANK SIMULATION
    + EXPLICIT TARGET UTILITY
    = RANK-AWARE DECISION VALUE

IMPORTANT BEHAVIOURAL CAUTION
-----------------------------
Being behind does not mechanically imply:

    "take more risk."

Being ahead does not mechanically imply:

    "copy the template."

Tournament research shows that interim rank can affect risk-taking behaviour,
including greater risk among participants close behind leaders, but observed
risk-taking is not necessarily optimal and pressure can reduce performance.

Pulse will calculate whether a change in mean, correlation or tail genuinely
improves the chosen target probability.

It will not imitate tournament behaviour merely because it is common.

CRITICAL PRE-LAUNCH STATUS
--------------------------
The 2026/27 FPL game had not launched at the research cut-off. Official Premier
League material said it would launch later in summer 2026.

Therefore:

1. Target-season manager population, ownership, rank, prizes and league data are
   not yet available.
2. Target tie-break, transfer counting, chip multipliers and API fields must be

3. Reference tie rules, ownership definitions and manager endpoints are not
   automatically authoritative for 2026/27.
4. Cohort effective ownership cannot activate until lawful, representative

5. Rank-aware optimisation must fall back to pure expected-points mode when:
   - target rules are unknown;
   - opponent/EO data are invalid;

   - Sebastian has not selected a competition objective.
6. No mass manager scraping is authorised by this document.
7. Exact mini-league/rival data use remains subject to FPL terms, privacy,

======================================================================
0. EXECUTIVE STRATEGY DECISION
======================================================================

0.1 DEFAULT OBJECTIVE
---------------------
The default objective remains:

    maximise expected remaining-season FPL points.

Rank-aware utility is activated only when:

- Sebastian selects a target;
- the target has a defined utility;
- opponent/rank data pass confidence gates;
- rank-aware decisions are likely to differ materially;
- DMFP-10 season-stage policy permits it.

0.2 SELECTED SEASON STRUCTURE
-----------------------------
EARLY SEASON:
Expected points and flexibility.

MIDDLE SEASON:
Expected points remain primary; measured leverage is a secondary/epsilon
criterion among near-optimal plans.

LATE SEASON:
Rank-aware expected utility can become primary when target probability is
decision-sensitive.

FINAL WEEKS:
Explicit probability of reaching/protecting the selected target rank or beating
specified rivals.

This structure is state-based, not a rigid Gameweek slogan.

A new manager, injury crisis or uncertain fixture schedule can make a late state
structurally uncertain.

A target may become mathematically secure or unreachable earlier.

0.3 SELECTED UTILITY IMPLEMENTATION
-----------------------------------
Rather than hide rank and points inside one arbitrary weighted sum, Pulse uses
epsilon-constraint and lexicographic modes.

PURE_POINTS:
Maximise expected remaining points.

MEASURED_LEVERAGE:
Require expected points within delta_points of the pure-points optimum, then
maximise target/rank utility.

TARGET_RANK:
Maximise P(final rank <= R_target), subject to:
- expected-points floor;
- squad fragility constraints;
- robust-regret constraints.

RANK_PROTECTION:
Maximise P(final rank remains <= R_protect) or P(beat protected rivals), subject
to an expected-points floor.

MINI_LEAGUE_WIN:
Maximise P(final mini-league rank = 1), with target-specific tie rules.

PRIZE/BAND:
Maximise expected user-defined prize/rank-band utility.

FINAL_WEEK:
Maximise exact target utility; use expected points and next-best target as
tie-breakers.

0.4 NO AUTOMATIC VARIANCE PREFERENCE
------------------------------------
Risk preference emerges from the target utility and opponent distribution.

If behind:

- lower correlation can increase catch-up probability;
- higher variance can help;
- but a large expected-points sacrifice can reduce target probability.

If ahead:

- higher overlap can reduce relative variance;
- but copying an inferior player can reduce protection.

The engine optimises the target probability directly.

0.5 EFFECTIVE OWNERSHIP DECISION
--------------------------------
Pulse computes multiple EO concepts:

- saved-team EO;
- projected predeadline EO;
- scenario/appearance-adjusted EO;
- realised EO;
- cohort-specific EO.

The scenario/appearance-adjusted EO is the preferred rank-sensitivity measure.

0.6 OPPONENT MODELLING DECISION
-------------------------------
Named mini-league rivals:
individual hierarchical policy models.

Large overall-rank field:
stratified synthetic manager cohorts and weighted sampling.

Both use the same DMFP-09 player-points scenarios as Sebastian.

0.7 RANK SIMULATION DECISION
----------------------------
For every shared scenario:

1. sample/resolve Sebastian's plan;
2. sample opponent transfers, lineups, captains and chips;
3. score every represented manager from the same player-point vector;
4. add transfer hits;
5. update cumulative points and tie-break state;
6. calculate mini-league and/or overall rank;
7. evaluate target utility.

0.8 DIFFERENTIAL DECISION
-------------------------
A differential is not valuable merely because ownership is low.

Selected definition:

    differential value
      =
      rank/target utility gain of choosing the action
      relative to the best relevant alternative,

under the opponent model and shared scenarios.

Low ownership is one feature explaining the gain.

0.9 TEMPLATE DECISION
---------------------
Template exposure is measured through:

- player-multiplier overlap;
- benchmark-score covariance;
- tracking error;
- cohort beta;
- captain/chip overlap;
- joint ownership clusters.

Template is neither good nor bad.

It can provide:

- expected points through strong popular players;
- protection when ahead;
- insufficient leverage when chasing;
- concentration risk if the cohort is wrong.

0.10 OUTPUT
-----------
Every rank-aware recommendation must show:

- unchanged points projection;
- points-mode plan;
- rank-aware plan;
- expected-points sacrifice/gain;
- target-probability change;
- EO/leverage change;
- template exposure;
- opponent assumptions;
- action stability;
- confidence.

======================================================================
1. DEFINITIONS
======================================================================

1.1 RAW OWNERSHIP
-----------------
Percentage of a cohort whose 15-player permanent or temporary squad contains the
player.

1.2 STARTING OWNERSHIP
----------------------
Percentage of a cohort that saves the player in the starting XI.

1.3 CAPTAINCY OWNERSHIP
-----------------------
Percentage selecting the player as captain, separated into:

- ordinary captain;
- Triple Captain;
- other future captain multipliers.

1.4 VICE-CAPTAIN OWNERSHIP
--------------------------
Percentage selecting the player as vice.

Vice ownership is not full effective ownership because it activates
conditionally.

1.5 COUNTED MULTIPLIER
----------------------
Number of copies of a player's raw FPL points counted in a manager's score in a
specific scenario.

Examples under reference mechanics:

- ordinary bench, unused: 0;
- normal counted player: 1;
- captain: 2;
- Triple Captain: 3;
- Bench Boost bench player: 1;
- vice after captain failure: captain multiplier;
- auto-subbed bench player: 1.

1.6 EFFECTIVE OWNERSHIP
-----------------------
Official FPL glossary gives the common simplified definition:

    percentage of teams starting a player
    + the amount who captained him.

Pulse generalises this to exact counted multipliers and chips.

1.7 DIFFERENTIAL
----------------
Official FPL glossary describes a differential as a low-owned player, commonly
under 10%, with points potential.

Pulse retains that label for communication but does not use 10% as a decision
boundary.

1.8 TEMPLATE
------------
Official FPL glossary describes a template as a squad containing highly owned
players.

Pulse defines a cohort-specific joint distribution of popular player,
captaincy and chip choices.

1.9 LEVERAGE
------------
Difference between Sebastian's counted multiplier and the cohort's effective
multiplier.

1.10 RANK PROTECTION
--------------------
Actions chosen to increase the probability of remaining inside a target
rank/band or ahead of rivals.

1.11 RANK CHASING
-----------------
Actions chosen to increase the probability of crossing an aspirational target
from behind.

1.12 TARGET
-----------
Examples:

- overall top 10,000;
- overall top 100;
- overall win;
- mini-league win;
- finish above manager j;
- top three of a league;
- maintain current rank band;
- maximise official prize utility.

1.13 COHORT
-----------
The group whose ownership/action distribution matters:

- all managers;
- current-rank band;
- top 10k/100k/1m;
- prior elite managers;
- country/club league;
- mini-league;
- named rivals;
- custom opponent set.

2. DATA, ACCESS AND PRIVACY
======================================================================

2.1 OFFICIAL OVERALL OWNERSHIP
------------------------------
The official player catalogue has historically exposed rounded
`selected_by_percent`.

This is raw overall squad ownership, not:

- starting ownership;
- captaincy;
- effective ownership;
- target-rank ownership.

2.2 MANAGER PICKS
-----------------
Cohort EO requires manager-level saved picks, captain, vice, chip and bench
information.

Exact target-season endpoint/access behaviour must be verified.

2.3 PREDEADLINE HIDDEN ACTIONS
------------------------------
Rival current transfers, captain and final lineup are generally not known before
the deadline.

Pulse must predict them.

It cannot use postdeadline saved teams as predeadline features.

2.4 POSTDEADLINE OBSERVATION
----------------------------
After the deadline, official/public picks may become labels for:

- opponent model;
- projected EO calibration;
- rank evaluation.

2.5 MINI-LEAGUE
---------------
Named rivals can be modelled only when:

- Sebastian is authorised to view/use the data;
- FPL terms permit it;
- access is rate-limited;
- personal data are restricted;
- outputs remain private unless separately permitted.

2.6 NO MASS SCRAPING
--------------------
DMFP-04's restriction remains:

- no scraping millions of teams;
- no quota/account evasion;
- no protected-page automation;
- no public redistribution.

2.7 SAMPLE PROVENANCE
---------------------
Every cohort estimate stores:

- population definition;
- sample frame;
- sample size;
- selection method;
- weights;
- collection time;
- endpoint/source;
- missingness;
- design effect;
- licence/privacy state.

2.8 FALLBACK
------------
If captaincy/EO sample is unavailable:

- overall raw ownership remains descriptive;
- exact named rivals may be used if valid;
- rank-aware overall optimisation confidence falls;
- pure expected-points mode remains.

3. COHORT DEFINITIONS
======================================================================

3.1 OVERALL
-----------
All active FPL entries, subject to denominator uncertainty and inactive/dead
teams.

3.2 RANK-BAND COHORT
--------------------
Managers near a target or current rank.

Examples:

- 1–10k;
- 10k–100k;
- 100k–1m;
- adaptive points/rank neighbourhood.

3.3 ELITE COHORT
----------------
Optional:

- prior-season top 1%;
- official elite league;
- repeated high finishers.

Must be target-season available and legally sampled.

3.4 MINI-LEAGUE
---------------
Exact named membership and current standings.

3.5 NEAREST RIVALS
------------------
Managers whose current/future score distribution is most relevant.

3.6 CUSTOM
----------
User-selected managers/rank targets.

3.7 COHORT DRIFT
----------------
Rank-band membership changes every Gameweek.

A top-100k cohort at GW10 is not the same people as the final top 100k.

Record definition:

CURRENT_RANK_BAND
FIXED_MANAGER_PANEL
FINAL_OUTCOME_COHORT_FOR_EVALUATION_ONLY

Do not train on final cohort membership as a live feature.

3.8 WEIGHTING
-------------
Use sampling/poststratification weights to represent the intended cohort.

======================================================================
4. EFFECTIVE OWNERSHIP MATHEMATICS
======================================================================

4.1 SAVED MULTIPLIER
--------------------
For player i, manager j and saved team state:

    m_saved[i,j]

Examples:

- ordinary non-BB bench: 0;
- ordinary starter: 1;
- normal captain: 2;
- TC captain: 3;
- BB bench: 1.

This ignores future autosubs and captain fallback.

4.2 SAVED EO
------------
For cohort c with manager weights a_j summing one:

    EO_saved[i,c]
      =
      100 x sum_j a_j m_saved[i,j]

4.3 SCENARIO MULTIPLIER
-----------------------
For football/manager scenario s:

    m[i,j,s]

is the actual counted multiplier after:

- appearances;
- automatic substitutions;
- captain/vice fallback;
- chip effects;
- fixture postponement;
- scoring rules.

4.4 SCENARIO EO
---------------
    EO[i,c,s]
      =
      100 x sum_j a_j m[i,j,s]

4.5 EXPECTED APPEARANCE-ADJUSTED EO
-----------------------------------
    EEO[i,c]
      =
      sum_s w_s EO[i,c,s]

This is the preferred predeadline rank-sensitivity EO.

4.6 EO INTERVAL
---------------
Uncertainty comes from:

- sampled manager actions;
- hidden transfers/captains;
- player appearances;
- chips;
- sample design;
- cohort size.

Output:

- mean;
- median;
- p10/p90 or credible interval;
- scenario distribution.

4.7 REALISED EO
---------------
After final scores/autosubs:

    EO_realised

from actual manager multipliers for the observed cohort.

Used as label/evaluation, not a predeadline input.

4.8 EO ABOVE 100
----------------
Valid because captaincy adds copies.
Triple Captain can increase further.

4.9 VICE CONTRIBUTION
---------------------
Saved vice ownership is reported separately.

Scenario EO automatically includes the vice when captain fallback occurs.

4.10 BENCH BOOST
----------------
Bench players count in saved EO for BB teams.

Normal bench players can enter scenario EO through automatic substitutions.

4.11 FREE HIT
-------------
Temporary-squad ownership belongs to the Gameweek cohort state.

Permanent-squad ownership remains separate.

4.12 MULTIPLE FIXTURES
----------------------
EO multiplier is a Gameweek scoring multiplier, while player points include all
fixtures.

======================================================================
5. CAPTAINCY AND CHIP OWNERSHIP
======================================================================

5.1 CAPTAIN OWNERSHIP
---------------------
Report:

- normal captain percentage;
- TC captain percentage;
- total named-captain percentage;
- effective extra multiplier.

5.2 CAPTAIN EO CONTRIBUTION
---------------------------
If ordinary captain and TC are separate:

    captain_extra_EO
      =
      normal_captain_pct
      + 2 x TC_captain_pct

assuming percentages are measured as proportions of the same cohort.

Total saved EO also includes starting/counting ownership.

5.3 VICE
--------
Report:

- vice percentage;
- expected fallback percentage;
- correlated captain/vice failure.

5.4 CHIP OWNERSHIP
------------------
For each cohort/Gameweek:

- chip usage rate;
- chip-specific manager actions;
- uncertainty.

5.5 FUTURE CHIP
----------------
Multiplier/effect derived from DMFP-13's generic chip grammar.

5.6 CAPTAINCY SOURCE UNCERTAINTY
-------------------------------
Cohort captaincy can change sharply close to deadline.
Use time-stamped projected distributions, not stale prior-week rates.

======================================================================
6. LOCAL LEVERAGE AND RANK SENSITIVITY
======================================================================

6.1 SEBASTIAN MULTIPLIER
------------------------
For scenario s:

    M_i,s

actual counted multiplier under Sebastian's plan.

6.2 PLAYER LEVERAGE
-------------------
Against cohort c:

    L_i,c,s
      =
      M_i,s - EO_i,c,s / 100

6.3 LOCAL RELATIVE SCORE
------------------------
If X_i,s is raw player Gameweek points:

    D_c,s
      =
      sum_i L_i,c,s X_i,s
      - relative transfer-hit difference
      + other configured manager-score differences.

This equals Sebastian's manager score minus the cohort's average score when the
multiplier representation and cohort mean are complete.

6.4 POSITIVE/NEGATIVE LEVERAGE
------------------------------
Positive:
Sebastian counts more copies than cohort.

Negative:
cohort counts more.

6.5 HIGH EO
-----------
If EO > Sebastian multiplier:

- a haul can reduce relative score;
- blank/negative points can improve relative score.

This is relative, not absolute projection.

6.6 CAPTAIN LEVERAGE
--------------------
Captaining a player changes multiplier and leverage.

The relevant leverage includes vice fallback scenarios.

6.7 LEVERAGE IS NOT UTILITY
---------------------------
A player with huge positive leverage can still be a bad decision if expected
points are poor.

6.8 ACTION LEVERAGE
-------------------
Compare full plans:

    Delta D_s
      =
      relative score(plan A,s)
      - relative score(plan B,s)

Use this rather than adding player leverage independently.

======================================================================
7. DIFFERENTIAL VALUE
======================================================================

7.1 COMMUNICATION LABEL
-----------------------
A low-owned player can be called a differential.

7.2 DECISION DEFINITION
-----------------------
For action a versus b:

    differential_value(a,b)
      =
      E[
        U(rank or target under a)
        - U(rank or target under b)
      ]

7.3 SOURCES OF DIFFERENTIAL VALUE
---------------------------------
- higher expected points;
- lower cohort ownership;
- captain multiplier;
- low correlation with rivals;
- fixture/position replacement;
- rival high-EO alternative sold/not owned;
- chip interaction.

7.4 FALSE DIFFERENTIAL
----------------------
Low ownership caused by:

- low expected minutes;
- injury;
- bad fixture;
- poor value;

does not create useful leverage.

7.5 POPULAR DIFFERENTIAL
------------------------
A highly owned player can still create positive leverage if Sebastian captains
them while the cohort mainly owns without captaining.

7.6 RELATIVE TO COHORT
----------------------
A player can be template overall and differential in a mini-league, or the
reverse.

======================================================================
8. TEMPLATE EXPOSURE
======================================================================

8.1 MULTIPLIER VECTOR
---------------------
Manager j's scenario multiplier vector:

    m_j,s = (m_1,j,s, ..., m_P,j,s)

8.2 SAVED OVERLAP
-----------------
Metrics:

- weighted Jaccard squad overlap;
- starting-XI overlap;
- captain overlap;
- multiplier cosine similarity;
- chip overlap.

8.3 SCORE BENCHMARK
-------------------
Let:

    Y_s = Sebastian future score
    B_c,s = average cohort future score.

8.4 TEMPLATE BETA
-----------------
    beta_template
      =
      Cov(Y,B_c) / Var(B_c)

if benchmark variance > 0.

Interpretation:
sensitivity to cohort score, not a finance claim of causal market risk.

8.5 TRACKING ERROR
------------------
    TE = SD(Y - B_c)

Lower tracking error:
more protection/less leverage.

8.6 EXPECTED RELATIVE SCORE
---------------------------
    alpha_relative = E[Y - B_c]

Do not pursue low tracking error with negative relative expectation unless the
target utility justifies it.

8.7 TEMPLATE CONCENTRATION
--------------------------
Measure:

- effective number of popular players;
- cohort score concentration;
- captain concentration;
- joint ownership clusters;
- share of cohort variance explained by top players.

8.8 HERDING
-----------
O'Brien, Gleeson and O'Sullivan observed template-like similarities/herding
among FPL decisions while also finding long-term planning and consistently good
decisions important.

Pulse treats template as an emergent benchmark, not evidence that every popular
choice is optimal.

======================================================================
9. PLAYER AND MANAGER-SCORE CORRELATIONS
======================================================================

9.1 SHARED FOOTBALL SCENARIOS
-----------------------------
Every manager must be scored using the same DMFP-09 player scenario.

This preserves:

- shared player ownership;
- teammate correlation;
- opposing-player correlation;
- clean-sheet stacks;
- captaincy;
- chips;
- postponements.

9.2 NO INDEPENDENT MANAGER DRAWS
--------------------------------
Do not independently sample each manager's player points.

That would destroy rank variance and overlap effects.

9.3 RELATIVE VARIANCE
---------------------
For rival j:

    Var(Y - R_j)
      =
      Var(Y) + Var(R_j) - 2 Cov(Y,R_j)

Overlap can materially reduce relative variance.

9.4 DIFFERENTIAL CORRELATION
----------------------------
A low-owned player highly correlated with a rival's player may offer less
effective leverage than ownership alone suggests.

9.5 STACKS
----------
- double/triple defence increases shared clean-sheet exposure;
- multiple attackers share team-goal environment;
- attacker against rival defender changes relative tails.

9.6 CHIPS
---------
A rival's TC/BB/FH creates temporary covariance changes.

======================================================================
10. OFFICIAL RANK AND TIE MECHANICS
======================================================================

10.1 POINTS
-----------
Classic rank is primarily determined by cumulative total FPL points.

10.2 REFERENCE TIE RULE
-----------------------
The current official rules surface states that tied teams are positioned by
fewer transfers made, with Wildcard and Free Hit transfers excluded from the
transfer total.

Target-season verification is required.

10.3 SAME TIE-BREAK
-------------------
If points and counted transfers are equal, managers can share a rank under
reference guidance.

Official prize rules may add registration-time tie-breaks for prizes.
Prize utility must use the exact active prize rules.

10.4 TRANSFER COUNT STATE
-------------------------
Opponent simulation must include:

- ordinary transfers counted for tie-break;
- chip-excluded transfers per active rules;
- future transfer actions.

10.5 RANK CALCULATION
---------------------
For manager j with final points P_j and tie count T_j:

j outranks Sebastian if:

- P_j > P_S; or
- P_j = P_S and T_j < T_S;

subject to target rules.

Shared ties handled exactly.

======================================================================
11. NAMED RIVAL AND MINI-LEAGUE MODEL
======================================================================

11.1 EXACT MEMBERSHIP
---------------------
Represent every named rival separately.

11.2 OBSERVED STATE
-------------------
Potentially known:

- cumulative points;
- prior saved squad;
- prior captain/vice;
- chips used;
- transfer history/count;
- team value/public history;
- prior actions.

Current predeadline transfers and tactics are latent.

11.3 RIVAL TYPE
---------------
Hierarchical latent attributes:

- expected-points quality;
- popularity/template sensitivity;
- recency/form sensitivity;
- price-change sensitivity;
- hit propensity;
- roll-transfer propensity;
- captain concentration;
- differential preference;
- chip timing;
- information/source quality;
- risk response to rank.

11.4 ACTION SET
----------------
Generate plausible rival actions through a simplified DMFP-12/13 action
generator:

- no transfer;
- common transfer;
- high-projection transfer;
- price move;
- hit;
- chip;
- captain alternatives.

11.5 CHOICE MODEL
-----------------
Conditional-logit/softmax or hierarchical random-utility model:

    P(a_j | state)
      proportional to
      exp(
        theta_j_points x perceived_points
        + theta_j_pop x popularity
        + theta_j_form x recent_points
        + theta_j_price x price_pressure
        + theta_j_risk x relative-risk_feature
        + chip terms
      )

Random utility prevents treating rivals as perfectly rational Pulse clones.

11.6 MANAGER BELIEF
-------------------
Rivals may use noisier/different forecasts.

Model perceived points as:

- public consensus;
- market/FPL form;
- noisy Pulse projection;
- manager-type bias.

11.7 UPDATE
-----------
After deadline:

- actual action is observed;
- update rival-type posterior;
- do not revise previous prediction.

11.8 EXACT MINI-LEAGUE SIMULATION
--------------------------------
For every scenario:

- sample each rival action;
- score exact team;
- update league table/tie-break;
- calculate Sebastian's mini-league rank.

11.9 SMALL LEAGUE
----------------
No cohort approximation is needed if all rivals can be represented.

11.10 HEAD-TO-HEAD
------------------
A head-to-head league requires separate fixture, match and playoff utility.

This document's main production target is classic cumulative-points leagues.
H2H is an extension with explicit rules.

======================================================================
12. OVERALL-FIELD OPPONENT MODEL
======================================================================

12.1 SCALE
----------
Millions of entries cannot all be simulated individually on every optimiser
evaluation.

12.2 STRATIFICATION
-------------------
Partition managers by:

- current points/rank band;
- activity;
- chip inventory/use;
- transfer style;
- template cluster;
- historic/elite status where permitted.

12.3 SYNTHETIC MANAGERS
-----------------------
Sample representative manager states/actions with weights.

12.4 OWNERSHIP MODEL
--------------------
A Dirichlet-multinomial or logistic-normal hierarchical model can represent
correlated player selections.

Haugh and Singal explicitly model opponent fantasy-team selections using a
Dirichlet-multinomial process with Dirichlet regressions in a contest setting.

FPL requires additional budget, position, club and transfer constraints.

12.5 CONSTRAINED GENERATION
--------------------------
Options:

- sample latent player preference then solve a noisy legal squad optimiser;
- generate from observed manager action transitions;
- conditional random-utility choice over candidate legal actions;
- importance sample around target-rank managers.

12.6 CAPTAIN/CHIP
----------------
Separate hierarchical choice models conditional on squad and fixture.

12.7 DEAD/INACTIVE TEAMS
------------------------
Overall ownership includes less-active entries.

Rank-band cohorts should model activity state.

12.8 COHORT TRANSITION
----------------------
Managers move between rank bands.
Do not assume a fixed rank-band sample over the entire season.

12.9 FIELD COUNT
----------------
Estimate active/total population from official data with uncertainty.

12.10 REPRESENTATION ERROR
--------------------------
Validate synthetic distributions against:

- raw ownership;
- captaincy sample;
- chip use;
- transfer rates;
- score/rank transitions;
- overlap statistics.

======================================================================
13. OVERALL RANK SIMULATION
======================================================================

13.1 DIRECT REPRESENTED SAMPLE
------------------------------
For sampled manager j:

    FinalPoints_j,s
      =
      current_points_j
      + future_manager_points_j,s

minus/add hits as appropriate.

13.2 TARGET POINTS DISTRIBUTION
-------------------------------
For target rank R*:

- simulate points threshold occupied by rank R*;
- include tie count;
- output threshold PMF.

13.3 FULL WEIGHTED SAMPLE
------------------------
Calculate weighted empirical CDF of final manager points conditional on shared
football scenario and action distribution.

13.4 OUTRANK PROBABILITY BY STRATUM
-----------------------------------
For stratum h:

    p_h,s
      =
      P(manager from h outranks Sebastian | shared scenario s)

Number outranking:

    O_h,s

Candidate approximations:

- binomial;
- beta-binomial for within-stratum dependence/overdispersion;
- weighted empirical count;
- common-factor conditional binomial.

13.5 FINAL RANK
---------------
    Rank_S,s
      =
      1
      + exact named managers outranking
      + sum_h O_h,s

with tie handling.

13.6 DEPENDENCE
----------------
Conditional independence across managers is imperfect because:

- teams overlap;
- ownership clusters;
- common action information;
- shared player outcomes.

Use:

- shared football scenario;
- cluster/factor action draws;
- beta-binomial/empirical calibration.

13.7 IMPORTANCE SAMPLING
------------------------
For extreme targets such as overall win/top 100:

- ordinary samples may contain few relevant managers;
- oversample high-current-rank/high-skill states;
- use weights;
- preserve tail managers.

13.8 RANK UNCERTAINTY
---------------------
Separate:

- football outcomes;
- rival action;
- cohort sampling;
- population/denominator;
- model;
- Monte Carlo.

======================================================================
14. TARGET-RANK PROBABILITY
======================================================================

14.1 OVERALL TARGET
-------------------
For target R*:

    P_target(a)
      =
      sum_s w_s 1[Rank_S(a,s) <= R*]

14.2 MINI-LEAGUE WIN
--------------------
    P_win(a)
      =
      sum_s w_s 1[MiniLeagueRank_S(a,s) = 1]

14.3 BEAT RIVAL
----------------
    P_beat_j(a)
      =
      P(
        final points/tie state outranks rival j
      )

14.4 PROTECT BAND
-----------------
    P_protect(a)
      =
      P(final rank <= protected boundary)

14.5 TARGET SHORTFALL
---------------------
- expected points gap;
- median gap;
- CVaR/expected shortfall to target threshold;
- probability within K points.

14.6 MULTIPLE TARGETS
---------------------
Piecewise utility:

- win mini-league;
- otherwise top three;
- otherwise maximise points.

Or lexicographic priorities.

User must specify.

14.7 MONTE CARLO ERROR
----------------------
Report MCSE/ESS for target probability.

Extreme small probabilities may require rare-event methods.

14.8 IMPOSSIBLE/SECURE
----------------------
If target probability is near zero or one across all feasible plans:

- chasing/protection utility may have little decision value;
- switch to secondary target or points objective;
- do not force reckless/defensive moves.

======================================================================
15. UTILITY FUNCTIONS
======================================================================

15.1 POINTS
-----------
    U_points = final cumulative points

Default.

15.2 TARGET INDICATOR
---------------------
    U_target(R)
      =
      1[R <= R*]

Directly maximises target probability.

15.3 SOFT TARGET
----------------
For optimisation smoothing/reporting:

    U_soft(R)
      =
      logistic((R* - R)/tau)

or use points gap to simulated target threshold.

Exact target probability remains reported.

15.4 RANK BAND
--------------
    U_band(R)
      =
      v_k for R in band k.

Values are user-defined.

15.5 MINI-LEAGUE
----------------
- win indicator;
- top-k;
- prize schedule;
- points-based friendly utility.

15.6 EXPECTED RANK
------------------
Minimising expected rank is a possible diagnostic.

It is not equivalent to maximising P(top R*) and can overweight/underweight
tails.

15.7 EXPECTED PERCENTILE
------------------------
Useful scale-independent diagnostic.

15.8 PRIZE UTILITY
------------------
Official or mini-league prizes can define U(R), subject to applicable rules.

Pulse does not assume monetary risk preferences.

15.9 LEXICOGRAPHIC
------------------
Examples:

1. maximise P(win mini-league);
2. among near-equal plans, maximise expected points;
3. among ties, minimise robust regret.

15.10 EPSILON CONSTRAINT
------------------------
To avoid reckless target chasing:

    maximise P_target(a)

subject to:

    E[points(a)]
      >= E[points(points-optimal)] - delta_points

and:

    robust regret <= limit.

15.11 UTILITY VERSION
---------------------
Every decision stores exact target, function, parameters and priority.

======================================================================
16. RISK PREFERENCE FROM RELATIVE POSITION
======================================================================

16.1 TWO-MANAGER INTUITION
--------------------------
Let current gap be g = Sebastian points - rival points.
Future relative score D = Sebastian future score - rival future score.

Win if:

    g + D > 0

or tie rule favours Sebastian.

The optimal action maximises:

    P(g + D > 0)

not mean(D) alone.

16.2 AHEAD
----------
When g > 0:

- lower relative variance can increase win probability;
- positive expected relative score still matters;
- overlap/captain matching can protect;
- copying an inferior rival action can reduce win probability.

16.3 BEHIND
-----------
When g < 0:

- lower correlation/differentiation may increase chance;
- greater relative variance may help;
- losing too much mean can reduce catch-up probability.

16.4 FAR BEHIND
---------------
If all feasible actions have tiny target probability:

- choose secondary target;
- maximise expected rank/points;
- or accept explicit long-shot utility.

Do not take arbitrary low-owned punts.

16.5 NEAR LEADER
----------------
Tournament evidence finds risk-taking may peak among participants trailing just
behind leaders, but also reports performance pressure effects.

Pulse treats this as behavioural evidence and models rivals' risk response.
Sebastian's action remains expected-utility optimal.

======================================================================
17. EARLY-SEASON POLICY
======================================================================

17.1 DEFAULT
------------
Pure expected remaining-season points and flexibility.

17.2 WHY
-------
- many Gameweeks remain;
- rank distribution is wide;
- current rank is noisy;
- opponent actions have long recourse;
- early differentials are not necessary for a target that can be reached by
  sustained points.

17.3 EO USE
-----------
Use EO for:

- diagnostics;
- rank-impact explanation;
- captaincy risk;
- opponent-model calibration.

Do not sacrifice expected points for overall-rank protection/chasing.

17.4 MINI-LEAGUE
----------------
Even in a mini-league, early target utility usually resembles expected points
because many future opportunities remain.

A special user-specified prize/constraint can override.

17.5 FLEXIBILITY
----------------
DMFP-10 flexibility and model uncertainty dominate small rank effects.

17.6 TEMPLATE
-------------
Popular high-EV players are selected because of projections, not because they
are popular.

Low-owned high-EV players are also selected.

17.7 OUTPUT
-----------
Rank-aware alternative shown only when materially different and data confidence
adequate.

======================================================================
18. MIDDLE-SEASON POLICY
======================================================================

18.1 DEFAULT
------------
Expected points primary with measured leverage.

18.2 EPSILON POLICY
-------------------
First solve points-optimal plan.

Among plans within configured expected-season/horizon points epsilon:

- maximise target probability;
- reduce/raise template tracking as target requires;
- preserve robustness.

18.3 LEVERAGE BUDGET
--------------------
No fixed number of differentials.

Measure plan-level target change.

18.4 CHIP
---------
Chip opportunity remains DMFP-13; rank utility can influence timing/captain
only through explicit target value.

18.5 RANK SIGNAL
----------------
Use:

- current gap;
- remaining variance;
- opponent skill/actions;
- target probability;
- rank-model confidence.

======================================================================
19. LATE-SEASON POLICY
======================================================================

19.1 ACTIVATION
---------------
Rank-aware utility can become primary when:

- target specified;
- remaining horizon shorter;
- current gap is decision-relevant;
- rival/field model is reliable enough;
- action changes target probability.

19.2 TARGET MODE
----------------
Maximise target probability with expected-points and robust floors.

19.3 PROTECTION
---------------
If ahead/protecting:

- consider high-EO/captain overlap;
- reduce tracking error;
- preserve expected points floor;
- avoid correlated nonappearance/fixture risk.

19.4 CHASING
------------
If behind:

- seek positive leverage and lower rival correlation;
- consider different captain/chip;
- retain expected utility floor;
- avoid fake differentials.

19.5 CHIPS
---------
Rank utility can make a lower-mean but higher-target-probability TC/FH/BB plan
optimal near the end.

Opportunity cost and remaining inventory still apply.

======================================================================
20. FINAL-WEEKS POLICY
======================================================================

20.1 EXPLICIT TARGET
--------------------
Required:

- exact overall/mini-league target;
- tie state;
- remaining chips/transfers;
- target utility.

20.2 FINAL GAMEWEEK
-------------------
No future team value/flexibility after the deadline except prize/tie rules.

Optimise the exact rank/target utility over the Gameweek scenario distribution.

20.3 CAPTAIN
------------
Captain can differ from expected-points captain if it improves target
probability.

20.4 RIVAL CAPTAIN
------------------
Matching can protect; opposing can chase.

Use rival captain probabilities, not assumptions.

20.5 TRANSFERS
-------------
Transfer hits affect:

- points;
- tie-break transfer count;
- player leverage;
- target probability.

20.6 CHIP
---------
Use remaining eligible chips if target utility improves; expiry alone is not
value if another action/chip is superior.

20.7 MULTIPLE RIVALS
--------------------
The best action against one rival may worsen chance of beating another.

Simulate entire league/prize structure.

20.8 OUTPUT
-----------
- P(target) by plan;
- expected rank;
- rank PMF;
- exact rival win matrix;
- target threshold points;
- expected-points cost;
- action-switch thresholds.

======================================================================
21. RANK PROTECTION
======================================================================

21.1 OBJECTIVE
--------------
Maximise:

- P(rank <= protected rank);
- P(win/hold mini-league);
- or minimise target shortfall/downside.

21.2 OVERLAP
------------
Increasing overlap with relevant rivals can reduce relative variance.

21.3 EO PROTECTION
-----------------
Owning/captaining high-EO players can reduce exposure to rank-killing returns.

But plan selection remains utility based.

21.4 PROTECTION FAILURE
-----------------------
Blindly copying:

- sacrifices mean;
- can preserve a shrinking lead poorly;
- may copy a rival's mistake;
- can concentrate fixture/injury risk.

21.5 PROTECTION PLAN
--------------------
DMFP-12 conservative plan can use:

- target-protection utility;
- expected-points epsilon;
- field-11 chance;
- robust regret;
- tracking error.

21.6 INSURANCE COST
-------------------
Report expected-points cost per percentage point of protection probability.

======================================================================
22. RANK CHASING
======================================================================

22.1 OBJECTIVE
--------------
Maximise target probability, not ownership novelty.

22.2 LEVERS
-----------
- transfer differential;
- captain multiplier;
- chip timing;
- player correlation;
- formation/squad exposure;
- hit;
- bench.

22.3 CORRELATION
----------------
A plan can have similar mean but lower correlation with leaders, improving
catch-up probability.

22.4 MEAN FLOOR
---------------
Maintain expected-points floor unless user explicitly chooses extreme
long-shot mode.

22.5 HIT
--------
A hit increases initial gap.

It is rational only if the relative distribution/utility compensates.

22.6 MULTIPLE DIFFERENTIALS
---------------------------
Too many low-mean differentials can collapse target probability.

Plan-level simulation controls.

22.7 CHASING LEADER'S UNOWNED PLAYER
------------------------------------
Sometimes the best catch-up move is a highly owned player the leader does not
own.

Cohort ownership is not the relevant rival overlap.
