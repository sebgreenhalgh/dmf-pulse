      What’s new in 2026/27 Fantasy: Changes to Bonus Points System

      20 Jul 2026

      See the tweaks that have been made to the system that decides who gets extra points from matches

      The Scout explains the key changes to the Bonus Points System for 2026/27 Fantasy Premier League.

      How does the Bonus Points System work?

      The Bonus Points System (BPS) is an important element of Fantasy, as it helps to determine the allocation of bonus points awarded in each match.

      The BPS utilises a range of statistics supplied by Opta that capture actions on the pitch, to create a performance score for every player.

      The players with the top three BPS scores in a given match receive bonus points - three points to the highest-scoring player, two to the second-best and one to the third.

      Bonus-point ties are resolved as follows:

      - If there is a tie for first place, Players 1 & 2 will receive 3 points each and Player 3 will receive 1 point.
      - If there is a tie for second place, Player 1 will receive 3 points and Players 2 and 3 will receive 2 points each.
      - If there is a tie for third place, Player 1 will receive 3 points, Player 2 will receive 2 points and Players 3 & 4 will receive 1 point each.

      What's changing this season?

      Following the introduction of defensive contribution points in Fantasy last season, a number of changes have been made to the BPS for 2026/27, which should boost the potential of goalkeepers, full-backs and attacking players to collect bonus points.

      The intention is to limit any crossover in players earning both defensive contribution (DC) points and bonus points in the same match, and to boost the potential of attacking players instead.

      Players no longer punished for being tackled

      In previous Fantasy seasons, players were handed a BPS reduction of -1 every time they were tackled. 

      This metric has now been removed from the BPS so that flair players who dribble more often - such as wingers and full-backs - are no longer punished for taking on an opponent.

      An assessment of the most-tackled players from 2025/26 shows that nine of the top 10 were attacking midfielders, with Manchester City’s new central midfielder Elliot Anderson the only exception in his final season at Nottingham Forest.

      Aston Villa’s Morgan Rogers leads the way and could be the most likely player to benefit from this change, having been tackled 156 times, while Man City wide men Antoine Semenyo and Jeremy Doku also rank highly.

      Brentford’s Michael Kayode will likely be among the main beneficiaries this time around, having been tackled 83 times last season.

      Less reward for clearances, blocks and interceptions

      Defensive actions such as clearances, blocks and interceptions (CBI) are partly involved in determining a player’s DC points AND bonus points in a match.

      Indeed, as many as 112 defenders earned both DC points and bonus points in a match last season, mainly because of their CBI. 

      So in order to address this imbalance, a player will earn now earn 1 BPS for every THREE CBI, instead of getting 1 BPS for every TWO CBI last season.

      This should slightly lessen the likelihood of centre-backs picking up bonus points, because they are the types of players who make the most CBI. 

      Goalkeeper saves

      Previously, a goalkeeper earned 3 BPS for saving a shot taken from inside the box and 2 BPS for saving a shot taken from outside the box.

      But that meant they might not be rewarded for saves made without their opponent taking a shot, such as a cross which deflects off a team-mate. So now, goalkeepers will earn 2 BPS for ANY save.

      Goalkeepers will continue to collect an extra 1 BPS for saving a shot taken from inside the box, but the “save from outside the box” metric has been removed for 2026/27.

      A goalkeeper will now earn an additional 1 BPS for saving a “big chance”, which is a situation where the player is expected to score.

      That means a further tweak has been needed, because a penalty is regarded as a big chance.

      Last season, a goalkeeper earned 8 BPS for saving a penalty, so this now drops to 7 for 2026/27, because they earn that extra 1 BPS back from the new “save from a big chance” metric. 

      Everton’s Jordan Pickford, Brentford’s Caoimhin Kelleher and Crystal Palace’s Dean Henderson were the standouts for saving big chances last season.

      Pickford ranked top with 28, with one of those being a penalty save. Kelleher saved three penalties on his way to second place in the goalkeeper standings on 143 points.
